| **Granovetter's Dashed Line** | Granovetter (1973), "The Strength of Weak Ties", *American Journal of Sociology* 78(6):1360–1380. The people you see most know what you know; the person you hardly see is your only line into another circle. A Swiss grid in winter, white, ink and one cold blue: a town of seven circles drawn in the paper's own code, solid lines for strong ties and dashed for weak. You are looking for a job and have three evenings a week for the people you know, one person an evening: your three friends at Home and eight people you once knew, one or two in every other circle. An evening raises a line one step, dashed to thin to thick; a line you give nothing thins by one step about one week in two, and never vanishes. The town reacts: two people you see often come to know each other, a person you see often introduces you to their circle and sees it less, and once a week you run into someone by chance, sometimes an old friend whose line comes back thick. Then seven openings travel as blue marks along the lines, always on a thick one, usually on a thin one, sometimes on a dashed one, less at every pair of hands, and stop short where someone did not mention it; the week's news stays on the map. Your friends tell you the same thing three times; the dashed line brings the news from elsewhere. Three weeks. A score in blue, what you heard as a share of what could have been heard on this town's news; the same news five ways (you, the best plan, your circle, nobody, the news known in advance); who told you first against the paper's Boston table; and the West End beside Charlestown, one town without dashed lines and one with. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/weakties/granovetters-dashed-line.html) · [memo](weakties/memo.md) |
