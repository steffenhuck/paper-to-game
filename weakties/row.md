| **Granovetter's Dashed Line** | Granovetter (1973), "The Strength of Weak Ties", *American Journal of Sociology* 78(6):1360–1380. The people you see most know what you know; the person you hardly see is your only line into another circle. A Swiss grid in winter, white, ink and one cold blue: a town of seven circles drawn in the paper's own code, solid lines for strong ties and dashed for weak. You are looking for a job and have seven evenings a week for the people you know: your three friends at Home and eight people you once knew, one or two in every other circle. Tap a name for one evening (a thin line, now and then), again for two (thick, often), none leaves it dashed. The town reacts: two people you see often come to know each other, a person you see often introduces you to their circle and sees it less, and once a week you run into someone by chance. Then seven openings travel as blue marks along the lines, always on a thick one, usually on a thin one, sometimes on a dashed one, less at every pair of hands, and stop short where someone did not mention it. Your friends tell you the same thing three times; the dashed line brings the news from elsewhere. Five weeks; a verdict in words against the paper's Boston tables, two other players on the same news, and the West End drawn without a dashed line. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/weakties/granovetters-dashed-line.html) · [memo](weakties/memo.md) |
