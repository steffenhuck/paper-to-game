## 1. Paper

Mark S. Granovetter (1973), "The Strength of Weak Ties", *American Journal of Sociology* 78(6):1360–1380. DOI 10.1086/225469; JSTOR stable URL https://www.jstor.org/stable/2776392. Available: the PDF only (the JSTOR scan), read whole, footnotes included. No code, no data beyond the numbers printed in the text; the figures are diagrams (the forbidden triad, two local bridges), not data.

## 2. What the paper hands over

**An argument, primary.** A verbal mechanism in four steps. (i) The strength of a tie is a combination of time, emotional intensity, intimacy and reciprocal services (p. 1361). (ii) The stronger the tie between A and B, the larger the share of their friends they share: strong ties take time, bring one's friends together, and are between similar people; cognitive balance then closes the triangle (p. 1362). Exaggerated for exposition into a rule: the triad with two strong ties and no third tie never occurs (Fig. 1, p. 1363). (iii) Hence no strong tie is a bridge; every bridge is a weak tie; and whatever diffuses crosses between groups along weak ties, reaching more people over greater social distance, provided the motivation to retell is dampened a little at each retelling (pp. 1364–1366, n. 11). (iv) So weak ties, not strong ones, bring the individual what is new (jobs, p. 1371), and a community of close circles with no weak ties between them cannot organise (the West End, pp. 1373–1375).

**Estimates, secondary**, all read from the text. Of 54 Boston-suburb job changers who found the job through a contact, 16.7% saw the contact often (at least twice a week), 55.6% occasionally (more than once a year, less than twice a week), 27.8% rarely (once a year or less) (p. 1371). Of 64, the information came directly from the employer in 39.1% of cases, through one intermediary in 45.3%, two in 12.5%, more than two in 3.1% (p. 1372). Davis's 651 sociograms: in 90% the two-choices-and-a-nonchoice triad is rarer than chance (p. 1364). Milgram chains from whites to a Black target completed in 50% of cases when the first Black recipient was an acquaintance, 26% when a friend (pp. 1368–1369). Rapoport and Horvath: more people are reached through seventh-and-eighth choices than first-and-second (p. 1369).

The game formalises the argument into an explicit toy town and calibrates its two output distributions (where a job lead came from, how many hands it passed through) to the two Boston tables. The places where the formalisation chose what the paper did not are in section 15.

## 3. The discovery, and its kind

**A counterintuitive result.** Everyone believes, and the paper says it is the "natural a priori idea", that the people most motivated to help you, your close friends, are the ones who find you a job. The paper shows that the news that matters comes from people you hardly see, and says why: your close friends are each other's close friends, so they know what you know; the person you see once a year lives in a circle you do not reach, and is the only line between that circle and yours. "It is remarkable that people receive crucial information from individuals whose very existence they have forgotten" (p. 1372). Underneath it, a mechanism made intelligible (the forbidden triad) and a piece of emergence (strong ties, breeding local cohesion, fragment the town).

## 4. The core narrative

You want to hear about jobs. You have a week of evenings and a list of people: three friends you see all the time, and a few people you once knew, from college, from your old job, from your street. Your friends want to help you. But they are each other's friends too, so whatever one of them hears, the others hear, and you hear it three times. The person you barely know hears things nobody in your circle ever hears, because she spends her evenings somewhere else. She may not bother to tell you. When she does, it is news. And a town where everyone has only close friends is a town of islands: a leaflet dropped in one never reaches the next, so when the bulldozers come, nobody can get the town together.

## 5. Who lives in the paper

- **Ego, the job changer** (pp. 1370–1372): controls how much time goes to whom, which by the paper's own definition is the strength of each tie. Anticipates where news will come from. The paper's own survey measured exactly this: how often did you see the contact?
- **The contact** who passes on the lead: controls whether to bother (motivation, the "natural a priori idea"). A dice roll in the game.
- **The news itself**, the rumour, the job opening: moves along ties, crosses bridges, dies after a few retellings (pp. 1365–1366). The non-obvious agent.
- **The town**, the network: reacts to ego by closing triangles (p. 1362) and by introducing ego's friends to one another (Gans's experience, p. 1374). The paper's "population".
- **The community organiser / the West Ender** (pp. 1373–1375): wants the town to act together; the structure decides whether it can.
- **Milgram's booklet sender** (pp. 1368–1369): chooses a friend or an acquaintance to forward to; completion depends on the choice.
- **The sociometrist** (p. 1366): the instrument that cannot see weak ties because it asks for three best friends.

## 7. The aim and the verdict

**The aim**, on its own line on the start screen, at the top of every week and first in How to play: *Spend your evenings so that you hear of as many job openings as you can.*

**The verdict** leads with, in words:

- *What Granovetter found.* Of 54 people in a Boston suburb who found their job through a contact, one in six saw that contact often, more than half saw them occasionally, and more than a quarter saw them rarely, once a year or less. The news came from the people they hardly saw, because those people spent their evenings in other circles.
- *What you found.* Over five weeks the town heard of N openings; you heard of M. Of yours, so many came from people you saw often, so many occasionally, so many rarely; so many straight from the person who had the opening, so many through one pair of hands, so many through more. In the same five weeks, with the same news, someone who gave every evening to their own circle would have heard of H; someone who gave one evening to one person in each circle they could reach, S.
- *Where you part* (one of several sentences chosen by the shares): you leaned on your circle and heard its news three times over; or you spread your evenings as the paper's job changers did; or you kept every tie weak and watched half of it fail to reach you, which is the motivation the paper weighs against structure.
- *What would have followed.* If the news that mattered came from close friends, a town of tight circles would be a town of well-informed people. It is a town of islands: the West End, read at the foot of the verdict with the town drawn without a single dashed line and a leaflet that never leaves its circle.

## 8. Three concepts

**A. The week (agent, strategic).** *Position:* ego, the job changer. *Format:* strategic (a town that reacts). *Controls:* one: tap a person on your list to give them an evening (none, one, two; two is "often" in the paper's own categories); seven evenings a week; Spend the week. *Deliberation before pressing:* who gets the evenings; whether to keep seeing the three friends; which acquaintance, in which circle, and whether two in the same circle are two or one. *What they see after:* lines thicken or turn dashed; new lines close between the people they see often; new names arrive (introductions, a chance meeting); then the news spreads, blue, hand to hand, and the week's count. *Discovery moment:* week one, the same blue mark arrives from Ann, from Ben and from Cal, and a different one comes down the dashed line from the college friend they gave nothing to. *Fun:* anticipation (the week plays out), agency (the lines are theirs), discovery (the triple arrival), mastery (week four's count against week one's). *Second playthrough:* the other strategies: everything weak and watch the dice; one strong tie per circle and watch the town close around you; the homebody to see the floor.

**B. The weave (controller, challenge).** *Position:* the planner of a working-class neighbourhood in 1960, the West End or Charlestown. *Controls:* place a fixed number of workplaces and clubs on the map of a town of family circles; each creates weak ties between the circles whose members meet there. *Aim:* when the renewal plan is announced, get the leaflet to every circle before the vote. *Deliberation:* which circles to join, with how few meeting places. *Discovery:* a town of warm circles with no workplaces is a town of islands; one club joining four circles does more than two clubs inside one. *Fun:* agency, discovery; mastery on replay. *Second playthrough:* a town that is already dense; a bigger town. *Weakness:* the paper's macro section is explicitly speculative ("in the absence of actual network data, all this is speculation", p. 1375), the player's variable is not one the paper measures, and the micro mechanism, the forbidden triad, is set by the designer rather than operated.

**C. The leaflet (subject, discovery). The reckless one.** *Position:* the player is the thing that spreads, a rumour, a leaflet, a job opening; the mechanism run from the news's point of view. *Controls:* a play screen with no words: the town drawn, the player's mark at one person; tap a line to be passed along it; solid lines always take you, dashed lines sometimes; every hand you pass through dims you a little, and after a few you stop. *Aim:* reach as much of the town as you can before you fade. *Deliberation:* which way out of a circle; whether to spend a retelling on a dashed line that may refuse. *Discovery:* every way out of a circle is a dashed line; inside a circle you go round and round. *Fun:* perception, anticipation, discovery; mastery on a second town. *Second playthrough:* another town, another start. *Weakness:* the player's choices are not a variable in the paper (news does not choose its path; the paper's flow is proportional to paths), and tie strength, the paper's variable, is scenery.

The hybrid: A's decision (where the evenings go) carried out with C's hands (the news drawn travelling, with the player watching it hesitate at dashed lines). That is A as built: the week's spread is C's screen without the taps.

**First, middle, last action for A.** First: where do my seven evenings go (my friends, naturally). Middle: which acquaintance in which circle, and is it worth two evenings to make one reliable, given that two evenings on one person thins her lines in her own circle and brings introductions I do not need. Last: one per circle, and where do the spare evenings go: to a second person in a big circle, or to a close friend. Three different thoughts.

## 10. The pick, and why

A. The player's control is the paper's measured variable (how often you see the contact), the town's reaction is the paper's own mechanism (the forbidden triad closed, friends introduced to friends), the thing watched is the paper's own diffusion argument (news crosses between circles on weak ties, dampened at each retelling), and the verdict is read against the paper's own table. The decisive deliberations have live wrong branches the paper names: every evening to the circle (the natural a priori idea); two evenings on a bridge to make it reliable (motivation, with closure as its price); two acquaintances in one circle (the second is not a bridge); everything rarely and let the dice decide (structure without motivation). The author test: the author knows the answer and still has to decide, with seven evenings and a town that keeps handing him his friends' friends, which ties to keep weak; the dice make the week worth watching. Originality: no game in the table draws a network and lets the player set its line weights, and none has let a population close in around the player. Runner-up: C, and what would make me switch: a stranger who finds the list of names and evenings a form and never looks up at the map, in which case the fix is C's hands: tap the people on the map itself, which is how it is built (the list and the map are one picture; the names are on the map). What C would have given if it had been the game: the wordless screen and the news's own point of view; the news's journey is kept as the week's animation.

## 11. The shape

Five weeks, one allocation each; no retry past the fifth. Screens: start; the week (map, row of names, Spend); the week playing (the same map, the news travelling, lines in the game's voice); the week's result; the verdict with the West End at its foot; How to play; About. Presets are the paper's own regimes, met in order of discovery rather than the paper's order: week one, the circle closed and the triple arrival (the forbidden triad, p. 1363), with week one's draw floored so that every circle has one opening; week two, the chance meeting (ties reactivated by chance, p. 1372) and the dashed lines from everywhere; week three, closure and introductions watched (two evenings on an acquaintance closes her to your other often-ties and brings her circle to you, pp. 1362, 1374) and her lines in her own circle gone thin (strength is time); weeks four and five, mastery, with the dice. Score: distinct openings heard, over five weeks, against the town's total and against two ghost players on the same news. Five to seven minutes.

## 12. Controls, mechanism, feedback, moment

**Control:** tap a person (on the map or in the list, the same thing) to cycle their evenings none → one → two → none; a counter of evenings left; Spend the week. Every week starts from none. **Mechanism:** tie strength this week is the evenings given (two: often; one: now and then; none but known: rarely). Closure: any two people ego sees often who are not tied become tied (rarely), and stay tied. Introductions: a person ego sees often introduces ego to one member of their circle not yet known (a new rarely tie), every week. Finite time: a person outside Home whom ego sees often has given ego two evenings, so their ties in their own circle are now-and-then for that week. Chance meeting: once a week from week two, ego meets one person not yet known, from a circle ego has no tie to if any remain, else anyone. News: each week seven openings arise at seven people drawn at random (not ego; week one floored to one per circle); each person who hears tells each neighbour with a probability set by the tie (often 1, now and then 3/4, rarely 3/10), multiplied by 0.8 per pair of hands the news has passed through; the town's own ties are often inside circles and rarely between them. Ego hears a piece once, however many tell it; the week counts distinct pieces, and the verdict counts them by the strength of the first tie that delivered and by the hands passed. **Feedback:** the lines (weight and dash), the new lines closing, the names arriving, then the blue marks travelling with a line of the game's voice at each event, and the count. **Moment:** week one, the same mark arriving down three solid lines, and a different one down the dashed line.

## 14. What is cut

Emotional intensity, intimacy and reciprocal services as components of strength (time alone, as the paper's own survey did, n. 15). Similarity as a reason for closure (time and balance carry it). The Milgram studies and the interracial chains (read on About). Rapoport and Horvath (About). The DHL model and transitivity (not in the game). The content of ties, negative ties, asymmetric ties (the paper leaves them out too). The direction of influence and the quality of a lead by path length ("earmarked for one person", p. 1373): the verdict reports hands passed, but every lead counts one. The community section is read, not played, with the town drawn once without dashed lines.

## 15. Divergences

- **Clock.** A week with seven evenings, five weeks. The paper's categories are per week and per year ("at least twice a week", "once a year or less"); the game's "rarely" is a tie given no evening this week, which the paper would call occasionally-to-rarely. Reason: one week is the unit the player can hold.
- **Strength.** Three levels, the paper's survey categories, and nothing in between; two evenings is "often". The paper's strength is continuous and four-dimensional.
- **Closure, exaggerated.** The forbidden triad never occurs: two often-ties close at once. The paper exaggerates it the same way and says so (p. 1363); Davis's 90% is the evidence that it tends to be absent.
- **Transmission probabilities** (often 1, now and then 3/4, rarely 3/10) and the **damping** (0.8 per pair of hands): ours; the paper says only that strong ties are more motivated and that retelling dampens. Tuned so that a mixed Boston week reproduces the two tables (section 17).
- **Finite time for the other party.** A person outside Home given two evenings sees their own circle now-and-then that week. Ours in form; the paper's premise in substance (strength is time, and a strong tie is a large time commitment). Without it a strong bridge costs nothing but evenings (section 18).
- **Introductions and chance meetings.** Ours, from Gans's account (p. 1374) and from "chance meetings or mutual friends operated to reactivate such ties" (p. 1372); rates ours: one introduction per often-tie per week, one chance meeting a week from week two.
- **The town.** Thirty people in seven circles (Home of three and six outer circles of four or five), ties inside circles all strong, nine dashed lines among the outer circles (a ring and three chords) and none from Home except ego's own; sizes ours. Ego starts knowing eleven: the three at Home and eight people once known and hardly seen, one or two in every outer circle. The paper gives no town; it gives the kinds of contact (an old college friend, a former workmate, p. 1372).
- **Payoff.** Distinct openings heard, seven a week for five weeks; the paper's respondents each found one job. The ghosts (every evening in the circle; one evening per circle, no one twice) are ours, run on the same news and the same coins.
- **Presentation.** Seven rings on a plane; names; the news as a blue mark that stops short where someone did not mention it; the dice shown as "usually" and "sometimes" with the odds in the rule line.

## 16. Mechanism kind and claim boundaries

**Toy.** A model built to illustrate the paper's argument, calibrated so that a mixed week reproduces the paper's two Boston tables. It demonstrates that, under this game's assumptions, evenings spread across circles bring more distinct news than evenings kept in the circle; that the circle's news reaches you more than once; and that the shares of who told you first, for a week like the respondents', look like the paper's table. It assumes the town, the pass probabilities, the damping, the news rate, the finite-time rule, the rates of introductions and chance meetings, and that an opening is heard or not with nothing about its quality. The paper establishes the argument from tie strength to bridges to diffusion as a hypothesis with the evidence it had, and the two tables from a small exploratory sample. A run of the game is no evidence for the paper's claim about the world; the About screen says so, with the three lines.

**Which discoveries follow from the paper and which from our choices.** That the circle tells you the same thing three times follows from the paper's exaggerated rule (every circle a clique) and holds under every parameter tried. That spreading across circles beats the circle follows from the paper's structural argument (your circle hears one circle's news; your weak ties reach the others) and holds under every parameter tried, with or without the finite-time rule, the floor, or the damping cap. That one strong tie per circle does *not* beat one weak tie per circle is a design outcome: it rests on the budget (seven evenings, a cap of two), on there being more circles than a week can hold close, and on the finite-time rule; without the finite-time rule the two are still level (section 18). The game does not present the second as the paper's finding: the verdict reads the player's shares against the paper's table and says, where the player made bridges strong, only that the circle's news reached them repeatedly and that in Boston the people seen often brought one job in six. The paper does not say what a player with seven evenings should do; it says what the respondents' contacts were like.

## 17. Calibration plan and result

Plan: (1) the paper's stated rules hold in the town (strong ties pass on more readily; retelling dampens; circles are cliques; every tie between circles is weak, so no strong tie is a bridge; Home has no line out but yours; no forbidden triad in the town as drawn); (2) after any week, no forbidden triad among ego's often-ties, over 300 random games; closures, introductions and the finite-time rule as declared; (3) under the reference week (two close friends often, three acquaintances now and then, the rest rarely), the shares of news first told by someone seen often / now and then / rarely within 12 points of the paper's 16.7 / 55.6 / 27.8, in the paper's order; (4) the paper's hands table read as the paper reads it: the share that came straight from the source or through one pair of hands within 8 points of the paper's 84.4, more than two pairs of hands at most 10 per cent, straight from the source among the two largest shares; (5) the heuristics table and the discovery; (6) the discovery under eleven variants of our choices; (7) the draw over 300 seeds; (8) determinism and the same coins for every strategy on a seed; (9) a clairvoyant best week by search, read for loopholes.

Result: 46 checks, all pass (`calibration-result.txt`). Reference week: 14.5 / 48 / 37.5 against 16.7 / 55.6 / 27.8; straight-or-one-hand 85.5 against 84.4; more than two hands 8.9. The hands table is loose in its middle: the toy gives too few two-hand paths and too many three-hand ones (6 and 9 against 12.5 and 3.1), because in a town where you know someone in every circle, news from a circle reaches you through that one person or not at all; a bigger town with circles you cannot reach would move it. The verdict shows the player's hands against the paper's as description, not as a target hit.
