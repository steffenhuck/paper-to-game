# Granovetter's Dashed Line — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-week1-planned.png` (the town, the circle thick, eight dashed lines out), `screenshot-week1-news.png` (a blue mark reaching Kim at the cousins), `screenshot-week1-result.png`, `screenshot-week4-new-names.png` (the introduced and chance names on the map and in the row) and `screenshot-end.png` (the verdict against the two Boston tables, the two ghosts, the West End). Then play it on a phone as someone who likes their friends: the things to judge are whether week one's "Ann and Cal tell you the same" lands as the paper's point, whether the week's animation is worth watching after week two or is skipped every time, and whether the row of names under the map is a form or a control.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`: the pass probabilities (often 1, now and then 0.75, rarely 0.3) and the damping (0.8 per pair of hands), the town (seven circles with names, the nine dashed lines among the outer circles, who you know at the start), the news rate (seven a week), the week-one floor, the finite-time rule, the reference week the Boston tables are checked against, and the two tables themselves (p. 1371, p. 1372, typeset text). Change a number and run `node calibration.js`; then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure. The seed is in the URL (`?seed=4821`) and on the end screen.
3. What we were least sure about: the finite-time rule (a person given two evenings sees their own circle less that week), which is the paper's premise applied to the other party but a rule of ours; the hands table, whose middle the toy misses (too few two-hand paths); whether a strong tie per circle being level with a weak tie per circle is the right outcome for the paper; the verdict's "where you part" sentences at the thresholds; and the width of the chips row for players with more than fourteen names by week five.
4. The game has had the builder's review only: screenshots at phone width and scripted sessions in a headless browser (`session-test.py`). No stranger has played this build.
