# Find a Job with Granovetter — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-week1-planned.png` (the town, the circle thick, eight dashed lines out), `screenshot-week1-news.png` (a blue mark reaching Kim at the cousins), `screenshot-week1-result.png`, `screenshot-week3-new-names.png` (the introduced and chance names on the map and in the row), `screenshot-end.png` (the score in blue, the same news five ways, the best plan, the Boston table, the two towns). Then play it on a phone as someone who likes their friends: the things to judge are whether week one's "Ann and Cal tell you the same" lands as the paper's point, whether the week's animation is worth watching after week two or is skipped every time, whether three evenings make each week a real choice or merely a short one, whether a line going thick on the second visit reads as the paper's "often", whether the coin on untended lines reads as life or as noise, and whether the five bars make a second town worth playing.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`: the evenings a week (three) and the cap per person (one), the pass probabilities (often 1, now and then 0.75, rarely 0.3) and the damping (0.8 per pair of hands), the town (seven circles with names, the nine dashed lines among the outer circles, who you know at the start), the news rate (seven a week), the number of weeks (three), the coin on which an untended line thins (one in two) and the share of chance meetings that revive an old line (one in three), the name of the best plan, the score's two band edges (85 and 60) and the name of the best plan, the week-one floor, the finite-time rule, the reference player the Boston tables are checked against (one who rotates), and the two tables themselves (p. 1371, p. 1372, typeset text). Change a number and run `node calibration.js`; then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure. The seed is in the URL (`?seed=4821`) and on the end screen.
3. What we were least sure about: the finite-time rule (a person given two evenings sees their own circle less that week), which is the paper's premise applied to the other party but a rule of ours; the hands table, whose middle the toy misses (too few two-hand paths); whether a strong tie per circle being level with a weak tie per circle is the right outcome for the paper; the verdict's "where you part" sentences at the thresholds; and the ceiling: it is found by hill-climbing with the news known, not by exhaustive search, so a lucky player can be the ceiling themselves (100 per cent), and the three fate sentences at the band edges.
4. The game has had the builder's review and six plays by the author (revisions 1 to 6: chosen people ringed; three weeks; the score and the crisper verdict; lines as states, the bet removed; the coin and the reactivated tie; three evenings, one person each). No stranger has played this build.
