# Granovetter's Dashed Line — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-week1-planned.png` (the town, the circle thick, eight dashed lines out), `screenshot-week1-news.png` (a blue mark reaching Kim at the cousins), `screenshot-week1-result.png`, `screenshot-week3-new-names.png` (the introduced and chance names on the map and in the row), `screenshot-bet-offer.png` and `screenshot-bet-result.png` (the close), and `screenshot-end.png` (the verdict against the two Boston tables, the four yardsticks, the West End). Then play it on a phone as someone who likes their friends: the things to judge are whether week one's "Ann and Cal tell you the same" lands as the paper's point, whether the week's animation is worth watching after week two or is skipped every time, whether the bet lands as the paper's line or as a trick, and whether the four yardsticks make a second town worth playing.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`: the pass probabilities (often 1, now and then 0.75, rarely 0.3) and the damping (0.8 per pair of hands), the town (seven circles with names, the nine dashed lines among the outer circles, who you know at the start), the news rate (seven a week), the number of weeks (three), the four answers to the bet, the week-one floor, the finite-time rule, the reference week the Boston tables are checked against, and the two tables themselves (p. 1371, p. 1372, typeset text). Change a number and run `node calibration.js`; then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure. The seed is in the URL (`?seed=4821`) and on the end screen.
3. What we were least sure about: the finite-time rule (a person given two evenings sees their own circle less that week), which is the paper's premise applied to the other party but a rule of ours; the hands table, whose middle the toy misses (too few two-hand paths); whether a strong tie per circle being level with a weak tie per circle is the right outcome for the paper; the verdict's "where you part" sentences at the thresholds; and the hindsight yardstick, which hears nearly everything (it knows the coins) and may read as a reproach rather than a target.
4. The game has had the builder's review and two plays by the author (revision 1: chosen people ringed on the map; revision 2: three weeks and the bet). No stranger has played this build.
