One. The two kinds don't exist. Ninety reconstructed cells, 25 measures, no split anywhere: curly and straight are the ends of a line, and a single number — how far a tree reaches divided by how long its longest path is — carries what the anatomists' eyes were doing.

Two. The cell bodies don't tell you where the groups are. At 40 µm the whole nucleus is one cluster. The structure is in which way the dendrites grow: most trees are lopsided, with the soma at the edge of its own field, and neighbours either share a field or avoid each other entirely. A few long straight cells reach across — the paper's "bridges".
