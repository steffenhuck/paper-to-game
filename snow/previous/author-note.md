# Snow's Cloudy Glass — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-1-milky.png` (the first knock: the glass has turned, the door is brown, one house is in the window), `screenshot-a-2-street.png` (Battersea: railings and seven crape doors before any knock), `screenshot-a-3-done.png` (Lambeth Church: the window after three streets), `screenshot-a-4-month.png` (Borough Road after the month), then `screenshot-a-end.png` (the verdict, your window beside Snow's). Then play it once on a phone.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the four street rows from Tables I and II, the compression (10), the knocks (7), the marks (5), the residents' rates, the floors and caps on crape, and the agreement band. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (67 checks against the paper), then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build if it fails or if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the denominator is got by knocking at living doors, where Snow had it from Parliament's return; the verdict says so, but a reader who knows the paper may find the second row a liberty; (b) three streets give small numbers, so about a fifth of sensible games find a narrower gap than Snow's and a quarter find no crape behind a blue door at all, and the verdict's words carry that rather than a number; (c) the verdict's "times as often" pools the three streets with the shares estimated from tested doors, which is an estimator of ours; (d) the compression of ten is announced once on the first street and on About and nowhere else; (e) the handbill's row labels in the window are small (9.5 px) at phone width; (f) "Battersea water" and "Thames Ditton water" are our names for the two companies' supply.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured.
