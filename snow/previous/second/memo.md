# Snow's Cloudy Glass — design memo (rebuild, 15 November 2026)

The first build and its memo are in `previous/`. Its paper reading (sections 1–5 there) stands and is not repeated beyond what the new design needs.

## 0. Given

Mine; author title, *Snow's Cloudy Glass* kept (no verb title came naturally). The brief that overrides the first build: that game was a demonstration with a budget; its own numbers table showed that no way of playing went wrong, its one decision (crape door or living door) was defused on the first screen, the houses were drawn independently so which door you tapped did not matter, and twenty-one identical knocks were far too long. Kept from it: crape on the doors before any action; the glass and the silver drops watched; the four-pane window; the handbill look; the verdict in four parts with no round table. New: the game is the decisions of the first memo's runner-up (Farr's Return: what to compare, how to know) made with the hands of its pick (the door, the glass). Two streets, no prediction street; six to eight decisions, each of a new kind; the player's two choices are the paper's two, and the paper names the wrong branch of each: the parish doctor's comparison of Battersea against Lambeth Church, read as the air; and the resident's word instead of the glass, which drifts the window toward the Board of Health's three and a half to one for the paper's reason. Text for a smart eleven-year-old. Push to main.

## 1. Paper

John Snow (1856), *Cholera and the Water Supply in the South Districts of London in 1854*, reprinted from the *Journal of Public Health*, October 1856; London: T. Richards. No DOI; read from the Project Gutenberg transcription (eBook 66507), typeset text and six tables, nothing read off a figure or a scan. The paper was in `inbox/` for the rebuild and is not committed.

## 2. What the paper hands over, read for this build

The same estimate (Tables I and II: houses and people on each company's water per subdistrict, deaths in houses on each, the rates), and, this time as the spine, the paper's two arguments about method. **What to compare:** districts differ in everything, so Farr's district comparison could be read any way; but the two companies' pipes "go down all the streets, and into nearly all the courts and alleys", and "a single house often had a different supply from that on either side", so the comparison can be made between a house and the house next door, where nothing differs but the water; and when it is, the parish stops mattering: crowded, dirty, very poor Lambeth Church and Waterloo by the river died little, thinly peopled, comparatively genteel Clapham and Battersea died much. **How to know:** Snow "did not rest content with the mere reply of the resident", took the water-rate receipt or the salt test; the Board of Health, matching company lists to death lists, found 125 against 37 per 10,000 instead of 160 against 27, three and a half to one instead of six, because "as the deaths were six times as numerous in the houses supplied by the Southwark and Vauxhall Company ... out of every six mistakes five would transfer a death from the former company to the latter, and only one would transfer a death from the latter company to the former."

## 3. The discovery, and its kind

**Identification**, as before, now played as its two halves: a comparison is only as good as the number of things that differ between the two doors compared (the parish doctor's pair differs in the street and the water; the next-door pair differs in the water alone), and a count is only as good as the way each door's water was known (the word errs one time in seven, and because nearly every crape door is on the town's water, nearly every error moves a death from the guilty water to the innocent one).

## 4. The core narrative

Cholera is in two streets. The grand terrace at Battersea has black cloth on five doors; the crowded court by the river has it on one. The parish doctor says: there you are, Battersea Fields are marsh, it is the marsh air. A younger doctor says: those two streets are different in a hundred ways. Let me find two houses that are different in one way only. He goes down the crowded court to the door with the black cloth and to the door next to it, and asks which water each drinks; and when the people are not sure he takes a glass from each tap and adds a few drops that turn salt water milky, because the town's water is salt with the tide and the upriver water is not. The door with the cloth drinks the town's water. The door next to it drinks the other. Same street, same air, same poverty; one thing different. He writes that down instead.

## 5. Who lives in the paper

As in the first memo; two of them now speak. **The parish doctor** (the Board of Health's way: compare parishes, read the air, take the lists' word) offers his comparisons for nothing. **Snow** (the player) picks a crape door, picks what to set against it, and picks how to know their water, with a morning's knocks to spend. **The residents** answer, wrong one time in seven, unsure one time in four. **The Board** receives one sentence at the end.

## 7. The aim and the verdict

**Aim:** *Find out whether it is the water: set a crape door beside a door without, and know what each drinks.*

**Yardstick during play:** the four-pane window, built from pairs made in the same street (top row the crape door of each pair, bottom row the door set against it, brown or blue, solid where the glass said it and outlined where only the word did); under it, in words, what the pairs say so far ("Of the crape doors, all brown; of the doors beside them, two blue and three brown. A brown door wears crape about [N] times as often as a blue one", or "no blue door wears crape in your book", or "you have set nothing side by side in the same street"). The doctor's pairs across the streets are counted on a line of their own, with what differs in them.

**Verdict:** 1. *What Snow found*: on the same streets, a house on the town's water lost someone about seven times as often in these weeks and fourteen times in the first four; crowded or genteel, marsh or hill, once the water was known the parish made no difference; and he knew it by the receipt and the glass, not the word. 2. *What you found*: the sentence you wrote, and your window read honestly: how many pairs in the same street, what they say, how many across the streets, what they cannot say; then the words that were wrong among your doors, and what the window says with them put right. 3. *Where you part*: your sentence against your window against Snow. 4. *Had yours stood*: the marsh air blamed a while longer; or the Board's three and a half to one, for the Board's reason.

## 8. Three concepts

**A. The Pair (experimenter, discovery; the pick).** Two streets on one screen, crape already on the doors: Battersea (thinly peopled, comparatively genteel; marsh) and Waterloo Road (crowded, dirty, very poor, by the river). The parish doctor opens with his pair for nothing: a Battersea crape door against a Waterloo living door, by the word. Then eight knocks. A pair is a crape door and a door set against it, anywhere on either street; the card under the pair says what differs between the two (the street; the water, once known; or nothing). Ask at both doors for one knock, or draw a glass at each for two; tap a door already answered to check its word with the glass for one. After four knocks the doctor offers three more pairs for nothing. Close the book and write one sentence for the Board: the marsh air, the town's water, or cannot say. *Thinking:* at the doctor's offer, what differs between his two doors; at each pair, which street, which neighbour, and whether the word is worth the saving; at a surprising word, whether to spend a knock on the glass; at the end, what the book can carry. *Moments:* the first next-door pair in Waterloo Road whose card says "different: the water" only; the first crape door whose word says blue and whose glass turns milky, and the line under the window jumping. *Fun:* anticipation (the drops), agency (the card and the line move with every choice), discovery (the card with one difference), mastery (the second street is paired with a plan).

**B. Farr's Return (controller; the first memo's runner-up).** The inquiry as a budget: unit (district, subdistrict, house) and method (lists, registrars, in person). Walkthrough as before: numbers without doors. Its decisions are now inside A.

**C. The Morbid Matter (agent; reckless).** As in the first memo; not picked for the same reason.

## 9. The first five minutes

## 10. The pick, and why

**A.** Every one of its decisions is one of the paper's: what to set against what (the doctor's pair or the next door), how to know (word or glass), whether a free comparison is worth having, whether to check a word that looks wrong, and what to tell the Board. Which door you tap matters because of what you tap it against: the same crape door makes a pair that says nothing beside one neighbour and a pair that says everything beside the other, and a pair across the streets says nothing however the glasses come up. The wrong branches are live because they are cheap: the doctor's pairs cost nothing and the word costs half; a player who takes them has a fuller book and a worse one, and sees the line under the window say so. The sentence afterwards has no "guess": *I set the crape door against the door next to it, tested both, and the only thing different was the water; the doctor's pair had two things different and could not say.* Originality: no game in the table has asked the player to choose what to compare, nor shown a pair with its differences written under it.

**Author test.** The identification argument as a card with one line under it ("different: the water"), and the Board's three and a half to one arriving in the player's own window from a resident's wrong word, with the glass to put it right for a knock.

**Runner-up: B.** Switch only if the author wants the inquiry's cost structure as the whole game; A contains its decisions.

## 11. The shape

One screen with both streets; eight knocks; the doctor's offer at the start and again after four knocks; *Close the book* once two pairs are made, or when the knocks are gone; the sentence; the verdict. Decisions in a typical game: the doctor's first offer; three to five pairs (each a choice of crape door, of the door against it, and of the method); a check of a word; the doctor's second offer; the sentence. Eight or so, no two about the same thing. No presets. The draw is seeded (URL and verdict); another draw puts crape on other doors.

## 12. Controls, mechanism, feedback, moment

- **Controls:** tap a crape door; tap a door to set against it (either street); *Ask at both doors* (1 knock) or *Draw a glass at each* (2 knocks) or *Change the doors*; tap a door already answered by word and *Draw a glass* (1 knock) to check it; *Take it, for nothing* / *No* at the doctor's offers; *Close the book*; one of three sentences.
- **Mechanism:** `makeStreet` (twelve houses from the row's share, crape at the row's per-house rate times ten, the words), `offer` (the doctor's pairs across the streets by word), `pair` (the two doors, the method, the knocks, what each door's water is now known to be and by what), `card` (what differs: street, water, nothing), `windowFrom` (same-street pairs), `line` (the words under the window, from the record as the player knows it), `check`, `closeBook`, `verdict` (the record against the truth, the sentence against both).
- **Feedback:** the two glasses side by side; the card under the pair; the window and its line; the doctor's line after the card.
- **Moment:** a card that reads "different: the water" with nothing else on it; the line under the window moving when a word is checked.

## 15. Divergences

- Two streets of twelve stand for two subdistricts (Battersea, Table II; Waterloo Road first part, Table I); crape drawn ten times as thick as the seven-week rates; each street's brown share and per-house odds are the row's. Battersea's one Lambeth house dies at the Lambeth rate for the twenty-one mixed subdistricts.
- The doctor is ours; his reading (the marsh) is the kind of reading the paper says a district comparison allows, not a quotation. His pairs are across the streets and by the word, which is the Board's method in miniature.
- Eight knocks; one for a word at both doors, two for a glass at each, one for a check: ours.
- The word wrong one time in seven and absent one in four, as before; and a floor that at least one Battersea crape door carries a wrong word, so that the drift can be met in every game (declared on About).
- Crape floors: Battersea three to seven, Waterloo Road one to two.
- The sentence for the Board and the pair card are ours; the window is Snow's four numbers.

## 16. Mechanism kind and claim boundaries

**Reconstructed.** Each street is a data-generating process built from one row of Table I or II: the row's share of houses on each company's water sets the street's twelve houses, placed at random so that a house is often on the other pipe from its neighbours; the row's deaths over houses supplied for each water, times ten, is each house's chance of crape, drawn independently. The ratio between the two waters on each street is the row's. Everything else is ours and declared: the doctor and his reading, his free pairs (across the streets, by the word), the eight knocks and the costs of a word, a glass and a check, the residents' error rates and the floor of one wrong word at a Battersea crape door, the floors on crape (Battersea three to seven; Waterloo Road one or two, one of them brown with a blue door beside it), the pair card, the sentence, and the line under the window (deaths over doors by colour from the same-street pairs, as Snow divided deaths by houses supplied).

- **Demonstrates:** that a pair can only answer for the one thing that differs between its two doors; that the parish comparison cannot choose between the street and the water, and the next-door pair can; that a count built from the word drifts toward the Board's number for the paper's reason (nearly every crape door is brown, so nearly every wrong word moves a death to the blue water), and the glass puts it back.
- **Assumes:** independence of deaths across houses; the compression; the word's error rates and the floor; that two streets of twelve stand for the twenty-one mixed subdistricts; that the doctor's marsh is the kind of thing a district comparison lets one say.
- **The paper establishes:** the counts and rates in its tables; the identification argument; the Board's attenuated ratio and why its mistakes ran one way. Not: anything about a particular house, nor a rate at which residents misreport.

**Where the discoveries come from.** The card's "different: the water, and nothing else" follows from the streets' construction (same street, waters placed at random); the drift follows from the error rate and the composition of the crape doors (the paper's reason), and the calibration check shows a single wrong word at a crape door turning "beyond count" into "about twice". The doctor's trap and the word's cheapness are design choices, and the verdict names them as the Board's method rather than as findings.

## 17. Calibration plan and result

`calibration.js`, 55 checks, all passing (`calibration-result.txt`): the two rows' rates per 10,000 recomputed from deaths and population (within 0.3; Snow's own rounding puts Waterloo Road at 25.6 where the division gives 25.4); supplied population as houses times one constant per street; the house split (11 of 12, 3 of 12) within six points of the rows; per-house odds ratios equal to the rows' deaths-over-houses ratios; the aggregates the verdict quotes (47.2 and 6.8; 107 and 8, "between thirteen and fourteen"; eight and five; 160 against 27; 125 against 37); the list error of about one in fourteen with nine mistakes in ten moving a death off the Southwark company, and the residents' rate as its double; forty grains of salt against one; over 3,000 unconstrained seeds the realised rate ratio per street within 20 per cent of the row's; crape within floors over 3,000 seeds; the wrong-word floor met in every game; residents one in four unsure and one in seven wrong; position real (a Waterloo Road crape door has a neighbour on the other pipe in 97 per cent of games, a Battersea one in 17); determinism; the doctor's offers (across the streets, worded, the second after four knocks); the rules of a pair, a check, the book and the sentence; the window built from same-street pairs only; `ratioOf` on all-brown, no-blue-crape and mixed windows, and one wrong crape word turning "beyond count" into "about twice"; the strategies' bands (below); robustness at compression 5 and 20 without floors and at six and ten knocks; no decimal, NaN, Infinity or undefined in 8,400 verdict and window lines.

Read: the two wrong branches the brief names are the two ways of going wrong, each for the paper's reason: the doctor's pairs never produce a pair that can choose, and the word drifts the window below Snow's gap in nineteen games in twenty while the glass agrees in nine in ten; checking the words recovers most of it for a knock each.
