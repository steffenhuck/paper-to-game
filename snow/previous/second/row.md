| **Snow's Cloudy Glass** | Snow (1856), *Cholera and the Water Supply in the South Districts of London in 1854*, reprinted from the *Journal of Public Health*, October 1856. The grand experiment as an 1854 London handbill, and as its two decisions: what to compare, and how to know. Two streets with crape already on the doors, genteel Battersea and the crowded court of Waterloo Road; the parish doctor offers a pair across them for nothing and reads the marsh air. You have a morning's knocks: pick a crape door, set a door against it (the one next to it, or any door on either street), and a card says what is different between the two, the street and the water or the water alone or nothing; then ask at both doors for a knock, or draw a glass at each for two and watch it turn milky or stay clear. A four-pane window fills from the same-street pairs, outlined where only the word said it, and the line under it drifts toward the Board of Health's three and a half to one when a crape door's word is wrong, until you check it with the glass. Close the book, send the Board one sentence, and the verdict reads it against your own book and against Snow.
