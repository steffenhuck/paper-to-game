## 0. Given

Mine; author title, *Snow's Cloudy Glass* kept. The brief after the second build: its mechanics were still odd. The glass-or-word choice was a decoy, since nobody would not test the water; and the rule that a pair must start at a crape door was ours, not the paper's. ## 1–2. Paper, and what it hands over for this build

Snow (1856), *Cholera and the Water Supply in the South Districts of London in 1854*, *Journal of Public Health*, October 1856; Gutenberg 66507. For this build the paper hands over one idea and the numbers to carry it. The idea: the thing suspected (the water) varies from house to house, because the two companies' pipes run down the same streets and a house is often on a different pipe from the house next door; everything else anyone could blame (the air, the ground, the crowding, the poverty) varies from district to district. So a comparison between districts cannot tell the water from the rest, and a comparison between neighbours can. The numbers: deaths over houses supplied for each company in the first seven weeks (Table I totals: 507 of 16,038 against 98 of 20,554, about seven to one; p. 4: thirteen to fourteen to one in the first four weeks), and the share of houses on each company's water in each subdistrict (Tables I and II), from 16 per cent at Lambeth Church to 98 per cent at Clapham, with the crowded riverside subdistricts mostly on Lambeth water and the thinly peopled inland ones mostly on Southwark and Vauxhall water (p. 7).

## 3. The discovery, and its kind

**Identification, spatial.** A cause that varies at the fine scale can be separated from confounders that vary at the coarse scale by comparing within the coarse unit. On the map, the crape is inland; so is the town's water; the doctor says the inland air. Test houses in the same row, where the side of town is the same for both, and the water stands alone.

## 4. The core narrative

Cholera is in six streets running from the river up to the inland side of town, and most of the black cloth is on the inland doors. The parish doctor says: the inland air. A younger doctor has a glass and a bottle of silver drops that turn the town's salty water milky, and eight houses' worth of time. If he tests houses inland, they come up milky, and the doctor says: so they would, inland. If he tests by the river, they come up clear. Only in the streets in the middle, where the pipes are mixed, can he find a milky house and a clear house side by side with the same air at both doors; and there the crape is at the milky one.

## 5. Who lives in the paper

Snow with the glass (the player; chooses where to test); the parish doctor (the Board's way: reads any map as air); the six streets (real subdistricts, with their real shares of each water, in the paper's order from the river inland); the houses (die at the paper's rates by their water).

## 7. The aim and the verdict

**Aim:** *Find out what does it, the water or the side of town: test houses so that the doctor cannot say "that is just the inland air".*

**Yardstick during play:** the row tallies on the map (brown x of n with crape, blue y of m, per row, from tested houses), the window for *same row, both waters*, and a line in the game's voice: all tests, then same-row tests, then what they say ("a brown house wears crape about five times as often as a blue one on the same street"; "no blue house in the same row wears crape"; "you have not yet tested a brown and a blue house in the same row"). The doctor's line follows, and changes with the state of the evidence.

**Verdict:** what Snow found; what you found (your sentence; all your tests; whether the doctor's objection stands; the same-row comparison; among the brown houses, riverside against inland); where you part; had yours stood. Then the map with every pipe revealed, your window beside Snow's, the later weeks, the citation.

## 8. Three concepts

**A. The Map (experimenter, discovery; the pick).** Six real streets from the river inland, six houses each, pipes mixed house by house in each street's real proportion; crape already on the doors, mostly inland. One tool: the glass. Eight tests, anywhere. Each test paints a door and updates its row's tally. The doctor objects after each, as far as the evidence lets him. Then one sentence for the Board. *Thinking before pressing:* where. Inland houses come up brown and prove nothing; riverside ones blue; the middle streets have both, and a brown house with crape beside a blue house without, in the same street, is the one thing the doctor cannot answer. *Moments:* the first same-row pair; the doctor's concession; the revealed map at the end. *Fun:* anticipation (the drops), agency (every test is a choice of place), discovery, mastery (the second map is sampled with a plan). *Second playthrough:* a new map; the player goes straight to the mixed streets.

**B. The Pair (the second build).** Two streets, pairs with cards, the word against the glass. Rejected by the brief: the method choice was a decoy and the pair rule was ours.

**C. The Doctor's Map (reckless).** The player is the parish doctor and must explain every map the game draws with an air story, and loses when a same-row pair is put in front of him. Walkthrough: an inversion with one joke; the player never holds the glass.

## 10. The pick, and why

**A.** One tool and one decision, repeated eight times with a different map state each time, is the whole idea of spatial identification done by hand: go where the treatment varies within the unit, and compare there. The doctor's objection is the confound stated in a teenager's words, and it is answered by the player's own placement of tests, not by a rule of ours. Nothing is a decoy: the glass is the only instrument, any house may be tested, and a test anywhere is informative about something; what differs is whether it can answer the doctor. The sentence afterwards: *I tested houses in the same street, where the air was the same, and the ones on the town's water had the crape.*

**Author test.** Stratification as a thing you do with your thumb on a map; the doctor's objection as the between-district comparison, and his concession as the within-district one.

**Runner-up:** none kept; B is in `previous/`.

## 15. Divergences

- Six rows of six stand for six subdistricts, ordered from the river inland (Waterloo Road, Lambeth Church, Trinity, Borough Road, Kennington, Clapham), with each row's share of houses on the town's water from Tables I and II and the pipes placed at random within the row. The paper's deaths-over-houses rates for the twenty-one mixed subdistricts (Table I totals) are used for every row, since the paper says the relative mortality is nearly the same throughout; drawn ten times as thick.
- Floors: five to ten crape doors; more crape inland than by the river (so the doctor's story has a pattern to point at); at least one row with a brown crape house and a blue house without (so the clean comparison exists). Declared on About.
- The doctor and his "inland air" are ours: the kind of reading a district comparison allows. Eight tests is ours. The sentence is ours.

## 16. Mechanism kind and claim boundaries

**Reconstructed.** The map is six of the paper's subdistricts in a schematic order from the river inland, six houses each, with the row's real share of houses on the Southwark and Vauxhall Company's water (Tables I and II) placed at random within the row, and crape drawn per house at the paper's deaths-over-houses rate for its water (Table I totals, first seven weeks), times ten. The tests, the doctor, the floors (five to ten crape doors; more inland than by the river; at least one street with a brown crape house and a blue house without) and the sentence are ours.

- **Demonstrates:** that tests on one side of town cannot separate the water from the side, and tests on one street can; what the map looks like when the water is the cause and the pipes are sorted by district.
- **Assumes:** independence of deaths; one rate for every street; the compression; the river-to-inland order; the floors; the doctor and his air.
- **The paper establishes:** the shares and the rates, and the identification argument. Nothing about a particular house or street beyond those.

**Where the discovery comes from.** The inland-brown, riverside-blue pattern is the tables' (unconstrained, more crape inland in 86 per cent of maps); the within-street comparison coming out for the water is the rates' (the calibration check runs it at compression 5 and 20 without floors). The rule that only a shared street answers the doctor is the paper's argument stated as a rule, and the verdict names it as such.

## 18. Playing it in numbers (build.md §3)

Read: where you test is the game. The doctor's comparison and the obvious one (go where the crape is) leave the water and the side of town tangled in most games; going to the mixed streets and testing beside a crape door answers him three games in four. The *flat* and *weak* bands are small samples (a brown crape house beside a blue house that also died, at one in twenty); the verdict says so. No option is dead: every house can be tested and every test paints the map; what differs is whether it can answer the objection on screen.
