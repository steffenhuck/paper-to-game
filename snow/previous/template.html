London · July 1854

Snow’sCloudy Glass

Two water companies pipe the same streets of south London, so that one house drinks from one and the house next door from the other. The Southwark and Vauxhall Company pumps from the Thames at Battersea, inside the town, where the sewers run in and the tide brings salt up from the sea. The Lambeth Company moved its works upriver to Thames Ditton in 1852, above both.

Cholera came in July. Most doctors blamed the air of crowded, dirty, low-lying streets. Dr John Snow went to the houses where people had died and asked which water they drank.

Where someone has died, black cloth hangs on the door: crape. A few drops of silver turn salt water milky, so a glass from the tap says which pipe a house is on, whatever the people in it think.

How to play

Find out whether the water makes the difference between the doors with crape and the doors without.

The street

Eighteen houses. Where someone has died of cholera, black cloth hangs over the door: that is crape. Every house is on one of two pipes, and you cannot see which. Battersea water is the Southwark and Vauxhall Company’s, pumped from the river inside the town. Thames Ditton water is the Lambeth Company’s, from above the town.

A knock

  1. Tap a door. The person who answers says which company they think they are on, or that they cannot say. A glass is drawn from the tap. Nothing is spent yet; you can tap another door instead.
  2. Press Add the silver drops. That spends one of your seven knocks on this street. Salt water turns milky; Battersea water is salt with the tide, Thames Ditton water is not. So a milky glass means Battersea water and a clear glass means Thames Ditton water, whatever the person said.
  3. The door is painted the colour of its water, and one small house goes into the window.

The window

Four panes. The top row holds the doors with crape you have tested; the bottom row the doors without. The left panes are brown, Battersea water; the right panes are blue, Thames Ditton water. The line under it counts the rows. You choose which doors to spend your knocks on.

The fourth street

On Borough Road the companies’ books are open: every doorstep already shows its water. Mark the five doors you think will wear crape by the end of the month, then press Come back in a month.

The verdict

What Snow found, what you found, where the two part, and what would have followed had yours stood; your window beside his.
