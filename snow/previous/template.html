London · July 1854

Snow’sCloudy Glass

Two companies sell water in south London. The Southwark and Vauxhall Company pumps it from the Thames inside the town, where the sewers run in and the tide brings salt up from the sea. The Lambeth Company pumps it upriver, above all that. Their pipes run down the same streets, so one house drinks one water and the house next door the other, and nobody can see which.

Cholera came in July. Where someone has died, black cloth hangs on the door: crape. Most of it is inland, away from the river. The parish doctor says: the inland air.

You have a glass and a bottle of silver drops. Salt water turns milky with them; the town’s water is salt, the upriver water is not. Eight houses’ worth of time. Where do you test?

What your hands will do

Tap a house.
Add the silver drops.

How to play

Find out what does it, the water or the side of town: test houses so that the doctor cannot say “that is just the inland air”.

The map

Six streets, six houses each, from the river at the bottom to the inland side of town at the top. Where someone has died of cholera, black cloth hangs over the door: crape. Every house is on one of two pipes, and you cannot see which until you test it. Battersea water is the Southwark and Vauxhall Company’s, pumped from the river inside the town. Thames Ditton water is the Lambeth Company’s, from above the town.

A test

  1. Tap any house. It shows you its street and what that street’s tests have said so far. Nothing is spent yet; you can tap another house instead.
  2. Press Add the silver drops. That spends one of your eight tests. Salt water turns milky, so a milky glass means Battersea water and a clear glass means Thames Ditton water. The door is painted that colour.
  3. Every street keeps a tally of its tested houses: brown so many, with crape so many; blue the same. The line under the map adds them up, first over all your tests, then over the streets where you have tested both a brown and a blue house.

The doctor

He speaks after every test. He will say that your brown houses are inland and inland is where the crape is, as long as that is true of your tests. He has nothing to say to a brown house and a blue house in the same street.

The window

Four panes: the same-street tests only. Top row the houses with crape, bottom row the houses without; brown on the left, blue on the right.

The Board

When the eight tests are spent, send the Board one sentence: the inland air, the town’s water, or that these tests cannot say. The verdict reads your sentence against your own tests and against Snow, and shows the map with every pipe revealed.
