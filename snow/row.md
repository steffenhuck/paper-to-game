| **Snow's Cloudy Glass** | Snow (1856), *Cholera and the Water Supply in the South Districts of London in 1854*, reprinted from the *Journal of Public Health*, October 1856. The grand experiment as a lesson in spatial identification, on an 1854 London handbill: a map of six of Snow's subdistricts from the river inland, six houses each, pipes mixed house by house in each street's real proportion, and crape already on the doors, mostly inland. The parish doctor says the inland air. You have a glass and the silver drops: tap a house, watch the glass turn milky or stay clear, and the door is painted; every tested house goes into a four-pane window, hollow until its street has both waters tested, and the doctor answers each glass as far as your evidence lets him. He concedes the moment one street holds a brown house with crape and a blue house without, and your count of glasses is fixed there: two or three if you go to the mixed streets, nine or never if you go to the crape. One sentence for the Board, then a verdict that reads your tests before your sentence, calls a right guess a guess, and reveals every pipe.
