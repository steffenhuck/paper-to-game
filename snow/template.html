London · July 1854

Snow’sCloudy Glass

Two companies sell water in south London. The Southwark and Vauxhall Company pumps it from the Thames inside the town, where the sewers run in and the tide brings salt up from the sea. The Lambeth Company pumps it upriver, above all that. Their pipes run down the same streets, so one house drinks one water and the house next door the other, and nobody can see which.

Cholera came in July. Where someone has died, black cloth hangs on the door: crape. Most of it is inland, away from the river. The parish doctor says: the inland air.

You have a glass and a bottle of silver drops. Salt water turns milky with them; the town’s water is salt, the upriver water is not. Each glass costs you a house call. Where do you test, so that the doctor has to concede, and how few glasses does it take?

How to play

Make the water stand alone: test houses so that the doctor cannot say “that is just the inland air”, with as few glasses as you can.

The map

Six streets, six houses each, from the river at the bottom to the inland side of town at the top. Where someone has died of cholera, black cloth hangs over the door: crape. Every house is on one of two pipes, and you cannot see which until you test it. Battersea water is the Southwark and Vauxhall Company’s, pumped from the river inside the town. Thames Ditton water is the Lambeth Company’s, from above the town.

A test

  1. Tap any house. It shows you its street and what that street’s tests have said so far. Nothing is spent yet; you can tap another house instead.
  2. Press Add the silver drops. That is one glass. Salt water turns milky, so a milky glass means Battersea water and a clear glass means Thames Ditton water. The door is painted that colour.
  3. Every street keeps a tally of its tested houses. The line under the map adds them up, first over all your tests, then over the streets where you have tested both a brown and a blue house.

The window

Every tested house goes in: top row the houses with crape, bottom row the houses without; brown on the left, blue on the right. A house is hollow while its street has only one water tested, because the doctor can wave it away (“that street is inland”), and fills in solid the moment its street has both.

The doctor

He speaks after every test. He will say that your brown houses are inland and inland is where the crape is, as long as that is true of your tests. He concedes when, in one street, you have tested a brown house with crape and a blue house, and the crape is at the brown. Your count of glasses is fixed at that moment; you may keep testing to see more of the map.

The Board

Whenever you like after the first glass, and at ten at the latest, write the Board one sentence: the inland air, the town’s water, or that these tests cannot say. The verdict reads your tests first: did the water stand alone? Then your sentence, judged against your tests and not against the answer. Then how many glasses it took.
