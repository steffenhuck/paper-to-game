VERDICT (identified)
  HEAD: The water stands alone.
  IDENT: In the same street, where the side of town is the same for both: brown 1 of 1 with crape, blue 0 of 1. No blue house there wears crape; the brown ones do. The doctor cannot answer that with the air, because the air is the same at both doors. That is identification: the one thing that differs between the two houses is the one thing that can explain the difference between them.
  SENTENCE: You wrote: “It is the Southwark and Vauxhall Company’s water.” Right, and your tests carry it.
  SPEED: The doctor conceded at your third glass. The quickest plan, a crape door in a street where the pipes are mixed and then its neighbours until one comes up clear, gets there at the second or third glass in most games. Testing where the crape is takes nine or more, when it gets there at all.
  HAD: Had the question been answered from lists instead of at the door, as the Board of Health answered it, the gap would have shrunk to three and a half to one, because with deaths six times as common behind one company’s doors, five mistakes in six move a death from the guilty water to the innocent one. Yours was answered at the door, with the glass, in the same street.
