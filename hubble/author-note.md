# Hubble's Twelve Nights — note for the author

1. Look first at the night-one staging, `screenshot-eyepiece-stage1/2/3.png` and `screenshot-spectrograph-stage1/2/3.png` (the instruments assembling, one tap each), then `screenshot-spectrograph-virgo.png` (the sandwich: lamp, nebula, lamp, with the crosshairs on the bars), then `screenshot-plate-first-far.png` (the first Virgo nebula landing far right, with Hubble's dotted dots under the player's) and `screenshot-humason-spectrum.png` (NGC 7619's bars a long way to the red).
2. Everything tunable is in the `CONFIG` block at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: Table 1 with the catalogue coordinates, Hubble's solar motion and his two K values, the brightest-star constant −6.3, the H and K wavelengths and the spectrograph's window, the twelve nights and the one/two/three-night costs, the dial ranges and the two reading-error bounds the heuristics assume. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (53 checks against the paper and the game's framing), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to finish if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the costs and the budget. Twelve nights with 1/2/3-night prices make the far nebulae expensive, which is the deliberation; the heuristics table in the memo shows the decision matters (Virgo-only shows nothing, near-only shows the relation only after the Sun's motion comes off, a mix shows it best). (b) The eyepiece reads every nebula by the brightest-star rule, including the six Hubble did by Cepheids and the four Virgo nebulae he did by cluster brightness; the About screen says so. (c) The paper's tables were transcribed from a scan with a bad OCR layer; the numbers were checked against the standard transcriptions of this paper and the calibration recovers Hubble's own solution from them, but a second pair of eyes on the 24 rows would be welcome.

Revised four times after play (memo, sections 19 to 21 and the wording note): split-field eyepiece, bars and bracket in the spectrograph, a self-running first night, Hubble's dots after each reading, a plate that describes and a verdict that concludes; then the in-play grading removed; then the instruments rebuilt so their parts assemble in front of the player on night one (a sandwich of lamp, nebula, lamp with crosshairs on a measuring screw; a comparison star brought nearer or pushed farther).

This game has had the builder's review and four plays only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and the five-minute length is an estimate.
