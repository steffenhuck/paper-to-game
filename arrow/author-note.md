What we were least sure about: (a) the middle of the game takes the choice away on purpose for two Sundays; that is Arrow's proof (eqs. 1 to 6) happening to the player, but it is the reckless part of the design; (b) the score out of 36 and the number 34 are ours, not Arrow's, and the verdict says so in one grey sentence that may be too quiet; (c) "custom" for a fixed order that settles every disagreement leans on the paper's first page and on Definition 4 without being either; (d) ballots have no ties, so the rule book has six rulings where Arrow's general case has more; (e) the DOI (10.1086/256963) and the JSTOR link are from memory and could not be opened from the session; (f) the PDF was a scan, but no number is taken from it that the check does not recompute.

Your question about the dictator is in the game three times: the line after a Sunday whose list is one voter's ballot ("One Sunday proves nothing"), the corner tabs on the sheet, and the first of the three questions on the verdict screen.
