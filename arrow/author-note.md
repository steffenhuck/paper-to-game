What we were least sure about: (a) the middle of the game takes the choice away on purpose for two Sundays; that is Arrow's proof (eqs. 1 to 6) happening to the player, and you have not yet said how it plays; (b) Clara's ballot is fixed, so her act shows the paradox of voting inside the two-voter game and is not Arrow's theorem for three voters; the verdict says so under "Would more voters fix it?"; (c) the score out of 36 and the number 34 are ours, not Arrow's; (d) Sunday 1 still carries the most text on any screen: what is settled, what a draw means, why there is no all-draw answer; (e) the last answer on the verdict ("So is voting broken?") speaks to the sixteen-year-old directly and goes a step beyond the paper; (f) the DOI (10.1086/256963) and the JSTOR link are from memory and could not be opened from the session.
