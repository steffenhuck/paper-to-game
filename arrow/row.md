| **Voting with Arrow** | Arrow (1950), *Journal of Political Economy* 58(4):328–346. A difficulty in the concept of social welfare, as a Swiss ballot paper in red, black and white: voting is easy, counting is the hard part. Two voters, three plans for the village square, one vote next Sunday, and you write the counting rule first, with no counting of points, because a ballot says only what is above what. Arrow brings three what-ifs (what if Anna writes this and Bruno that?); where the two disagree it is a tie, your answer breaks it, and the book keeps the answer. Then he checks all 36 ways the vote can go, struck through where your own answers go round in a circle; then the real Sunday, drawn by chance and counted by your book. Two rules: never contradict yourself, nobody the boss; a score from 0 to 100, where 100 still has two contradictions in it and a boss scores 0. The debrief: why the best is 34 and not 36, Arrow's proof in nine pictures, why no points, and all 729 books tried on all 36 votes, two of them clean, each a dictator. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/arrow/voting-with-arrow.html) · [memo](arrow/memo.md) |
