| **Voting with Arrow** | Arrow (1950), *Journal of Political Economy* 58(4):328–346. A difficulty in the concept of social welfare, as a Swiss ballot paper in red, black and white: voting is the easy part, counting is where it gets hard. Two voters, three plans for the village square, and you count: where the two disagree it is one vote each, a tie, and you break it, the same tie the same way every Sunday. For three Sundays you choose; on the fourth and fifth your own tie-breaks leave one ranking and finish the book for you. Then Arrow checks all 36 pairs of ballots the two could ever hand in: squares struck through where the results chase each other in a circle, or one voter's corner on every square. Then hire a tie-breaker: Clara, a third voter who never changes her mind, gives a real majority and still two Sundays in a circle; Anna or Beat gives a boss. Of the 729 tie-break books there are, two never fail, and each is a dictator. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/arrow/voting-with-arrow.html) · [memo](arrow/memo.md) |
