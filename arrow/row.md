| **Voting with Arrow** | Arrow (1950), *Journal of Political Economy* 58(4):328–346. A difficulty in the concept of social welfare, as a Swiss ballot paper in red, black and white: two voters, three plans for the village square, and you are the counting rule. Every voting Sunday you turn their two ballots into one list, and whatever you rule when they disagree is kept. For three Sundays you choose; on the fourth and fifth your own rulings leave one list and write the rest of the rule book for you. Then Arrow checks all 36 pairs of ballots the two could ever hand in: squares struck through where your rulings chase each other in a circle, or one voter's corner on every square. Of the 729 rule books there are, two never fail, and each is a dictator. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/arrow/voting-with-arrow.html) · [memo](arrow/memo.md) |
