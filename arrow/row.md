| **Voting with Arrow** | Arrow (1950), *Journal of Political Economy* 58(4):328–346. A difficulty in the concept of social welfare, as a Swiss ballot paper in red, black and white: voting is easy, counting is the hard part. Two voters, three plans for the village square, one vote next Sunday, and you write the counting rule first. Arrow brings three what-ifs (what if Anna writes this and Beat that?); where the two disagree it is a tie, your answer breaks it, and the book keeps the answer. Then he checks all 36 ways the vote can go, struck through where your own answers go round in a circle; then the real Sunday, drawn by chance and counted by your book. The debrief: three other tie-breakers (a third voter who never changes her mind still leaves two circles; a voter who breaks every tie is a boss), Arrow's proof in nine pictures, and all 729 books tried on all 36 votes, two of them clean, each a dictator. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/arrow/voting-with-arrow.html) · [memo](arrow/memo.md) |
