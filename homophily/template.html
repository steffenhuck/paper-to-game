HOW DO THEY KNOW IT'S NOT JUST BETTER PROPOSALS?

The researchers compared the same proposal as marked by its different judges, so quality is held fixed. They also compared each judge with their own marks for other applicants, so a generous judge is not mistaken for a biased one. The extra marks are top marks, not a wider spread, so it does not look like inside knowledge. And the bonus stays when they account for judges who work on the same topics or have written papers with the same people.

The study calls it homophily: favouring people like yourself. It does not say whether anyone means to.

HOW TO PLAY

The European Research Council gives out big grants. Each research field has a panel of about 15 judges, and each proposal is marked by about four of them.

Tokens. Every judge is a token. The ring is their passport. The middle is the country they work in. Yellow means one of the five biggest applicant countries (Germany, the UK, France, Italy, Spain). Teal means any other country. Lilac means outside Europe.

The home bonus. The study found that a judge gives the same proposal about a sixth of a point more (marks run 1–5) when the applicant shares the judge's passport and workplace. That is a home star. Sharing only the passport is worth about half a star. Sharing only the workplace is worth about a third.

The jars. BIG FIVE is for applicants who work in those five countries; EVERYONE ELSE is for the rest. Each jar shows how many home stars an average applicant in that group collects per 10 juries. The dashed line is the real ERC's level.

  • Round 1: tap judges in the pool to seat them; tap a seated judge to send them back. When the 15th seat is taken, the jars are uncovered.
  • Round 2: a panel built like the ERC's. Tap a judge at the table, then one on the bench, to swap them. You have four swaps. Get the jars within half a star of each other. RESET starts the round again.

Tap any judge to see whom they favour, and how many applicants that is.
