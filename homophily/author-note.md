# Home Judges — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-r1-reveal.png` and `screenshot-r2-scrolled.png`. The game turns on two readings: a panel that mirrors who applies leans to the big five (your Table 2, at about 2½×), and taking a big-country home judge off moves the jars far more than adding a small-country judge. Then read the verdict (`screenshot-end.png`), which carries your estimates and, labelled as correlational, your 1.7 vs 0.8 funding numbers.
2. Every number is in the `CONFIG` block at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism, tagged with its source (Supp. Tables 1–2, Table 1, Table 2, p. 4–5) or "our choice": the bonuses, the applicant population, the round 1 pool, the round 2 panel and bench, the swap budget and the level target. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (35 checks against your estimates and Table 2), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to finish if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the applicant population (51 passport/workplace types, about a quarter working abroad) is our construction; it lands near your six Table 2 shares but misses "others, citizenship only" by a point; (b) random assignment within the panel and one average bonus for every judge are simplifications the ERC's expertise-based assignment does not follow; (c) the aim, levelling the jars, is the game's goal, not a recommendation from the paper; the About screen says so.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and the four-minute length is an estimate. The previous game from this paper, *One of Us*, is in `previous/`.
