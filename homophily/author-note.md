# Home Judges — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-r1-reveal.png` and `screenshot-r2-selected.png`. In Round 1, a panel that mirrors the panel-member file gives applicants with both a Big Five passport and a Big Five workplace about five times the tokens of applicants with neither. In Round 2, benching Big Five home judges moves the GAP meter; swapping smaller-country judges for each other barely does. The GAP compares those two named groups only; the two mixed groups are shown as bars but left out (revision 3, memo §22).
2. Everything is in the `CONFIG` block at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the embedded applicant file, the token rule (3 / 1 / 0), the three rosters, the swap budget, the LEVEL zone (±0.3 tokens) and the meter's range. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (31 checks), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to finish if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the token rule is a game scale, not the paper's estimates (with the paper's own relative sizes the same lessons hold; see calibration §6); (b) judges are drawn at random from the panel, while the ERC assigns by expertise; (c) the rosters are hand-curated from the panel-member file, and the LEVEL zone and four swaps are game choices. The applicant file has 30 rows, not the 50 the brief mentioned; all 30 are used.

Iteration 2 (tokens, the authors' data, a fixed phone screen) is described in memo §21; revision 3 (the published citation, the Big Five legend and colour key, the US coloured like every other non-Big-Five country, the gap as BOTH minus NEITHER) in memo §22.
