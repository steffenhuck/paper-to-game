WHAT YOU DID

WHAT THE PAPER FOUND

Reviewers give higher scores to applicants who share their passport or work country. The bonus is strongest when both match.

WHAT THE DATA SHOWS

Applicants who work in Big Five countries meet matching reviewers more often, so they receive more home advantage overall.

ABOUT

THE PAPER

Nicola Fuchs-Schündeln and Felix Holub, Homophily in Grant Evaluations (WZB Berlin / Goethe University Frankfurt, manuscript). Using 37,073 ERC applications, they compare the same proposal across its reviewers: reviewers who share the applicant's citizenship or country of work give higher scores, most of all when both match.

THE GAME

The applicant mix is the authors' applicant file (30 passport and work-country combinations with their shares), built into the page. The judges are curated from their panel-member file. Two simplifications are the game's own: the token rule (both match 3, one match 1, none 0) is a game scale, not the paper's 1–5 scores; and each applicant's judges are drawn at random from the 15 on the panel (about four each), while the ERC assigns by expertise.

So the game demonstrates how panel composition spreads home advantage across applicant groups under these simplifications. It assumes the token rule and random assignment. The paper establishes that matched reviewers score the same proposal higher; it does not test other panels. The LEVEL target and the four swaps are game choices, not recommendations.
