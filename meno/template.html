    How to play

    Bring the boy to a square twice this size, by asking, not telling, before Meno has to go.

    The floor. A grey-green square is laid in it, two tiles each way: four tiles. A square twice its size holds eight tiles. Glowing pegs mark where a line from the square's corner can end: along the floor, or from corner to corner across the tiles. Tap a peg to lay a line to it; the square on that line shows faintly. Tap it again to lift it.

    Your answer. Before the lesson you tap the line you would choose yourself. Nobody acts on it.

    The lesson. You have Meno's five questions. Each button uses one.

    • Ask him: which line? (no line laid): he lays the line he believes in, in white, and counts the square on it.
    • Ask him: how big is the square on this line? (a line laid): if he still thinks he knows, he ignores your line and tests his own; once he has said "I do not know", he counts the square on yours.
    • Tell him: it's this line. He says yes. He does not count it.

    The count. He lays a white tile on every tile of the square, then the halves, two halves to a tile. The column at the right of the floor stacks what he counted against two marks: four (the first square) and eight (twice). Tiles past eight are red.

    Colours. White is his own: what he lays, counts and says. Ochre is yours. Grey-green was given.

    His statue. Beside him stands a small statue of what he believes. A count that proves him wrong knocks it over. A line he counts himself raises a white statue, tied down. A line you tell him raises an ochre one, not tied.

    The end of the lesson. When he has a line for eight, by his count or by your word, or when Meno has to go.

    Next morning. Meno gives him a square of three tiles each way, nine, and asks for its double. You only watch. Then the end screen says where he stands, on four steps: sure and wrong; stuck; right, but told; right, and found.
