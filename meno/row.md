| **Double the Square with Plato** | Plato (c. 385 BCE), *Meno*, tr. Benjamin Jowett. A Greek floor laid in black, white and ochre tiles: you are Socrates, and a slave boy who has never had a lesson stands at a square of four tiles. Bring him to a square twice the size, by asking, not telling, before Meno has to go. Five questions: ask him which line (he lays his own and counts it: double the side, sixteen; three, nine; then "I do not know"), lay a line from the corner and ask how big the square on it is, or tell him the answer. While he thinks he knows he will not look at your line; a count that proves him wrong knocks over the little statue of his belief. Next morning Meno gives him a square of three: the boy who counted the corner-to-corner line himself does it again, the boy you told repeats yesterday's line, the boy told while still sure goes back to doubling. The end screen puts him on four steps: sure and wrong, stuck, right but told, right and found. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/meno/double-the-square-with-plato.html) · [memo](meno/memo.md) |
