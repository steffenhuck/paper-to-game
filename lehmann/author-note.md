# Find Your Inner Core with Lehmann — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-6-graze-core.png` (a dive of 33½° coming up at 175° where 34° came up at 112°: the jump), then `screenshot-a-reveal.png` (the whole fan, the hatched shadow and its four dark posts), `screenshot-b-3-result.png` (a faster ball: rays glancing off it into the shadow, all four posts lit) and `screenshot-end.png`. Then play it once on a phone.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: Lehmann's shells (radii and speeds, with their pages), her own ray results used by the check (33.75°, 112.5°, 186.2°, 153.9°, 16°, 160.7°, 14.85°, 118.8°), the observed limits of the real shadow, and everything ours (18 posts every ten degrees, a three-degree ear, twelve waves, the dials' ranges and starting places, three quakes, the Moon as the yardstick for the ball's size, the words). Edit, run `node calibration.js` (43 checks and the size-by-speed table), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to build if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the citation: only the *Episodes* commentary was available, not Lehmann's original, and the article's DOI could not be verified from the session, so the About screen cites volume and pages and links the journal; (b) the posts are placed at 10°, 20°, …, 180° so that the four in the shadow (120°–150°) are all reached by Lehmann's ball, whose arrivals begin at 118.8°; with posts at 115° the sliver her own model leaves dark (112.5°–118.8°) would show, and the check records that case; (c) the drum's up-and-down is the cosine of the dive, standing for the vertical component the article discusses, not a computed amplitude; (d) a much slower ball (three quarters of the core's speed) also fills the shadow in this model, from the far side, and the verdict says so rather than hiding it; (e) the dial stays where the player left it, against the skill's reset rule, and the − and + buttons move a tenth of a degree, so the span between 33° and 34° can be searched; the line that says "something big happens between 33.5° and 34°" is the one place the game comments on the player's choice, by your request; (f) the ball's size is fixed at Lehmann's and only its speed is played (revision 1); `quake()` still takes a size if the dial is wanted back.
