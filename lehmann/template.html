A game from Inge Lehmann’s paper “P′” (1936)

Lehmann’s Shadow

The Earth is a ball. When the ground shakes somewhere, the shaking runs right through the ball and comes out on the far side. You are the earthquake: a wave sent through the ball. Find out how it bends, through your listening posts, which record what comes out on the other side.

Choose the direction of your wave: does it go straight through the middle, through the core, just touch it, or pass it by? Each time, the listening posts tell you what they recorded. Pay attention to it, and you will learn how the Earth is made up, right down to its inner core. Twelve waves; then a puzzle, and what Inge Lehmann found in 1936.

How to play

Light as many listening posts as you can with twelve waves. Then find what sends waves into the shadow.

The Earth is a ball. When the ground shakes somewhere, the shaking runs right through the ball and comes out on the far side. You are that shaking: one wave. The picture is the Earth cut open, as Inge Lehmann drew it in 1936. Ochre is the mantle, rock where a wave runs fast. Rust is the core, where a wave runs slower, so going in it bends toward the middle and coming out it bends back. A wave runs straight and bends only where something changes. The burst at the top is the quake, where you start. The dots round the right-hand edge are eighteen listening posts, one every ten degrees round from the quake. A post feels a wave that comes up within three degrees of it, and lights up.

  1. Dive. Slide the dial: 0° dives straight down through the middle, 90° runs along the ground. The − and + buttons move a tenth of a degree, for dives very close together. A dotted stub at the quake shows the direction. The dial stays where you leave it.
  2. Fire the wave. Watch yourself run, bend and come up. The box under the Earth says where you came up, whether a post felt you, and how the ground there bounced; the strip above it draws that bounce. A steep wave makes the ground bounce up and down hard; a flat one hardly at all.
  3. The job. After six waves one post gets a ring round it. Try to hit it with the six waves you have left.
  4. After the twelfth wave, every dive fires at once, and you see the band on the far side where no wave comes up: the shadow.
  5. The real posts. A card shows what three real listening posts inside the shadow wrote down in 1929. They felt a wave. So something sends waves into the shadow.
  6. Dive again. The same Earth, the same dial, twelve more waves. Light the four shadow posts. If you are bent where no colour changes, you have touched something hidden, and it leaves a white mark.

The verdict says what you found, what Inge Lehmann found, and what became of her idea.
