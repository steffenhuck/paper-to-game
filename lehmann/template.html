A game from Inge Lehmann’s paper “P′” (1936)

Find Your Inner Core with Lehmann

The Earth is a ball. When the ground shakes somewhere, the shaking runs right through the ball and comes out on the far side. You are the earthquake: a wave sent through the ball. Find out how it bends, through your listening posts, which record what comes out on the other side.

Choose the direction of your wave: does it go straight through the core, just touch it, or pass it by?

In 1936 everyone thought the Earth had two layers: a mantle, and one core inside it. But after real earthquakes most listening posts shook hard, and one band of them only quivered. If an Earth with one core can do the same, it is right. If it can’t, something is missing, and there might be a core within the core.

How to play

Does the Earth have one core, or a core within the core?

The picture is the Earth cut open. Ochre is the mantle, rock where a wave runs fast. Rust is the core, where a wave runs slower, so going in it bends toward the middle and coming out it bends back. A wave runs straight and bends only where the colour changes. The burst at the top is the earthquake. The dots round the right-hand edge are eighteen listening posts.

  1. The facts. Three real earthquakes, each shown when you press the button. You see the waves run through the Earth along the paths everyone agreed on, and what the posts recorded each time, kept as three rows of dots. Most light up. One band only quivers. That band is then marked. There is nothing for you to do here but watch.
  2. Imagine one core. Now you are the earthquake, in an imagined Earth with one core. Aim by dragging on the picture or sliding the dial: 0° dives straight down through the middle, 90° runs along the ground. The − and + buttons move a tenth of a degree. Fire, and the post nearest to where you come up lights. Try to make the band quiver. Four goes, then one last try.
  3. Every direction at once. The game then fires every direction you could have chosen. This is not a go of yours; just watch.
  4. Imagine a core within the core. Three goes in an imagined Earth with a second, smaller core inside the core. Only a dive steeper than 16° gets that deep. Then every direction at once again, and the verdict.
