Lehmann’s Shadow

The Earth is a ball. When the ground shakes somewhere, the shaking runs right through the ball and comes out on the far side, where listening posts feel it. You are that shaking: one wave.

Choose how steeply to dive in, fire, and see where you come out: the stuff inside the Earth bends you on the way. Twelve waves to light as many posts as you can. Then a puzzle: some posts can never be reached, unless something is hidden in the middle. That is what Inge Lehmann found in 1936.

How to play

Light as many listening posts as you can with twelve waves.

The Earth is a ball. When the ground shakes somewhere, the shaking runs right through the ball and comes out on the far side. You are that shaking: one wave. The picture is the Earth cut open, as Inge Lehmann drew it in 1936. Ochre is the mantle, rock where a wave runs fast. Rust is the core, where a wave runs slower, so going in it bends toward the middle and coming out it bends back. A wave runs straight and bends only where the colour changes. The burst at the top is the quake, where you start. The dots round the right-hand edge are eighteen listening posts, one every ten degrees round from the quake. A post feels a wave that comes up within three degrees of it, and lights up.

  1. Dive. Slide the dial: 0° dives straight down through the middle, 90° runs along the ground. The − and + buttons move a tenth of a degree, for when you want to try dives very close together. A dotted stub at the quake shows the direction. The dial stays where you leave it.
  2. Fire the wave. Watch yourself run, bend and come up. The box under the Earth says where you came up, whether a post felt you, and how the ground there bounced; the strip above it draws that bounce. Every wave stays drawn, so your twelve waves build Lehmann’s own figure.
  3. After the twelfth wave, every dive fires at once, and you see the band on the far side where no wave ever comes up: the shadow.
  4. The ball. In 1936 the posts in the shadow felt something anyway. Lehmann’s idea: a smaller ball inside the core, where waves run at a different speed. One dial sets that speed: slower than in the core around it, the same, or faster. Where rock gets slower, a wave bends toward the middle; where it gets faster, a wave bends away, and a wave hitting faster rock at a glancing angle bounces off. Press Quake and every dive fires at once. Three quakes: make the four shadow posts feel one.

The verdict says what your ball did, what hers did, and what became of her idea.
