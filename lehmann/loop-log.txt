VERDICT
  Your ball does what hers does.
  You fired 13 waves into Lehmann’s Earth of two shells and found the shadow: four posts, from 120° to 150° round from the quake, that no angle could reach. Then you put a ball a bit smaller than the Moon inside the core, running a little faster than the core, and all four shadow posts heard the quake.
  --
  Lehmann’s ball was a bit smaller than the Moon (about 1400 km from its centre to its edge) and ran a little faster than the core. Waves bounce off it and come up from about 119° round, right inside the shadow, arriving steeply, with a strong up-and-down, and stronger the further round they land: what the posts had recorded. She called it a hypothesis that seemed probable and could not be proved from the data at hand.
  --
  It held. Two years later Gutenberg and Richter computed an inner core about 1200 km from centre to edge; in 1939 Jeffreys showed the creeping-round explanation could not work; by 1946 the inner ball was agreed to be solid. The Earth is a ball inside a ball inside a ball, and the innermost was found with cardboard cards, oatmeal boxes and a pencil.
  "We take it that, as before, the Earth consists of a core and a mantle, but that inside the core there is an inner core in which the velocity is larger than in the outer one."
