# Coase's Cows — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-q1-year2-tally.png` (the first quarrel ruled a second time, a year on: two signs side by side, the same valley, the money the other way, which is where the Coase theorem lands and is now produced by the player rather than announced by a ghost card), `screenshot-q4-tally.png` (the chimney under liability: the works buys the $12 filter because it cannot buy $5 of clothes horses from a hundred houses) and `screenshot-q5-tally.png` (the railway under liability: no trains, $210 of $230, and the line that one train would be best and no ruling gets it). Then play it once ruling "make them pay" every time, which is most strangers' first run: it now misses the lawyer, the chimney and the railway, finds three of six, and the verdict names the three and says why.
2. Everything tunable is in `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: `CONFIG` at the top carries every number with its page in Coase or its reason (the meat values, the corn's worth, the lawyer's fee, the turnips, the chimney's and the orchard's figures are ours, including the two fixes, the works' $12 filter and the houses' $5 of clothes horses; the rest is Coase's); the six quarrels with their facts, rulings, signs and the preview lines are in `quarrels()`; every sentence the game says during play is in `beats()`, `plantLine()`, `countLineLiable()` and `resultLines()`; the verdict's lines, the reasons a ruling missed and the fates are in `verdict()` and `missLine()`; the year-after comparison is `compareLines()`. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (120 checks against the paper), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to finish if the embedded mechanism differs from the file. The drawings are in `template.html` (`buildFarm`, `buildSmoke`, `buildRail`, `icon`).
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the third steer brings $1, not $2, so that every bargain settles in whole dollars on screen; Coase's text allows either, and the check confirms the discoveries hold with $2 too (half dollars aside); (b) the chimney's and the orchard's figures are entirely ours, built on the setting Coase gives and no numbers; the chimney now turns on the sufferers having the cheaper fix (clothes horses, no agreement needed) while the works can only buy the dear one, which is Coase's p. 9 "or whether the occupants should move" and the reciprocity of section II rather than a literal figure from the paper; (c) the verdict counts quarrels found (the first by its first year), not dollars, so that the railway's hundreds do not let the fair instinct look nearly right; the dollar sum is still shown; (d) under "the harmer must pay" the sufferer plants whatever is worth most standing, since he is made whole, which is what makes the strip get planted in the railway quarrel and the corn stay corn in the lawyer's; this is Coase's own reasoning (pp. 15–16) but it is applied uniformly; (e) in the smoke quarrel only the works can fit the filter, which stands in for the hundred households' inability to organise as much as for the physics.
