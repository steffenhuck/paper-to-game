# Singer's Chalk Line — design memo

Built 25 November 2026 under the `paper-to-game` skill, from the PDF alone.

## 0. Given

Mine (Steffen's signature and counter), author title, no ideas. Every choice below is the engine's; nothing overrode precedent.

## 1. Paper

Peter Singer, *Animal Liberation* (New York: Avon Books, 1990, new revised edition), pages 1–23, the opening chapter "All Animals Are Equal"; first edition 1975. What was available: the excerpt, "sometimes simplified in wording", printed as *Animal Liberation* in Harry J. Gensler, Earl W. Spurgin and James C. Swindal (eds.), *Ethics: Contemporary Readings* (London: Routledge, 2004), pages 284–292, with the editor's headnote, study questions and notes. Nine pages, no data, no figures. The chapter grew from Singer's essay "All Animals Are Equal", *Philosophic Exchange* 1(5), 1974, which is online open-access at the SUNY Brockport repository; the 1973 *New York Review of Books* piece of the same title as the book is behind a paywall. The PDF stays in `inbox/` and is not committed.

## 2. What the paper hands over

An **argument**, and nothing else: no model, no estimate. Its parts, in the paper's order: (i) equality cannot rest on factual sameness, because people differ and because a "more sophisticated opponent of equality" could then draw the line anywhere a fact lets him (IQ below 100); (ii) equality is a prescription, not a description: equal *consideration* of like interests, which permits different treatment; (iii) the boundary of consideration is the capacity to suffer or enjoy, "the only defensible boundary"; (iv) speciesism has the same shape as racism and sexism, "the pattern is identical"; (v) animals feel pain, inferred from the same signs we use for other humans; (vi) the practical scale: "the same amount of pain" is defined by the blow that produces it (a slap for a baby, a heavy stick for a horse), and counts the same whoever feels it; (vii) killing is a different scale: the value of a life depends on self-awareness, plans, ties, and so a normal human is normally saved over a mouse, but "whatever criteria we choose … they do not follow precisely the boundary of our own species": a chimpanzee, dog or pig outranks an infant born with massive brain damage or a person in advanced senility; (viii) the painkiller: with one dose and two injured humans of different capacities, "it is not so clear how we ought to choose", because the evil of pain is unaffected by the sufferer's other characteristics, while the value of life is.

Formalised here as a toy: a cast of eleven beings taken from the paper's own examples, each with a reading on four rulers the paper names (reasoning; self-awareness and plans for a future; close ties to others; the capacity to feel pain), the readings being the paper's orderings drawn as chalk marks of lengths that are ours; a line on a ruler is a cut in the sorted cast; the species boundary is a fifth ruler that is not a measurement. For the second half, two scales: a pain scale on which a dose goes where the pain is, and a life scale on which a rescue goes where the plans are, with species carrying no weight on either. Where the formalisation chose something the paper did not: section 15.

## 3. The discovery, and its kind

**A counterintuitive result**, twice over, and a mechanism made intelligible between them. The stranger believes two things: that there is some difference between humans and animals that puts every human in and every animal out of moral concern; and that Singer, if he denies that, must think a mouse is worth a man. The paper shows that no measurable difference draws the line at the species, only the species itself, which is the racist's kind of line; and that denying the line does not make a mouse a man, because pain and life are counted on two different scales. The mechanism in between: equality as a rule about how to treat, not a fact about who is treated.

## 4. The core narrative

Some people say animals do not count the way people do. Singer says: show me the ruler. Being clever? A baby is not clever, and a chimpanzee is cleverer than some people will ever be. Having friends who would miss you? Some dogs have more than some people. The only ruler that puts every person on one side and every animal on the other is the one that just says "person" on it, and that is the ruler a bully uses when he says "not one of us". So Singer says: if it can hurt, it counts, whoever it is, and a bigger hurt counts more than a smaller one, whoever it is. But a hurt and a life are different things. A life with plans in it is worth more than a life without, and that is true whoever is living it, which is usually the person, and sometimes not.

## 5. Who lives in the paper

- **"We"**, the people who want to defend women's rights and keep their speciesism, who must answer the parody and the sophisticated opponent. Controls: which difference to appeal to, and where to put the line. Could be asked to anticipate: which being each ruler leaves on the wrong side.
- **The sophisticated opponent of equality**, who draws the line at IQ 100. Controls: the criterion. The same hands as "we", with the opposite wish.
- **The chooser** in the paper's thought experiments: one dose of painkiller, one life to save, a slap, an experiment. Controls: whom. Could be asked to anticipate: what the choice reveals about which scale he used.
- **The beings**: the adult, the baby, the infant born with massive brain damage, the adult with a severe intellectual disability, the person in advanced senility; the chimpanzee, the dog, the pig, the horse, the mouse; and the thing that cannot suffer. They control nothing; they carry the readings.
- **The doctor** asked by the parents to kill the infant painlessly; **the law**, which reflects the sanctity-of-life view. Controls: the verdict in one case.
- **The experimenter** choosing between an adult (with dread), an orphan and an animal. Controls: the subject.

This game: **dilemma** (the skill's own position for a normative paper; not used by the last three): the player is the "we" of the paper's first page, who wants to keep every person in and every animal out and must say with which ruler, and then the chooser of the paper's own thought experiments. **Discovery**: the player probes the cast with four rulers and finds a fact about it (no ruler has a gap where the species boundary is), then meets the paper's choices with the rulers in hand; not challenge, not strategic. **Device, one of our own: the chalk line and the sponge.** The player draws the line across the board as a chalk stroke; the board then circles in red every being the stroke put on the wrong side, and the sponge wipes the stroke before the next ruler. **Chassis: Drawn family, chalk on a blackboard**: board green, chalk white, red chalk, yellow chalk, a wooden frame; Fredericka the Great and Architects Daughter, neither used before; buttons are chalk-drawn boxes; dark, after two light games. No game in the table is chalk (an unshipped early build of Gödel was, and sits in `godel/previous/`); none has had the player draw a line through a cast.

## 7. The aim and the verdict

**The aim:** *Draw the line: who counts, and how much.* On the first board it reads *Draw one line that keeps every person in and every animal out, with a ruler you can read.* On the second, *Everyone who can hurt is in. Now give the one dose, and the one rescue.*

**The verdict leads with:**

- *What the paper says.* No ruler you can read draws the line at the species. The one that reads the same for all of them is pain, and it puts everyone in but the oak. A pain counts by how much it hurts, whoever hurts. A life counts by what is in it, whoever lives it. Species sets nothing.
- *What you found.* Reasoning left the baby and the infant under the chimpanzee. Plans left the elder under the dog. Ties left the infant under the pig. Pain read the same for all of them. Only Species drew your line, and Race drew the same line.
- *What you chose.* The dose went to the one in more pain, and to either when the pain was the same. The rescue went to the life with more in it, and once to the human over the chimpanzee.
- *Where you part.* In the fire, you saved the infant the paper's own rulers rank below the chimpanzee. That is the species line, the one no ruler could draw.
- *Had yours stood.* If a human's lesser pain outweighs an animal's greater pain, the paper says the shape of the thought is the racist's; if the paper's stands, it says the practical consequences are radical changes in diet, farming, experiments, hunting, furs, circuses, rodeos and zoos.

## 8. Three concepts

**A. The chalk line** (dilemma; discovery). Controls: a ruler (four, then a fifth) and a tap between two rows of the sorted cast; then four two-way choices. Deliberation: with the cast sorted by reasoning, where do I cut: above the chimpanzee and lose the baby, or below the baby and take the pig? Which ruler next, and which human will it cost me? Then: one dose, a man slapped and a pig beaten: whom? What they see after: the line as a chalk stroke, red circles on the misfits and a count in the aim's units; in the second half, what the paper would have done and why, in a line. Discovery moment: the pain ruler reads the same for all of them, so the only line it allows is under the lot; then the species ruler works, and the board rewrites its label *Race* and the line stays where it was. Fun: discovery, agency (the cut is theirs), anticipation (the dose and the rescue), a little mastery (by the third ruler they know what to look for). Second playthrough: the other order of rulers, the other answers in the fire, and the verdict's other branches.

**B. The painkiller cabinet** (agent; challenge). The player has five doses a round and a ward of seven beings in pain of different amounts and species; a dose takes one mark of pain from one being; the aim is to leave as little pain in the ward as possible. Deliberation: where the pain is against who feels it. After each round the board shows the pain left, and at the end reads the player's rule off their doses: how much a pig's mark weighed against a man's. Discovery: the player's own weight, and that it tracks species and not any reading. Fun: agency, mastery, discovery. Replay: beat your own weight. Risk: the yardstick is Singer's, and a player who rejects it is being scored by the paper rather than compared with it; and the second half of the paper (life is a different scale) has no place in a ward.

**C. Behind the screen** (experimenter; discovery) — the reckless one. The paper's own inference ("Do animals feel pain?") handed to the player: a being behind a chalk screen, only its signs visible as the paper lists them (writhing, drawing away, fear at the prospect of repetition, a nervous system like ours, a common origin), one at a time; the player says *feels pain* or *does not*; the screen lifts. Discovery: the signs are the same for a dog and for your friend, so a rule that shuts out the dog shuts out the friend. Fun: anticipation, discovery. Replay: none. Walkthrough: the first card is a real judgement; by the third the player has seen that every card shows the same signs, and the middle and last answers are the first answer again. Hands and no decision after one minute.

## 9. The first five minutes

**The pick.** 0:00 A blackboard. *Singer's Chalk Line* in chalk capitals, the aim under it, a stick of chalk and a sponge drawn in the tray. Three lines: Singer says no ruler draws the line between people and animals; here are the rulers; try. *Pick up the chalk.* 0:20 Eleven figures in a column, chalk silhouettes, named: an adult, a baby, an infant born with massive brain damage, an adult with a severe intellectual disability, an elder in advanced senility, a chimpanzee, a dog, a pig, a horse, a mouse, an oak. A tray of four rulers: Reasoning, Plans, Ties, Pain. The aim: one line, every person in, every animal out. They tap Reasoning. The column shuffles: adult, chimpanzee, dog, pig, horse, disabled adult, elder, baby, mouse, infant, oak, each with a yellow mark of its length. They frown: the chimpanzee is second. 0:50 They touch a gap under the adult; a faint stroke previews there; under it, the count would be four humans out. They try under the baby: five animals in. There is no gap. They chalk under the adult, because at least nobody who is not a person gets in. The stroke draws itself; four red circles: baby, disabled adult, elder, infant. *Four people out. The sponge.* 1:20 Plans: the chimpanzee is still second, the dog fourth. They chalk under the horse this time, thinking "take the animals, keep the people": the elder, the baby and the infant are still under the mouse. 1:50 Ties: they grin, the baby is second now; but the dog is above the disabled adult, and the infant, whose parents asked the doctor to end it, is at the bottom with the oak. 2:20 Pain: every mark the same length but the oak's. The only gap is above the oak. They draw it there; no circles; eleven in. The board says: this ruler cannot tell them apart; a line on it puts them all in or all out. 2:45 *One ruler left.* Species. The cast splits, five and six, one gap. They chalk it. No circles. *Every person in, every animal out. It works.* Then the sponge wipes the word *Species* and writes *Race*, and the line stays; then *Sex*; Singer's sentence appears: the pattern is identical. They sit with that. 3:15 Second board: everyone who can hurt is in. So is a mouse a man? One dose: the adult, slapped, two marks of pain; the pig, beaten with a stick, seven. They weigh it, give it to the pig, half-expecting to be told off, and the board says the paper agrees, a bigger pain counts more whoever has it. 3:45 One dose: two adults, same injury, six and six, one with the plans ruler full, one short. They give it to the full one; the board says the paper would not choose here: pain is the same scale for both. 4:15 A fire: the adult or the mouse. The adult, easy; the paper agrees, and says why: by what is in the life, not by species. 4:40 A fire: the infant born with massive brain damage or the chimpanzee. They stop. They pull the plans ruler: infant nothing, chimpanzee seven. They save the infant anyway, or they don't. 5:00 The verdict.

**The runner-up (B).** 0:00 A cabinet of five doses, a ward of seven in a row, pain marks over each. They dose the man with four marks, the woman with three, then the pig with six. 0:40 Pain left: twelve. The board says the pain left would have been nine. They see it: the pig. 1:10 Round two, they dose by marks, grudgingly. 2:00 Round three has a baby with one mark and a horse with eight; they dose the baby first and know it. 3:00 The board reads their rule: a pig's mark weighed a third of a man's. They are being told, in numbers, that they are a speciesist, by a game that assumed Singer's score; and there is nowhere to go after it. Dead for the author by 3:10.

## 10. The pick, and why

A. It is the paper's own argument played with the paper's own cast: the sophisticated opponent's move (a line on a fact) is the player's move, and the marginal cases (the infant, the elder, the disabled adult) are the beings the rulers keep leaving out. Deliberation is real on the first action (where to cut on Reasoning: which failure to accept), the middle one (which ruler to try and which human it costs) and the last (the infant or the chimpanzee, with the rulers in view). The two halves carry the two counterintuitive results, in the order they land. The author test: the author knows that no line can be drawn, and still has to choose where to fail four times, and still has the fire. The impossibility is shown as the result and named at the moment it happens, as Cantor was. Originality: no game in the table has had the player draw a line through a cast and watch the label change under it. Runner-up: B, and what would make me switch: a reader who finds the first board a puzzle they solve in their head before chalking, in which case the fix is to hide the marks until a ruler is picked and the sort shown, which is how it is built. The reckless concept's loss: the screen lifting on a friend; it is a one-minute opener and the game does not need one.

## 11. The shape

Two boards and a verdict, about five minutes, no score.

**Board one, who counts:** five rounds, one per ruler, the player's order among the first four (Reasoning, Plans, Ties, Pain: the paper's own criteria, from the killing section and the equality section), then Species offered as the fifth. Each round: pick a ruler, the cast sorts, tap a gap, chalk, see the circles and the count, sponge. Four distinct things to find: Reasoning sacrifices the baby and the infant to the chimpanzee; Plans sacrifices the elder to the dog; Ties sacrifices the infant whose parents want it dead to the pig; Pain cannot tell them apart. The fifth is the species ruler and its mirror (Race, Sex), one tap each. A round cannot be skipped, because each costs a different person.

**Board two, how much:** four rounds, the paper's own thought experiments in the order the discovery needs: (1) one dose, the adult slapped against the pig beaten (pain: the amount, section "Practical consequences"); (2) one dose, the adult against the adult with a severe intellectual disability, same injury (pain is unaffected by capacities, section "Killing"); (3) one rescue, the adult against the mouse (life by plans, "Killing"); (4) one rescue, the infant born with massive brain damage against the chimpanzee (the special case, "Killing"). Each a two-way choice, binary because the paper's own case is one dose and one life. After each: what the paper would do, in a line, and why.

**The verdict:** what the paper says; what you found on board one; what you chose on board two; where you part; what would have followed.

## 12. Controls, mechanism, feedback, moment

**Controls.** Board one: a tray of ruler buttons (four, then one), and the gaps between rows of the sorted cast, each a tap target that previews a faint stroke and the count it would give; *Chalk it* commits. Board two: two large figures, each a tap target; *Give the dose* / *Pull out* commits the one touched. A small ? on every board.

**Mechanism.** `mechanism.js`: `CAST`, eleven beings with a name, a species flag and four readings; `RULERS`; `sortBy(ruler)` returns the cast ordered by reading, ties kept adjacent and marked; `cut(ruler, k)` returns the humans below the cut and the animals above it, the count in the aim's units, and whether the cut falls inside a tie (not allowed: the ruler cannot tell them apart); `gaps(ruler)` returns every legal cut and its misfit count, so the calibration can prove the paper's claim on this cast: on every reading ruler, every legal cut has at least one misfit, and on Pain the only legal cut is above the oak; `SCENES`, four, each with two beings, the scale (pain or life), the readings in play and the paper's answer (one being, or either); `judge(scene, pick)` returns agree, part, or either; `verdict(lines, picks)` returns the lines of section 7 as text branches.

**Feedback.** The stroke drawing itself along the gap; red chalk circles on the misfits, drawn one at a time; a count line in the aim's units ("3 people out, 0 animals in"); the sponge smear that clears it; on board two, the paper's answer written under the figures after the player's, with its one-line reason.

**The moment.** Twice: the Pain ruler's eleven marks of one length, and the word *Species* sponged into *Race* with the line still there.

## 13. The look

**Register: chalk on a blackboard** (Drawn family). Where it came from: the paper has no picture of its own, and reached me as a classroom reading with study questions at its foot; and the paper's own figure of speech, where a line is drawn and who falls on which side, is a thing done with chalk. The board is dark, after the Register's cream and the Even Hand's chalk paper. **Palette:** board green `#2F4A3E`; chalk `#F3EEDD`; red chalk `#E8664D` (the line and the misfit circles); yellow chalk `#F0D27A` (the marks and the chosen ruler); frame oak `#8C5E36`. **Type:** Fredericka the Great for the title, the ruler labels and the big words the board writes; Architects Daughter for everything else; both embedded. **Buttons:** chalk-drawn boxes, a slightly uneven 2 px chalk outline on the board, chalk capitals; the commit button filled chalk with green text. **The one visual rule: everything on the board is chalk, and nothing vanishes, it is wiped.** A stroke is drawn, not shown; a failed line is sponged, leaving a smear; the ruler's label is rubbed out and rewritten. **The play screen at 380 px:** the aim in one line with the ? box; the ruler tray (four boxes in a row); the cast, eleven rows of 34 px, each a chalk silhouette, the name in chalk and a yellow mark whose length is the reading; a count line; *Chalk it*. ## 14. What is cut

The experiment argument (adults' anticipatory dread; orphans; the question it forces); the readability of the parody ("The Rights of Animals" against women's rights) beyond the start screen's sentence; the factual-equality passages on race and sex averages; the inference that animals feel pain (section C, noted as a one-minute opener and not built); the list of other practices (whales, dolphins, kangaroos); the religious note; the study questions. All of these are read on the About screen or not at all.

## 15. Divergences

- **The cast** is eleven beings assembled from the paper's examples; the paper never lines them up. The oak is ours, standing for "a being not capable of suffering", which the paper names but does not instance.
- **The readings** are ordinal stylisations: the paper says a chimpanzee, dog or pig surpasses the brain-damaged infant and the senile person in self-awareness, that some intellectually disabled people can never reach the intelligence of a dog, that a baby cannot vote; the lengths are ours, the orderings are the paper's, and every ordering the calibration checks is one the paper states (section 17, after `build.md`).
- **Ties** uses the paper's own example for the infant (the parents who ask the doctor to end it) and gives the dog the ties the paper gives "chimpanzee, dog, or pig": "capacity for meaningful relations with others". Ties for the mouse ("mice do not have to the same degree") is low by the paper.
- **The Pain ruler** reads one length for every sentient being. The paper grades pain by amount, not capacity; capacity is the boundary, and on the boundary ruler everything past it reads the same. The horse's thick skin is an amount, and lives on board two.
- **The mirror** (Race, Sex) is one board that re-labels the same line; the paper's sentence is "The pattern is identical", and the game draws the pattern, not a history.
- **Board two's answers** are the paper's where the paper gives one (the pig over the slapped man is by extension from the horse and the stick; the adult over the mouse; the chimpanzee over the infant is by extension from "there may be special cases"), and "either" where the paper says "it is not so clear". The About screen says which is which.
- **No clock, no score**; the count after a chalk line is the aim's units, not a score.

## 16. Mechanism kind and claim boundaries

**Toy.** The paper hands over an argument and nothing else; the mechanism is a model built to illustrate it. **The game demonstrates** that on a cast of the paper's own examples, ranked as the paper ranks them, no line on any measured ruler keeps every person in and every animal out, that the only line that does is the species itself, and that in the paper's own cases pain and life are counted on two different scales. **It assumes** the cast of eleven, the four rulers, the lengths of the marks, the oak, the pig standing in for the horse in the first scene, the pain amounts in the two dose scenes, the one ordering the paper does not state (the adult with a severe intellectual disability above the chimpanzee on Plans; our choice, so that Plans costs a different person from Reasoning), and the wording of the paper's answers. **The paper establishes** the argument as an argument: a reader who rejects a premise (equality as a prescription; suffering as the boundary) rejects the line too, and the game shows where the line goes under the premises, not that the premises hold. All of this is on the About screen. The discovery on board one follows from the paper's stated orderings and not from any design choice: section 17 checks it against random lengths. The discovery on board two (two scales) is the paper's own statement of its cases; the game adds nothing but the pig.

## 17. Calibration plan and result

`calibration.js` checks, against the mechanism in `mechanism.js`: (1) every ordering the paper states (`CONFIG.PAPER_ORDERINGS`, six of them, each tagged with its section) holds in the cast, and Pain reads one length for every being that can feel and nothing for the oak; (2) on every reading ruler every legal line (a line cannot part two with the same mark) has at least one misfit, the fewest being 4 on Reasoning, 3 on Plans, 3 on Ties and 5 on Pain, whose only legal lines are above the lot, under everyone who can feel, and under the lot; Species has exactly one line with none; and the three reading rulers cost three different sets of people at their best line; (3) robustness: 500 random casts with readings drawn 0–10 that keep only the paper's stated orderings all fail on every reading ruler and all work on Species, so the discovery rests on the orderings, not the lengths; (4) the four scenes carry the paper's answers, go by the right scale, and every being in every scene is picked by some way of playing. Result: all checks passed (`calibration-result.txt`). The build script runs the check and refuses to build on a failure or on a mechanism that differs from the file checked.
