# Singer's Chalk Line — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-one-reasoning-sorted.png` (the cast sorted, the chimpanzee second), `screenshot-one-reasoning-chalked.png` (the line and the four red circles), `screenshot-one-pain-chalked.png` (eleven marks of one length: the moment), `screenshot-one-race.png` (the word rewritten, the line where it was), `screenshot-one-reply.png`, `screenshot-two-scene4-result.png` (the infant and the chimpanzee) and `screenshot-end.png`. Then play it on a phone as someone who wants to keep eating meat: the things to judge are whether choosing where to fail on each ruler feels like a decision or a chore by the third ruler, and whether the mirror (Race, Sex) lands as the paper's point or as a lecture.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the eleven beings with their four readings (ordinal, 0–10; each tagged with the passage it comes from), the four rulers and their one-line descriptions, the two words of the mirror, the four scenes with their pain amounts and the paper's answers and reasons, and the six orderings the paper states, which the calibration checks. Change a reading and run `node calibration.js`: it re-proves that every line on every reading ruler has a misfit, that the three reading rulers cost different people, and that the claim survives 500 random casts that keep only the stated orderings. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the one ordering the paper does not give, the adult with a severe intellectual disability above the chimpanzee on Plans, chosen so that Plans and Reasoning cost different people; the About screen declares it, and setting it to 6 puts the ruler back to the paper's silence at the cost of a repeated round; (b) whether the mirror should be two taps or one; (c) the pig standing in for the horse in the first scene, so that the dose scenes and the fire scenes use beings the first board has shown; (d) the names: the paper's own words for the infant, the disabled adult and the senile elder are on screen, and the 2004 excerpt's wording is older than the paper's later editions.
4. The game has had the builder's review only: screenshots at phone width and two scripted sessions in a headless browser. No stranger has played it.
