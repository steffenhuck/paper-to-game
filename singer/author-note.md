# Draw a Line with Singer — note for the author

Revision 11 of 28 November 2026 (memo, sections 27 to 30): three experiments with three fixed menus of three, the nine creatures that can suffer once each (the third menu is the paper's own test: the baby, the dog, the grown-up, to save a thousand lives; the stone is on no menu and appears once at the Suffer question, as the boundary); the board asks whether any one rule can make sense of the three choices (each experiment on its own: a rule is fine with a column if every tick sits below every cross); the count is stated, then Reason, Talk and Aware come one at a time and the player taps the experiments he expects the rule to complain about, then Check, before the reveal (a red cutoff mark in each column the rule is fine with, red circles round the worst pair in each column it complains about, and a tally of what the player found); then "can they suffer?" for the nine; then the name, the sponge with Race and Sex, Singer's answer, and the verdict. A player who would use nobody who can suffer is sent to Foot.

1. Look first at `screenshot-phone-species-exp1.png`, `-did.png` (the three choices and the count), `-guess.png` (the question before the reveal, with Experiment 2 tapped), `-reason.png` (Reason objects in experiments 2 and 3, with its cutoff mark in column 1; and `screenshot-phone-singer-reason.png` for a rule that agrees, with its three cutoffs), `-ask.png` and `-suffer.png`, `-name.png`, `-race.png`, `-end.png`; the laptop versions have the same names with `laptop`. Then play it four ways: animals used, people spared (Species); nobody for a child's year, everyone from one life on (Singer's line); nobody ever (Foot); the mouse, then the hurt grown-up, then the baby (a Reason line, and Talk's and Aware's). The things to judge: whether the nine-row board reads on a phone; whether tapping the experiments you expect the rule to complain about makes the rule screens feel like play; whether the intermediate "let's make sense of what you did" lands.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`: the nine (and the stone, which stays in the cast for the Suffer screen), the three menus, the three rungs with their stories, the cost, the rules, the mirror's two words, and the seven orderings the paper states. The test is `explainsColumn()` and `explains()`; the words for each shape are in `shapeWords()` and the verdict in `verdict()`. Change anything and run `node calibration.js` (about ten seconds: 512 x 512); then `python3 build.py`; `python3 session-test.py` plays six sessions at two widths and refreshes the screenshots; `node loop.js` narrates one play headlessly.
3. What we were least sure about: the nine-row board on a phone; the tie objections; the verdict's length; whether the per-experiment sentences in the rule's words are too long when all three columns complain.
4. The game has had the builder's review only. No stranger has played this build.
