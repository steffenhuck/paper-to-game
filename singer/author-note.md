# Draw a Line with Singer — note for the author

Revision 9 of 27 November 2026 (memo, sections 27 and 28): three experiments with three fixed menus, all ten creatures once (the third menu is the paper's own test: the baby, the dog, the grown-up, to save a thousand lives); the board asks whether any one rule can make sense of the three choices (rising cutoffs per experiment; the stone free); the count is stated, then Reason, Talk and Aware come one at a time and the player guesses, agrees or objects, before the reveal; then "can they suffer?" for the nine; then the name, the sponge with Race and Sex, Singer's answer, and the verdict. A player who would use nobody who can suffer is sent to Foot.

1. Look first at `screenshot-phone-species-exp1.png`, `-did.png` (the three choices and the count), `-reason.png` (Reason objects; and `screenshot-phone-singer-reason.png` for a rule that agrees, with its three cutoffs), `-ask.png` and `-suffer.png`, `-name.png`, `-race.png`, `-end.png`; the laptop versions have the same names with `laptop`. Then play it four ways: animals and the stone used, people spared (Species); the stone alone for a child's year, everyone from one life on (Singer's line); nobody ever (Foot); the mouse and the stone, then the hurt grown-up, then the baby (a Reason line, and Talk's and Aware's). The things to judge: whether the ten-row board reads on a phone; whether the guess makes the rule screens feel like play; whether the intermediate "let's make sense of what you did" lands.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`: the ten, the three menus, the three rungs with their stories, the cost, the rules, the mirror's two words, and the seven orderings the paper states. The test is `explains()`; the words for each shape are in `shapeWords()` and the verdict in `verdict()`. Change anything and run `node calibration.js` (about twenty seconds: 1024 x 512); then `python3 build.py`; `python3 session-test.py` plays six sessions at two widths and refreshes the screenshots; `node loop.js` narrates one play headlessly.
3. What we were least sure about: the ten-row board on a phone; the tie objections; the verdict's length.
4. The game has had the builder's review only. No stranger has played this build.
