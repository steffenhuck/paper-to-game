| **Draw a Line with Singer** | Singer (1974), "All Animals Are Equal", *Philosophic Exchange* 5(1):103–116. You are the scientist in the paper's thought experiment. Three experiments of rising stakes come to your desk (a child's year of illness, one life, a thousand lives; the stories change each play, the cost never does: a day of bad pain, no lasting harm), and four subjects are on offer every time, the paper's own: a baby with no parents, a grown-up whose brain is badly hurt, a chimpanzee, a mouse. Tick everyone you would use. The board reads your line off what you did, then three rules with marks come to it one at a time, Reason, Talk and Aware, each agreeing with your order of use or objecting and circling the two it objects to; then Bentham's question with no marks, can they suffer?, and the Suffer rule's complaint built from your own two answers. The board names your line (Singer's, a Reason line, Species, your own order, or nobody at any price, which is Foot's question and links to her game), the sponge writes *Race* and *Sex* over the line most people draw, the paper's own exercise, and the verdict is about what you did.
