| **Singer's Chalk Line** | Singer (1974), "All Animals Are Equal", *Philosophic Exchange* 5(1):103–116. Draw one line that keeps every person in and every animal out, with a ruler you can read. Chalk on a blackboard: ten of Singer's own beings (an adult, an orphaned infant, an adult with irreversible brain damage, an adult with a severe intellectual disability, a chimpanzee, a dog, a pig, a horse, a mouse, a stone) and Bentham's rulers as the paper quotes them, Reason, Talk, Suffer, plus the paper's own, Aware; each sort has no gap at the species, and every line you chalk gets red circles round whoever it cost. Then the one ruler that works, and the sponge writes *Race* over its label with the line still there, the paper's own exercise. Second board: you are the experimenter in the paper's one thought experiment, one subject for an experiment that would save many lives, and the paper asks whether you would have used the orphaned infant instead, and why not. The footnote on killing is read, not played.
