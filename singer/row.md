| **Draw a Line with Singer** | Singer (1974), "All Animals Are Equal", *Philosophic Exchange* 5(1):103–116. Who counts? Tap them. Ten of Singer's own examples on a blackboard (a grown-up, a baby with no parents, a grown-up whose brain is badly hurt, a grown-up who cannot learn much, a chimpanzee, a dog, a pig, a horse, a mouse, a stone); you draw your line first, with no ruler in sight. Then Bentham's rulers as the paper quotes them, Reason, Talk, Suffer, plus the paper's own, Aware, each say whether they could have drawn your line, and if not, which two you put the wrong way round. The board names your line by its shape: Species, Singer's, a Reason line with its price, Species with favourites, your own; and the sponge writes *Race* and *Sex* over the line most people draw, the paper's own exercise. Second board: you are the scientist in the paper's one thought experiment, and Singer asks why not the baby. Built for a smart eleven-year-old.
