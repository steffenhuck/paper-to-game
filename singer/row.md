| **Draw a Line with Singer** | Singer (1974), "All Animals Are Equal", *Philosophic Exchange* 5(1):103–116. You are the scientist in the paper's thought experiment. Three experiments of rising stakes come to your desk (a child's year of illness, one life, a thousand lives; the stories change each play, the cost never does: a day of bad pain, no lasting harm), each with its own menu of three, the nine of the paper's examples that can suffer across the three, from a grown-up to a mouse; the last menu is the paper's own test, the baby beside Bentham's dog. Tick everyone you would use. Then the board tries to make sense of your three choices: can any one rule have made them? A rule is fine with an experiment if every tick sits below every cross on its marks. It says how many of its rules can explain all three, and Reason, Talk and Aware come to the board one at a time; before each speaks you tap the experiments you expect it to complain about, then it marks its cutoffs and circles the pairs it objects to. Then Bentham's question with no marks, can they suffer?, with the stone appearing once as the boundary, and the Suffer rule's complaint built from your own answers. The board names your line (Singer's, a Reason line, Species, your own order, or nobody at any price, which is Foot's question and links to her game), the sponge writes *Race* and *Sex* over the line most people draw, the paper's own exercise, and the verdict is about what you did.
