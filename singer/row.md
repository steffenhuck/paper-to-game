| **Singer's Chalk Line** | Singer (1975/1990), *Animal Liberation*, chapter 1, as excerpted in Gensler, Spurgin and Swindal (eds.), *Ethics: Contemporary Readings* (Routledge, 2004), pp. 284–292. All animals are equal: draw one line that keeps every person in and every animal out, with a ruler you can read. Chalk on a blackboard: eleven of Singer's own beings (an adult, a baby, an infant born with massive brain damage, an adult with a severe intellectual disability, an elder in advanced senility, a chimpanzee, a dog, a pig, a horse, a mouse, an oak) and four rulers the paper names; each sort has no gap at the species, and every line you chalk gets red circles round whoever it cost. Then the one ruler that works, and the sponge writes *Race* over its label with the line still there. Second board: one dose of painkiller, twice, and one rescue from a fire, twice, the paper's own cases, to find that pain and life are two scales. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/singer/singers-chalk-line.html) · [memo](singer/memo.md) |
