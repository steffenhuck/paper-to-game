| **Draw a Line with Singer** | Singer (1974), "All Animals Are Equal", *Philosophic Exchange* 5(1):103–116. Most people think every person counts and no animal does; Singer asks, measured how? Chalk on a blackboard: ten of Singer's own examples (a grown-up, a baby with no parents, a grown-up whose brain is badly hurt, a grown-up who cannot learn much, a chimpanzee, a dog, a pig, a horse, a mouse, a stone) and Bentham's rulers as the paper quotes them, Reason, Talk, Suffer, plus the paper's own, Aware. Draw a line anywhere; the board circles whoever landed on the wrong side and says whether the ruler drew the line at all or your hand did. Then one line with no ruler, which the board names, and the sponge writes *Race* and *Sex* over the name, the paper's own exercise. Second board: you are the scientist in the paper's one thought experiment, and Singer asks why not the baby. Built for a smart eleven-year-old.
