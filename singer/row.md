| **Draw a Line with Singer** | Singer (1974), "All Animals Are Equal", *Philosophic Exchange* 5(1):103–116. Whose pain counts as much as yours? Tap them. Ten of Singer's own examples on a blackboard (a grown-up, a baby with no parents, a grown-up whose brain is badly hurt, a grown-up who cannot learn much, a chimpanzee, a dog, a pig, a horse, a mouse, a stone); you draw your line first, with no ruler in sight. Then Bentham's rulers as the paper quotes them, Reason, Talk, Suffer, plus the paper's own, Aware, come to the board one at a time, each showing at once whether it could draw your line; move anyone in or out and watch the verdict redraw, or keep your line. The board names your last line by its shape and says who moved you: Species, Singer's, a Reason line with its price, Species with favourites, your own; and the sponge writes *Race* and *Sex* over the line most people draw, the paper's own exercise. Second board: you are the scientist in the paper's one thought experiment, and Singer asks why not the baby. Built for a smart eleven-year-old.
