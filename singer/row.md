| **Draw a Line with Singer** | Singer (1974), "All Animals Are Equal", *Philosophic Exchange* 5(1):103–116. Whose pain counts as much as yours? Tap them. Ten of Singer's own examples on a blackboard (a grown-up, a baby with no parents, a grown-up whose brain is badly hurt, a grown-up who cannot learn much, a chimpanzee, a dog, a pig, a horse, a mouse, a stone); you draw your line first, with no ruler in sight. Then three rules with marks come to the board one at a time, Reason, Talk and Aware, each said out loud when it arrives, each agreeing with your line or objecting and circling the two it objects to; move anyone and it checks again, or keep your line. Then one more question with no marks, Bentham's: can they suffer? Asked only about the beings you count for less, and the Suffer rule's complaints are built from your own two answers. The board names your last line by its shape and says who moved you: Species, Singer's, a Reason line with its price, Species with favourites, your own; and the sponge writes *Race* and *Sex* over the line most people draw, the paper's own exercise. Second board: you are the scientist in the paper's one thought experiment, and Singer asks why not the baby. Built for a smart eleven-year-old.
