| **Draw a Line with Singer** | Singer (1974), "All Animals Are Equal", *Philosophic Exchange* 5(1):103–116. You are the scientist in the paper's thought experiment. Three experiments of rising stakes come to your desk (a child's year of illness, one life, a thousand lives; the stories change each play, the cost never does: a day of bad pain, no lasting harm), each with its own menu of three, the nine of the paper's examples that can suffer across the three, from a grown-up to a mouse; the last menu is the paper's own test, the baby beside Bentham's dog. Tick everyone you would use. Then the board tries to make sense of your three choices: can any one rule have made them? Four rules come to the board one at a time, Reason, Talk, Aware and Suffer; before each speaks you tap the experiments you expect it to complain about, then it marks its cutoffs and circles the pairs it objects to. Suffer's marks are all equal, because every one of the nine can suffer, so it complains wherever you used some and spared others. The board names your line (Singer's, a Reason line, Species, your own order, or nobody at any price, which is Foot's question and links to her game), and the verdict draws it: who you would use and who you would spare, with the name on the chalk line between, beside Singer's line with the nine on one side and the stone on the other.
