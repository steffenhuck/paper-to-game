Draw a Line with Singer

You are a scientist. Three experiments are on your desk, and each one would help people, some a little, some a lot. Each experiment needs a subject: someone it is done to, and it hurts. Each experiment comes with its own menu of three who are on offer, nine creatures in all, from a grown-up to a mouse. You decide who may be used, and for what. Then four rules come to the board, one at a time, and each asks whether it could have made your choices. Then the board draws your line, and Singer's.

Whose pain would you spend? Draw the line.

How to play

Whose pain would you spend? Draw the line.

The experiments

You are a scientist, and three experiments come to your desk, one after the other. The first would spare one child a year of illness. The second would save one life. The third would save a thousand lives. Each experiment needs a subject: someone it is done to. Whoever you use will be in bad pain for a day, and then be fine again, with no lasting harm. Each experiment has its own menu of three who are on offer; across the three menus you meet nine: a grown-up; a baby a few days old, with no parents; a grown-up whose brain is badly hurt, for good; a grown-up who cannot learn much; a chimpanzee; a dog; a pig; a horse; and a mouse. Every one of them is an example from Singer's paper.

On each experiment, tap everyone you would be willing to use for it. You may tap one, several, or nobody at all. Tap again to change your mind. Then tap Done. Nothing you tick is done to anyone; the game only wants to know where your line is.

The four rules

Then the board tries to make sense of what you did. A rule is a way of deciding whom to use first. Reason's rule is: the less a being can think, the sooner you may use it. Four rules come to the board, one at a time: Reason, Talk, Aware and Suffer. Each one sorts the nine by its marks (the marks follow what Singer's paper says) and shows what you did on each menu: a ✓ for everyone you would use, a ✗ for everyone you would spare. A rule could have made your choice in an experiment if the ones you would use there are the ones it would use first. Before each rule speaks, you guess: tap the experiments you think it will complain about, then tap Check; or tap Check straight away if you think it has no complaint. Then the board tells you, experiment by experiment, and shows where the rule's complaints are with red circles. Suffer is the fourth rule, and its marks are all the same, because every one of the nine can suffer; you will see what that means for its complaints.

The name, and the verdict

The board tells you what your line is called, and what Singer says about it. Then the verdict: a drawing of your line, who you would use and who you would spare; a drawing of Singer's line; where you and he part; and what follows. No score. Play again to draw a different line; the stories change each time.
