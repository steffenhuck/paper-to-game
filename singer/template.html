Draw a Line with Singer

You are a scientist. Three experiments are on your desk, and each one would help people, some a little, some a lot. Each experiment needs one subject: someone it is done to, and it hurts. Four are on offer: a baby, a grown-up whose brain is badly hurt, a chimpanzee and a mouse. You decide who may be used, and when. Then Singer looks at what you did, and asks what rule could be behind it. Then he asks one more question of his own.

Whose pain would you spend? Draw the line.

How to play

Whose pain would you spend? Draw the line.

The experiments

You are a scientist, and three experiments come to your desk, one after the other. The first would spare one child a year of illness. The second would save one life. The third would save a thousand lives. Each experiment needs one subject: someone it is done to. Whoever you use will be in bad pain for a day, and then be fine again, with no lasting harm. Four are on offer every time: a baby a few days old, with no parents; a grown-up whose brain is badly hurt, for good; a chimpanzee; and a mouse. Every one of them is an example from Singer's paper.

On each experiment, tap everyone you would be willing to use. You may tap one, several, or nobody at all. Tap again to change your mind. Then tap Done. Whoever you were willing to use for a smaller good is already ticked when a bigger good comes; you can untick them. Nothing you tick is done to anyone; the game only wants to know where your line is.

Your line

From your three answers the board reads, for each of the four, the smallest good for which you would use them, or that you would never use them. That is your line: whom you would spend first, whom you would spare. Nothing can change it now, except playing again.

The rules

Then three rules come to the board, one at a time: Reason, Talk and Aware. A rule is a way of deciding whom to use first. Reason's rule is: the less a being can think, the sooner you may use it. Each rule sorts the four by its marks; the marks follow what Singer's paper says. Beside each one is what you decided. If the rule agrees with your order, it says so. If it objects, red circles go round the two it objects to: one you would use sooner although it has the higher mark, one you would spare although it has the lower mark, which by that rule is the wrong way round. The circles are the rule's complaint, not the board's. Tap to bring the next rule.

Then one more question, with no marks: can they suffer? Tap yes or no for each of the four. The Suffer rule is this: if a being can suffer, its pain counts as much as anyone's, so among beings that can suffer none may be used sooner than another. If you say two beings can suffer and you would use one of them sooner, Suffer objects, and the circles are drawn from your own two answers.

The name, and the verdict

The board tells you what your line is called, and what Singer says about it. Then the verdict: what you did, what each rule said, your line and its name, where you and Singer part, and what follows. No score. Play again to draw a different line.
