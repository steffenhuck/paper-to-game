How to play

Who counts? Draw the line.

The first board

Ten are on the board, every one an example from Singer's paper: a grown-up; a baby a few days old, with no parents; a grown-up whose brain is badly hurt, for good; a grown-up who cannot learn much; a chimpanzee, a dog, a pig, a horse, a mouse; and a stone. Most people think every person counts and no animal does. Your job is to find the ruler that draws that line.

Tap a ruler in the tray: Reason, Talk, Aware or Suffer. Each one sorts the ten by one thing, highest at the top, with a yellow mark for each. Tap any row and a red line appears under it: everyone above is in (counts), everyone below is out. Tap Chalk it and the line is drawn for good. The board circles in red every person left out and every animal let in. And if your line runs between two equal marks, the board says so: the ruler did not draw that line, your hand did. Sponge wipes the line; the smear stays. Each ruler is used once.

After the four rulers you draw one line with no ruler at all, wherever you like. The board tells you what that line is called.

The second board

You are the scientist in Singer's own story: one experiment that would save many lives, and it needs one subject, someone it is done to. Four are on offer, with their marks from the first board, and there is a fifth answer: no one. Tap one, then Use this one. If you took an animal, Singer asks why not the baby, and maybe one more thing. Then he says what your answers add up to.

The verdict

What Singer says; what you found on the board, ruler by ruler; what you did as the scientist; where you and Singer part; and what follows. No score.
