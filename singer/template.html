How to play

Draw the line: who counts.

The first board

Ten beings are on the board, every one an example from the paper: an adult; an infant a few days old, an orphan; an adult with severe, irreversible brain damage; an adult with a severe intellectual disability; a chimpanzee, a dog, a pig, a horse, a mouse; and a stone. The aim here: draw one line that keeps every person in and every animal out, with a ruler you can read.

Tap a ruler in the tray: Reason, Talk, Aware or Suffer. The first three are Bentham's questions as the paper quotes them, the fourth is the paper's own. Each sorts the ten by one thing, highest at the top, with a yellow mark for each; the marks follow the paper's rankings. Tap a row and a faint line appears under it: everyone above it is in (counts), everyone below is out. Two with the same mark cannot be parted; the line goes under both. Tap Chalk it and the line is drawn for good. The board then circles in red every person left out and every animal let in, and says what this ruler cannot do. Sponge wipes the line; the smear stays. Each ruler is used once; after the four there is one ruler left.

The second board

You are the experimenter in the paper's own case: one experiment that would save many lives, one subject needed. Four are on offer, with their marks from the first board, and there is a fifth answer: no one. Tap one, then Use this one. If you took an animal, the paper asks its question: would you have used the infant instead? Answer, and it may ask once or twice more. Then it says what your answers add up to.

The footnote

What the paper says about killing, which is one footnote. Read it; nothing to do.

The verdict

What the paper says; what you found on the first board, ruler by ruler; what you did as the experimenter; where you part from the paper; and what follows. No score.
