# Live from Brussels — note for the author

1. **What to look at first.** Play the Brussels item (`screenshot-play-comm.png`), then read the result card. The line the game rests on is "It made you drum about a third more taps a second than the tabloid". That figure follows only from your formula and the scripts' scores; it does not depend on the player's skill. The lit windows depend on the thumbs as well as the text: at two-thumb speed, the UK and Brussels scripts come out about level (`calibration-result.txt`, section 4).
2. **Where the numbers are.** Every number is in the `CONFIG` block at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism. Fig. 2 values were read off at 600 dpi by pixel position. Each design choice (beat, rush, tolerance) is tagged "our choice". The scripts are just below, in `SCRIPTS`. Syllables are marked by hand with "·", and those marks are what the player taps. After editing, run `node calibration.js` (it checks each script's score against your averages, and the ordering under alternative splits and settings), then `python3 build.py`. The build refuses to finish if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. **What we were least sure about.**
   - (a) The scripts are invented. Each matches its sender's mean reading ease, but a single 40-word text is one point in a wide cloud. Real releases from the replication corpus (Dataverse was unreachable from the build environment) would make this much stronger.
   - (b) Turning reading ease into tapping is our toy. The calibration shows the game weighs sentence length against word length at about the formula's rate (0.010–0.014 syllables per word for one word of sentence length, against 0.012), but no reader was ever measured.
   - (c) The archive pick shows the flat line through three years only.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px, with taps from a model player. It has not been played by real strangers, and the 4–6 minute length is an estimate. The previous game from this paper, *Say It Plainly*, is kept in `previous/`.
