# Meitner's Drop — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-u1-set.png` (the pinch set at 56 | 36 with the landing cells outlined), `screenshot-a-u2-result.png` (the five tubes come down out of the dashed row), `screenshot-a-u3-result.png` (the hold: the 24-minute body, and the quiet 93) and `screenshot-a-end.png`. Then play it: the things to judge are whether the column rule reads as "the chemists could not tell them apart" rather than as a game convention, whether the 1939 table reads at phone width, and whether the exactly equal first pinch, which lights little, feels fair.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, each line tagged with its page in the paper or marked as ours: the drops, the radius constant fitted to 200 Mev, the valley-of-stability coefficients, the skin, the smallest piece, the neutron budgets, the two shelves of tubes with their sources, and the paper's three chains for the check. Change anything and run `node calibration.js`; then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure. `node session-test.js` plays three sessions in headless Chromium and refreshes the screenshots; `node loop.js` prints a session and the strategies table.
3. What we were least sure about: the column rule as the whole of "acts like" (it lets the rare earths light lanthanum's tube and lets gadolinium pass for lanthanum); the collapsed rare-earth cell; eka-iridium, eka-platinum and eka-gold on the shelf, which come from the 1937 scheme the paper cites rather than from the letter itself; the deterministic neutron share where the paper says chance; and whether five neutrons is too generous once the column trick is seen.
4. The game has had the builder's review only: screenshots at phone and laptop width and three scripted sessions. No stranger has played this build.
