# Meitner and the Phantoms — note for the author

Revision 5 of 27 November 2026 added hand-holding (memo, section 23): the game now says what you will learn if the drop splits and if the cut fails, before you fire and after. Revision 4 of 26 November 2026, on your note that rev. 3 was too hard to understand and the slider too short (memo, section 22). The cut now goes anywhere from 1 to 91, the chance is a hill under the drop, the game shows what a cut will do before you fire, the Hold button is gone (a failed cut lights the 24-minute tube), and the words are plain. The numbers and the odds are rev. 2's.

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-bench-56.png` (the hill, 25%, the shaded columns and the line under the table), `screenshot-a-bench-edge.png` (the far end of the range), `screenshot-a-u1-result.png` (the five tubes come down) and `screenshot-b-end.png` (the verdict). Then play it, with `?seed=2` for the run where barium's cell refuses twice: the things to judge are whether you can now say, after one minute, what the game asks you to do; whether showing what a cut will light takes the fun out or makes the chance the whole game; whether the trail preview makes the odds-versus-reach choice clear to a kid without giving the answer, whether the first-neutron guide is enough now that the shelf screen is gone, whether the odds read as the paper's "roughly equal" rather than as a dice roll, whether four neutrons is the right pressure, and whether a refused pinch paying the 24-minute tube feels like physics or like a consolation prize.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, each line tagged with its page in the paper or marked as ours: the drops, the radius constant fitted to 200 Mev, the valley-of-stability coefficients, the skin, the smallest piece, the odds curve (`ODDS_FLAT`, `ODDS_HALF`, `ODDS_POWER`), the neutron budgets, the two shelves of tubes with their sources, and the paper's three chains for the check. Change anything and run `node calibration.js`; then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure. `node session-test.js` plays three sessions in headless Chromium and refreshes the screenshots; `node loop.js` prints a session and the strategies table.
3. What we were least sure about: the odds curve, which is ours and not the paper's; the hold being weakly dominated while barium is still wanted, since a refused pinch pays it; the column rule as the whole of "acts like" (it lets the rare earths light lanthanum's tube and lets gadolinium pass for lanthanum); the collapsed rare-earth cell; eka-iridium, eka-platinum and eka-gold on the shelf, which come from the 1937 scheme the paper cites rather than from the letter itself; the deterministic neutron share where the paper says chance; and whether four neutrons is too harsh for a first-time player, who clears the shelf about two times in five by aiming at barium's own cell.
4. The game has had the builder's review only: screenshots at phone and laptop width and three scripted sessions. No stranger has played this build.
