# Leibniz's Even Hand — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-choose.png` (the first screen, three of nine picked), `screenshot-a-1-placards.png` (the ban-heavy page placed by all eight, with the four counts under the bench), `screenshot-a-2-placards.png` (eight grey question marks: the sign), `screenshot-a-3-placards.png` (the homework quarrel: the mirror of counts caught by the left-out readers alone), `screenshot-a-1-selected.png` (a line selected on the page, the four tools) and `screenshot-a-end.png` (the verdict, with "What the readers cannot see"). Then hand it to a teenager on a phone: the things to judge are whether one reading of the phones quarrel is enough to learn the four readers, and whether the homework quarrel, where a fair page has to give the louder side more of the page, lands as a discovery or as a trick.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the lines a full point, a short point and a reply take; the four readers' labels and one-sentence rules; the nine quarrels in three pools of three (question, side names, stance labels, budget and minimum, every point in full and short and every reply), one per pool picked by the player on the first screen, no defaults; the words the game says. A tenth quarrel is a new object in the right pool, with the same budget and minimum as its pool-mates; the chooser lists whatever the pool holds. Change anything and run `node calibration.js`: it re-enumerates every page of every quarrel and tells you whether a fair page still exists and which ways of playing are caught. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the readers have no tolerance: one line places you, which is "picking out words everywhere" but may feel harsh to a teenager on the first page; a tolerance is a threshold in `placedBy()`; (b) the left-out reader counts points, not shares, so in the homework quarrel (four points against two) a fair page carries three scrap points against one keep point, or all six; that is Leibniz's note 5 taken literally, and a reader who counts shares is one of the robustness checks; (c) six short points with no answers at all passes the pets quarrel because it is mirrored, which Leibniz would call ploy 4 twice over; (d) the readers count and cannot weigh, so two mirrored pages with different points chosen are both fair; the verdict now says so in its last section, and the calibration check confirms it; (e) the translation's page address, `leibniz-translations.com/controversies.htm`, could not be checked from the build session (the site was unreachable); the credit lines and About use it, so check the link before sending the game on; (f) the quarrels' points are ours, written to be fair to both sides; the cars and office quarrels are the adult pair, the rest are ones a teenager has a view on; the author of a side will disagree about its best point; (g) the chooser labels the rounds (ten lines; six lines; one side has twice as much to say), which tells the player the shape before they have met it; honest, and the write screen shows the budget anyway, but it is the one place the game explains ahead of the move; (h) the three new quarrels (uniforms, seasons, votes at sixteen) have had the calibration run and the scripted session only.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and scripted browser sessions at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured.
