| **Leibniz's Even Hand** | Leibniz (1680), *On Controversies*, A IV 3, 204–212, in Lloyd Strickland's translation (2021). The sign of a fair account of a dispute: no reader can tell whose side the writer is on. You are the one who reports, on a 2020s protest-print poster: nine quarrels people still have in three rounds (phones in school, cars in the city centre, school uniforms; dogs or cats, pineapple on pizza, summer or winter; homework, the office, votes at sixteen), three picked on the first screen and each written up on one short page from what both sides said, in your order, in full or in short, with or without the answers. Eight readers read it, four a side and every one of them on a side, and they count rather than weigh: who speaks more lines, whose points were left out, whose stand unanswered, who gets the first word and the last; then each holds up a placard in the colour of the side he places you with, or a grey question mark. One page a quarrel, final once read; the short page, then the uneven quarrel where one side has twice as much to say. The verdict in words, where you said you stood against where the readers put you, and what the readers cannot see. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/controversies/leibnizs-even-hand.html) · [memo](controversies/memo.md) |
