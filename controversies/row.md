| **Leibniz's Even Hand** | Leibniz (1680), *On Controversies*, A IV 3, 204–212, in Lloyd Strickland's translation (2021). The sign of a fair account of a dispute: no reader can tell whose side the writer is on. You are the rapporteur, on a 2020s protest-print poster: three quarrels people still have (cars in the city centre, dogs or cats, back to the office), each written up on one short page from what both sides said, in your order, in full or in brief, with or without the answers. Six partisans read it, three a side, and they count rather than weigh: the lines each side speaks, each side's reasons left out and left unanswered, the first line and the last; then each holds up a placard in the colour of the side he places you with, or a grey question mark. One draft and one revision a quarrel; the short page, then the uneven quarrel where one side has twice as much to say. The verdict in words, and where you said you stood against where the readers put you. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/controversies/leibnizs-even-hand.html) · [memo](controversies/memo.md) |
