# Brown's Restless Dust — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-day1-result.png` (the Sphinx's dust dancing, the first slip on LIFE), `screenshot-a-day2-mid.png` and `screenshot-a-day2-result.png` (the open drop: streams, then the dry ring on the glass, and no slip), `screenshot-a-day3-mid.png` (the drop sealed in almond oil with the hour on the clock), `screenshot-a-day6-mid.png` (the coarse grind: lumps still, dust dancing), and `screenshot-a-end.png` (the verdict, Brown's lines, and the coda "What came later"). The vignettes are in the `-vignette` shots.
2. Everything tunable is in the `CONFIG` block at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: Brown's sizes and times with the page of the 1866 reprint each was read from; our vivacity curve (`VIV_ALPHA`, `LUMP_REST_D`) and currents curve (`CURRENT_POWER`, `CURRENT_OVERWHELM`); the five suspects and the fourteen looks with the suspects each sets aside and the page that says so; the day budget; the clock compression. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (31 checks against the paper's statements, the heuristics table and the robustness runs), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to finish if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the whole thing is a toy over prose; the numbers are Brown's measurements in words, read from a scan of typeset text, and every curve between them is ours; (b) the mapping of looks to suspects: "one speck alone" is credited with setting aside pull, drying and currents at once because Brown's oil-drop paragraph (p. 482) answers all three with one experiment, and the inverted experiment alone answers the skin; a reader may want the heat test counted as its own suspect rather than folded into life; (c) the impossible aim: the game tells the player at the verdict, and in the help, that the dust cannot be made to lie still, and a stranger who expected to win may feel tricked; the yardstick under the plate says what happened each day in plain words, which is the skill's prescribed honesty for an impossibility result; (d) the coda: it is the only place the later interpretation appears, and it says that two common claims about Brown (that he watched pollen grains move; that he thought the specks alive) are not in his pamphlet, which is true of this text; whether that is the right length for a verdict screen is for the author to say.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and two scripted browser sessions at 380 px (the physicist's week and the vitalist's week). It has not been played by real strangers, and the four-to-five-minute length is an estimate.
