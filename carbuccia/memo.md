# The Crèche Counter — design memo

## 0. Given

Not Steffen's: a gift for the paper's authors, with the beta line in the footer and no signature or counter. No recipient named. No ideas given; every choice below is the engine's.

## 1. Paper

Laudine Carbuccia, Arthur Heim, Carlo Barone and Coralie Chevallier, "A randomized controlled trial on the effect of administrative burden and information costs on social inequalities in early childcare access in France", *Nature Human Behaviour* 10 (January 2026): 64–79. Published online 11 September 2025. DOI [10.1038/s41562-025-02293-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02293-4). Preregistered as AEARCTR-0009901. Data and replication code on OSF (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/RH7EB) and GitHub (LaudineC/RCT_RR_NHB).

What was available: the typeset PDF with the main text, Tables 1–2, Figures 1–6, Extended Data Figures 1–3 and Extended Data Tables 1–6, and the reporting summary. Not the supplementary information, not the data. Numbers from the Extended Data tables are typeset and exact; the subgroup effects of Figures 5 and 6 are read off the figures where the text does not give them, and are tagged as such in the config block.

## 2. What the paper hands over

**Primary: an estimate.** A three-arm RCT with 1,849 pregnant women recruited in eight Paris-region maternity wards, randomised to an active control, five text messages with short videos (information only, €5.50 a family), or the same videos plus phone calls from a trained helper who diagnosed the family's needs, chose a childcare solution with them and filled in the forms (information + support, €55 a family, 15 minutes on average; about half of those offered the calls took them). One year later: did the household apply for early childcare, and did it get a place. Intention-to-treat effects overall, by the mother's education (the paper's SES measure), by whether she was born abroad, and for subgroups facing high information or psychological costs; average effects on those who actually took the calls, by instrumenting take-up with assignment.

**Secondary: an argument.** The multilevel framework of non-take-up: information costs, process costs (the administrative burden of the forms), psychological and social costs, and beneath them all structural barriers: too few places and town-hall allocation rules ("previousness") that favour dual-earner families. The paper's reading of its own null on access rests on this argument; the trial does not identify the allocation rules.

The game reconstructs the estimate as a data-generating process and uses the argument only in words, where the paper does.

## 3. The discovery, and its kind

**A counterintuitive result, twice over.** Policy and much of the public believe that families who do not use a service need to be told about it: cheap, scalable information. The paper shows that five videos and reminders change nothing it can measure, while a helper on the phone gets mothers without a degree and mothers born abroad to apply about as often as the others: for those who took the calls, the application gap nearly closes. Then the second turn: the applications go in, and the places do not follow. The gap in places between mothers with a degree and mothers without is as wide as before. Where the calls did win crèche places, they went to mothers with a degree, both parents in work, who swapped a childminder for a crèche. What stands between an application and a place is not in the mother's hands.

The kind of result shapes the game: the player must be able to act on the belief (send information), meet the null, then act on the finding (send help), cheer at the applications, and meet the wall.

## 4. The core narrative

A hundred women are waiting at a maternity ward in Paris, each with a baby on the way, and most of them want a crèche place for it. You hold a purse of public money for four years. You can send everyone a few text messages with little films about how to apply, which is cheap. Or you can have a helper phone some of them and fill in the forms with them, which costs ten times more a family. The films change nothing. The phone calls get the mothers who were falling behind to send in their forms. But the town hall gives out the places the way it always did, and the mothers who were falling behind still get fewer. The help worked on the mothers; it could not work on the town hall.

## 5. Who lives in the paper

- **The expectant mother** (the subject): has an intention (81% want early childcare), faces the forms, the phone call, the June commission. Controls whether to watch, pick up, apply. Could be asked to anticipate whether applying is worth the trouble. The paper gives her only an average response; playing her would turn an average effect into an individual one.
- **The fund** (the Cnaf, which co-funded the trial and employs a co-author; the policymaker): controls what to send, to whom, at what cost. Anticipates which barrier is binding. The paper's own question is this seat's.
- **The helper on the phone** (the research assistant): controls the call; the paper reports what the calls did on average, not what any one call did.
- **The experimenter**: randomises, waits a year, phones everyone back; controls the arms and the comparison; anticipates noise. The identification (ITT against the half who took the calls) is a real point of the paper but a secondary one.
- **The town hall's commission** (the structural barrier): controls the allocation rule. The paper describes it and does not model it.

## 7. The aim and the verdict

**The aim**, on its own line: *Four years and a purse: get the mothers without a degree a place as often as the mothers with one.*

**The yardstick**, in the aim's units: the board shows two numbers, each the gap between the two groups in a ward of a hundred mothers: how many more mothers with a degree applied than without, and how many more got a place. The second is the aim.

**The verdict** leads with: *You spent [the purse] on [the four orders]. The gap in applications [closed by a third in the years you called the mothers without a degree]; the gap in places [hardly moved: 33 to 30 in a hundred]. The paper found the same: the videos changed nothing it could measure; a helper on the phone got mothers without a degree, and mothers born abroad, to apply nearly as often as the others; places did not follow, except for the few who knew least about the system; and the crèche places the calls did win went mostly to mothers with a degree. What stands between an application and a place is not in the mother's hands: too few places, and the town hall's rules for giving them out.* Then what the paper says about the rules. No score, no best path.

## 8. Three concepts

**A. The Trial** (Experimenter, Discovery). The player runs the RCT: splits the ward into arms, picks the arms (nothing, videos, calls), picks how many families, waits a year, and reads noisy results; a second trial lets them re-split. Controls: arm sizes. Deliberation: how big a trial to see a 4-point effect. Discovery: the videos' null, then that the half who picked up the phone look different from the half who did not, and why assignment, not take-up, is the fair comparison. Fun: anticipation, discovery. Replay: a different split. Weak for the stranger: the action is sample sizes, and the discovery is the method's, not the mothers'.

**B. The Counter** (Controller, Strategic) — *the pick.* The player is the fund. Each year a new ward of a hundred expectant mothers, a purse that carries over, two tools at the paper's prices, five targets; the player gives one order, sends it, and the year runs: messages go out, phones ring, the June commission sits, the board lights up. Controls: tool × target, one order a year, four years. Deliberation: cheap and wide or dear and narrow; and whom. Discovery: the videos do nothing; the calls close the application gap for the targeted mothers; the places do not follow; the places the calls do win go to the front; the one target where places move is the mothers who knew least. Fun: anticipation (the year runs), agency (the application bars move for the mothers you chose), discovery (the board's second number), mastery (year three is spent better than year one). Replay: a different order of orders, the birthplace cut, the mothers not in work.

**C. The Commission** (the reckless one: the structural barrier as the player). The player sits at the town hall in June with the Seine-Saint-Denis supply (35 places per 100 babies), a stack of applications, and the rule to set: first come, both parents in work, social priority, a lottery; the applications are those the calls produced. Discovery: no rule closes the gap at 35 per 100 unless it favours the back, and "both parents in work" hands the places to the mothers with a degree. Fun: agency, discovery. Replay: other supplies (Paris at 80). *Why it is reckless*: the paper does not model the allocation; every number in the commission would be ours, and the discovery would follow from our rule, which build.md names as a mechanic and not a finding. Its walkthrough is in section 9.

A fourth, written out and dropped: **The Waiting Room** (Subject): one mother, no degree, born abroad, walking the year: watch the video or not, pick up or not, fill in the form or not. Every outcome would be a probability drawn for one person from an average effect, which is the trap build.md names; and the player is told, not finding.

## 9. The first five minutes

**The pick, B.** Minute 0: a pale wall, a black board with red digits reading 100, a title, three lines (a maternity ward, a purse, two tools), the aim on its own line, a ticket that says BEGIN. Tap. Minute 1: the ward with no help. A staircase of three steps up to a counter window: WANTS A PLACE, APPLIED, GOT A PLACE. Two bars on each step, blue (with a degree) and orange (without). The bars shrink as they climb, the orange faster; the board reads APPLIED 24, PLACES 33. A line says what the stairs show and that this is what happens when the fund does nothing. The player is wondering: where does the orange go? Tap NEXT. Minute 2: year one. The purse reads 3000. Two tools as ticket stubs: VIDEOS, €5.50 a mother, five text messages with short films on how to apply; CALLS, €55 a mother, the videos plus a helper who phones and fills in the forms. Five targets: everyone (100), without a degree (39), born abroad (46), who know least about crèches (15), not in work (30). The player taps VIDEOS and EVERYONE: the board shows the cost, 550, against the purse. They are weighing: that reaches everyone for the price of calling ten. They tap SEND. The year runs: messages go out across the ward, the board counts the months, the stairs fill, the shutter opens, the places bars fill, the shutter drops. The board reads APPLIED 24, PLACES 33. The game says: a hundred mothers got the five messages; about as many applied as with nothing, and about as many got a place; the paper found the same. Frown. Minute 3: year two, purse 5450. Calls, without a degree, 2145. SEND. The orange APPLIED bar grows past its ghost: the board reads APPLIED 15. Grin. The PLACES bar: 30. The game says: of the 39 you called, about half picked up; the mothers without a degree applied nearly as often as the others; the town hall gave out the places as before; the paper found the same, and says why. Minute 4: year three, purse 6305. The player wants to know whether anyone can be got a place. Calls to everyone, 5500? Or the 15 who know least, 825? They try the 15: the board's PLACES number drops to 28, and the game says that of the fifteen, one in twenty had got a place before and now one in three; a small group, the paper says, and an imprecise estimate. Minute 5: year four, calls to everyone; the board's places number barely moves and the game says where the crèche places went. The verdict.

**The reckless, C.** Minute 0: the same board, the commission's table. Minute 1: a stack of applications, orange and blue; 35 places. The rule as four ticket stubs. The player picks "both parents in work" because it sounds fair; the places go blue. Picks the lottery; the gap closes to the application gap. Picks social priority; the gap reverses. By minute 3 the player has found that the rule decides, which is true and not the paper's; nothing in the paper says what a lottery would do in Paris. "And I don't know what I'm supposed to feel": the player operates a thing the authors did not measure. Dropped; what it gives is kept in words at the shutter.

## 10. The pick, and why

**B, The Counter.** The player is in the paper's own seat with the paper's own two purchases at the paper's own prices, and the two reversals land inside the play, in the order the stranger will meet them: the cheap thing first, then the helper, then the wall. The sentence afterwards has no "guess": *I sent everyone the videos and nothing happened; I had the mothers without a degree phoned and they applied like everyone else; and the town hall gave out the places exactly as before.* Originality: no game in the table has had the player buy a policy for a population and watch an RCT's estimate answer, nor made a null result the first thing the player meets.

**Author test.** The authors would recognise the seat (the Cnaf's), the prices, the half who picked up, the application gap closing for the targeted mothers, the places not following, the crèche places going to the dual-earner mothers with a degree, and the mothers who knew least as the one place the support moved access. What they will enjoy is watching a player do what policy does: buy the videos first.

**Runner-up: A, The Trial.** Switch if the author wants the identification lesson (the half who picked up against the half who did not) as the spine, for a methods class; it costs the stranger the mothers.

**What the reckless concept would have given.** The structural barrier under the player's hand. Kept: the counter's shutter, which opens in June and drops with tickets still held, and the line at the end about the town hall's rules. Given up: setting the rule.

## 11. The shape

- **Screens**: start; the ward with no help (year 0, the control group, read not played); four years, each with an order screen and a result screen on the same staircase; the verdict; How to play; About.
- **Rounds**: four years, one order each. The purse: €3,000 a year, unspent money carrying over (ours). Calls to everyone cost €5,500, so it needs two years' money: the first real decision is whether to spend year one cheaply or save.
- **Presets** are the paper's regimes: the tools are its two arms; the targets are its heterogeneity cuts (SES, migration, the preregistered ones) and two of its exploratory subgroups (low knowledge, Fig. 5; inactive mothers, Fig. 6). The order is the player's; the control ward comes first so the gap is seen before anything is spent.
- **Score**: none. The board's two numbers describe; the verdict concludes in words.

## 12. Controls, mechanism, feedback, moment

- **Controls**: two tool stubs, five target stubs, SEND. One order a year; the order resets every year. A tool or target touched before SEND shows only its cost against the purse, never the stairs.
- **Mechanism**: for the chosen tool and target, each group's share at each step is the control mean plus the paper's conditional intention-to-treat effect for that tool, times the share of the group the order covers (coverage one when the group is the target, the cross-share when the target is a subgroup). Access is capped at application. Crèche places within places from Extended Data Table 4. The half who pick up is the paper's first stage.
- **Feedback**: the year runs on the staircase (messages, rings, the shutter), the bars fill over their ghosts, the board's two numbers light up; then the game's line: what you sent, to whom, how many picked up, what the steps did, what that means, and what the paper found.
- **Moment**: the orange APPLIED bar passing its ghost while the orange PLACES bar stays on it, and the board's second number not moving.

## 14. What is cut

The information-only arm's subgroup estimates (all nulls; the game uses the overall null for every group); the minority-norms, never-used and low-trust subgroups (two exploratory targets are enough); the mothers' labour supply (one line in the verdict); the Paris / Val-de-Marne / Seine-Saint-Denis supply differences (one ward); the post-lasso and logistic robustness; the balance table; attrition; the timing of allocations within the year; the paper's comparison with the German trial.

## 15. Divergences

- **A ward of 100** for 1,849: shares become counts of mothers. Ours.
- **One cohort a year, four years**, when the trial ran one cohort once: the paper's estimates are applied to each year's ward alike. Ours.
- **The purse and the carry-over** (€3,000 a year): the paper's costs scaled to the ward; the purse is ours, set so that calls to everyone need two years' saving.
- **Targeting**: the paper treated whole arms; a targeted order applies the paper's conditional effect for the targeted group to that group alone, and none to the rest. Assumes no spillover between mothers and additivity across years. Ours.
- **Cross-shares**: the share of the low-knowledge mothers and of the inactive mothers who are without a degree, which the paper does not report, are ours.
- **Deterministic shares**: the ward shows expected shares, not draws; the paper's uncertainty is said in words where it matters (the place effects for mothers without a degree are imprecise; the low-knowledge estimate is from a small group).
- **Wants**: intention at baseline is drawn as the first step and is unchanged by any order, as in the trial (measured before treatment).

## 16. Mechanism kind and claim boundaries

**Kind: reconstructed.** `mechanism.js` runs a data-generating process built from the paper's estimates: for each group, the untreated share at each step (wants, applied, got a place, with crèche places inside places), plus the paper's intention-to-treat effect of the chosen tool for that group, times the share of the group the order covers; access is capped at application and crèche places at both. The information-only arm is played as zero: its point estimates (−0.01, −0.02, +0.01, −0.01) sit inside simultaneous confidence intervals that include zero, and applying a non-zero null to one group would widen a gap by rounding.

- **Demonstrates**: the paper's estimated effects, as the ward shows them: the videos' null; the calls' effect on applications for mothers without a degree (0.60 to 0.69) and born abroad (0.67 to 0.76); no measurable effect on places for them; crèche places for mothers with a degree (0.23 to 0.31) when they are called; the large effect on places for the mothers who knew least (0.05 to 0.30 in ITT terms, about one in two for those who took the calls).
- **Assumes**: that offering a tool to a subgroup yields that subgroup's conditional ITT and nothing for the rest (no spillover, additivity across groups), with nothing carried between years; the cross-shares and the low-knowledge group's size; the ward of 100, the four cohorts, the purse and its carry-over; expected shares rather than draws.
- **The paper establishes**: the ITT effects of both arms overall and by education and birthplace, with their confidence intervals; the effects on those who took the calls, by instrumenting take-up with assignment; and, exploratory and imprecise, the subgroup effects by knowledge, past use, norms, activity, trust and present bias. It does not establish the allocation rules, what targeting does, or external validity beyond Paris 2022–23.

**Where each discovery comes from.** The videos' null, the application effect for the targeted mothers, the absence of a place effect for them, and the crèche places for mothers with a degree are the paper's estimates (Table 2, Extended Data Tables 3 and 4). The low-knowledge place effect is the paper's exploratory estimate (text and Fig. 5), converted from ATT to ITT by the first stage. The purse and the four years are design choices and produce no finding: they decide only how many orders the player gets and which combinations are affordable. The calibration check runs every order under alternative cross-shares and confirms that no order brings the place gap by degree under 25 in a hundred (floor 26), that only calls narrow the application gap by five or more, and that videos move nothing; the upper bound of the paper's interval for places among mothers without a degree (+0.15) would leave a gap of 18, which the result screen names as the loose end.

## 17. Calibration plan and result

`calibration.js` checks, against the paper's typeset numbers: the two cuts' weighted control means reproduce Table 2 within 0.02 (five outcomes, two cuts); the information-only point estimates sit inside their intervals, which include zero and rule out effects beyond ±0.08; the subgroup ITTs for calls average to Table 2's overall ITT within 0.02 (four outcomes, two cuts); ATT = ITT / 0.52 within 0.015 of Extended Data Table 2 (four outcomes), and the text's subgroup ATTs (0.17, 0.15) scale the same way; the derived low-knowledge and inactive ITTs land where Figures 5 and 6 put them; the headline order reproduces Extended Data Table 3 exactly (0.69, 0.39; gaps 24→15 and 33→30) and the complier gap is under 0.10; crèche places under calls to everyone reproduce Extended Data Table 4; cross-shares are consistent; the rest-of-ward ladders reconstruct Table 2; all 11 orders × 7 groups keep crèche ≤ place ≤ applied ≤ 1; costs are the paper's prices times the counts; the purse rules; the robustness runs of section 16. **Result: 72 checks, all pass** (`calibration-result.txt`).

No score, but the obvious ways of playing were run against the mechanism before any screen existed. The gap by degree (mothers per hundred), applications / places, after each year; start 24 / 33:

Reading: the decisions matter for the application gap (24 down to 15) and for the purse, and nothing the player can buy moves the place gap by more than four. That is the paper's sentence, and the game lets the player find it rather than telling it: the one simple rule that wins everywhere on applications (call the mothers without a degree) loses on places like every other, and the cheapest order that moves places at all (the fifteen who knew least) is the exploratory one, which the result screen says.
