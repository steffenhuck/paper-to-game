What then is time? If no one asks me, I know: if I wish to explain it to one that asketh, I know not.Augustine, Confessions XI

Eleven measurements, made with nothing but your mind: a moment of your own life, a voice after it has stopped, last night against yesterday, nothing, a voice in the middle of two others, a silence you plan, the sun standing still, three things from yesterday, a crowd. Each time you press and hold a black circle, or say which of some voices was yours. There is no clock.

How to play

Where is a length of time held? Measure, with no clock but your mind.

The circle is now. In the middle of the screen is a black circle on a black line. It is the present moment. Above the line is what is still to come, drawn dashed. Below the line is memory: what is over, drawn solid.

Nothing is drawn while it lasts. While a voice sounds, the circle turns red, and that is all. When the voice stops, a red bar drops into memory. While you hold, the circle turns blue. When you let go, a blue bar drops in, so you can see which was longer.

Holding. Most stations ask you to press and hold the circle for as long as something lasted: a moment of your own life, a voice you just heard, the sun standing still, a meal you had yesterday. The game then says in words how close you came: the same, nine tenths, half, double. Where there is nothing to check against, it says so.

Choosing. Some stations end with a question in big blue letters and a row of buttons under it: which of two or three voices was yours, which of your three things from yesterday was longest and by how much, how many voices were in the crowd. Touch one.

Night and day. Two holds in a row, a low note for last night and a high note for yesterday, so that the two sounds stand to each other as the night stood to the day. Only the relation is kept.

Nothing. You are told something will sound, and you listen for it three times. Then you guess how long it was, with a hold. There was nothing; the station says why no guess could be right.

The middle. Hear a short voice and a long one; hold the voice right in the middle. Twice, with two pairs. Then hold half of the last long one, and double the last short one.

The tumult. Press and hold, and a crowd of voices starts. Let go the moment the low one stops; the others go on. Then say how many voices there were.

The last page says what you did, what Augustine says about it, which kinds of scale you measured on, and shows the spans that were only ever in you beside what was offered for them.
