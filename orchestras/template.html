The More the Better?

For most of their history, symphony orchestras were all men. By 1990 some were a tenth women, some half. A study visited 78 of them and asked the players how life there was.

Draw the line the study found: how life in an orchestra changes as women go from a handful to half of it.

How to play

Draw the line the study found: how life in an orchestra changes as women go from a handful to half of it.

The setting. In 1990–91 a study visited 78 professional orchestras in the United States, the United Kingdom and the two Germanies and surveyed their players. The orchestras ranged from 2% to 59% women. The study sorted them into five groups by share of women and compared what players said.

The bet. First, one quick question with two answers. Pick one; the card turns.

The charts. Then four charts, one per measure. Each has five columns, one per group of orchestras, from a few women to about half. The first column already has a dot: that is what players in the orchestras with the fewest women said, on a scale from 1 to 7. Tap or drag in the other four columns to draw where you think the answer was with more women. When all four dots are placed, press Show what the study found, and the study's line is drawn over yours.

The yardstick. Shape right means your line falls and recovers where the study's line does (or doesn't). The miss is how far your dots were from the study's, on average, in points on the 7-point scale.

What the game shows. Whether "the more the better", the common view of what happens when women join a men's institution, describes what the study found.
