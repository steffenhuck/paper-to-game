Harvard, 1912

Leavitt’s Cloud

A far-away cloud of stars, on glass

In 1912, Henrietta Leavitt at Harvard looked at photos of a far-away cloud of stars, taken on glass night after night. Some of the stars blink: they get bright, fade, and get bright again, over and over.

You will look at her photos, turn a dial to find how long each star takes to blink, and put the star on a chart. Then you look at the chart. Is there a pattern?

How to play

The photos

Each glass photo shows a far-away cloud of stars on one night. Every black dot is a star. The bigger the dot, the brighter the star. The star in the ring is the one you are timing.

Each photo becomes a dot in the box below it. Further right means a later night. Higher means the star was brighter that night. At first the dots look messy, because the photos were taken on random nights.

The dial

The blink time is how long the star takes to blink once: a quick jump up to bright, then a slow slide down. Move the dial to guess it. The dots jump to new places: now they show where each night fell inside one blink, and the box shows two blinks side by side.

Wrong guess: the dots stay messy. Right guess: they make the same shape twice, a jump up and a slow slide down. The top dial goes from 1 day to 128 days. The lower dial is for fine tuning.

Careful: if you see two jumps in each half of the box, your guess is twice too long.

Write it down

When the dots line up, press Write it down. Then you see Leavitt’s own blink time for that star, in violet, and her blink drawn over your dots.

The chart

Each star goes on the chart as a line from its faintest to its brightest. Left to right is the blink time you wrote: slow blinkers on the right. Each step to the right doubles the time. Up is brighter. Watch what the lines do.

The last star

For the seventh star you guess its blink time from how bright it is, before its photos arrive. A dashed line shows where your guess would put it on the chart. Then its photos come in and you time it as before, starting from your guess.

Violet

Everything in violet is Leavitt’s. You only see it after you have written down yours.
