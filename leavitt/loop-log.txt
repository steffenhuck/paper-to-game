VERDICT
  You timed seven blinking stars. Your blink times matched Leavitt’s, except at Harvard 1505, where yours fitted two of her blinks. You guessed the last one from its brightness alone, and your guess was close. 
  The pattern: the brighter the star, the slower it blinks. When a blink takes about three times as long, the star gives off about two and a half times as much light. 
  Why this matters: all these stars are in the same cloud, so they are all about the same distance from us. That means the brighter-looking ones really are brighter. So if you find a star like this anywhere in the sky, its blink tells you how bright it really is, and then how faint it looks tells you how far away it is. Leavitt did not say that in 1912. She hoped someone would find out.
