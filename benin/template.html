The Honest Billboard

Benin City, Nigeria. In 2017 almost 1 in 4 households saw someone leave for Europe by the road across the desert and the sea. Europe pays for posters here, telling people not to go.

About four minutes. Two campaigns of three billboards. Every number comes from a 2018 survey of 555 households in Benin City.

    How to play

    The setting. Benin City in Nigeria is where more people leave for Europe by the dangerous road across the Sahara and the Mediterranean than almost anywhere else. European governments pay for poster campaigns there, mostly saying that Europe is not as good as people think.

    What you do. You choose 3 billboards out of 6. Every one states a true fact. Then each one turns over to show how people in Benin City answered the same question in a real 2018 survey.

    The yardstick. Of 100 people, a billboard warns those who believed something rosier than the truth, is old news to those who knew it, and backfires on those who believed something gloomier, because for them the truth makes Europe or the trip look better. A billboard's score is warned minus backfired. A campaign's score adds up its three billboards, and you see the best score possible next to yours.

    Two campaigns. After the first campaign you get a second. The billboards you have seen keep their scores, and the rest you must judge from what you learned. If your first campaign is already the best possible, the game ends there.

    What the game is trying to show. Whether the people these campaigns target are really as misinformed as the campaigns assume, and about what.

    A billboard's score is about beliefs: whether the fact is better or worse than what someone thought. The survey cannot say whether anyone would then stay or go, and neither does the game.
