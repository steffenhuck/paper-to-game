# Jevons's Noon: author note

**Look first at** the ruler (both rows: how big the fire gets, how long the coal lasts), then the 1885 engine card and the burn after it. Play to the end: the verdict leads with the score as an even share, shows the best path dotted on your sheet and in hollow bars in your ledger, and flags that it is a plan for the engines. Then *The curve, continued* (silent, as your Given had it), and the button after the date, *Why it stopped*, for the coda.

**The config block** is at the top of `jevons/mechanism.js`. Every number is tagged with where it comes from: the frontispiece, the preface, ch. I, ch. VII, or "our choice". The production engine's anchors are `ALPHA` and `FUEL_SHARE_1865`; the engines' terms are in `ENGINES`; the box after is `AFTER_YEARS`; the coda's substitutes are `SUBSTITUTE`, and its data `MIX`. The best paths (the score's, the one without engines, the coda's two) are searched by `jevons/optimum.js` whenever `python3 jevons/build.py` builds the page, and `calibration.js` checks them against a fresh search. The words are in `jevons/words.js`.

**Least sure about:** the score is ours three times over (the planner: log utility, every generation the same, no discounting; the heads: the people Britain would have had at the census rate; the box after). The coda's substitute is a floor of twice the 1865 income per head, which is crude; the direction it shows (an earlier substitute, a faster best path, less coal left) is the robust part, not the numbers. The coda's energy data before 1965 has no breakdown, so the stack starts there, at a marked rule. The real coal series before 1900 is recalled from Mitchell, and Hull's 83,544 is from ch. II.

The game has had the builder's review only (screenshots and a scripted browser session); no stranger has played it yet.
