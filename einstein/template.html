How to play
Deliver flowers to the stars at a profit.

Every order is a star and a customer. The customer names a fee, the most they will pay. What they actually pay depends on what they care about.

Customers

A florist pays for freshness: the full fee for flowers that haven't aged at all, less as they age, nothing once they are older than the flowers keep. A hospital pays for speed: the full fee if the rocket arrives as soon as light could, less for every year it arrives later than that, nothing once its patience runs out. A gallery pays half for each.

Boosters and fuel

Each booster makes the rocket go half as fast as light, faster than it already was. You choose 0 to 6 per rocket; 0 refuses the order. Every booster burns fuel, and the bill climbs steeply the closer the rocket gets to light speed. Profit is what the customer paid minus the fuel.

The race

Every rocket races a beam of light launched at the same moment. Yellow is always light. Red is always your rocket.

The two clocks

Home clock: the star's station clock, set to agree with home; how long the customer waited. Flowers' clock: a clock riding in the rocket with the flowers; how much they aged. The bar at the bottom of each dial is where that customer stops paying. Hand in the white half: still worth something.

Rounds

  • Test range: not the game yet. One florist, as many launches as you like, nothing counts. Slide the boosters and watch the race, the clocks and the till.
  • Order book: the game. First choose whom you fly for: the florists' guild (every order pays for freshness), the hospital service (every order pays for speed), or a mixed book. Then four orders: set each rocket's boosters, or refuse the order, and launch them all. Each card shows what the customer would pay and what the fuel costs before you launch; the launch confirms it. In the mixed book one order can't be made to pay: refusing it is right.
  • The long haul: a museum, a hundred light-years away.
