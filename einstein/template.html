How to play
Sell flowers to the stars. Make money today.

Where everyone is

You are in Bern and keep Bern time. The customer is at a star, light-years away; the star's station clock has been set to agree with Bern's by light signals (that is the first thing Einstein's paper does). The flowers are in the rocket, and the rocket has its own clock, which runs slow: the faster the rocket, the slower.

The two clocks

Station clock: when the rocket arrives, in Bern time. That is when you are paid. Rocket clock: how much time passed inside the rocket, which is how old the flowers are when they arrive. The bar at the bottom of the rocket clock is the keep-time: hand still in the white, the flowers are alive; past it, they are dead and nobody pays.

Money today

The fee is paid on arrival. A fee paid in years from now is worth less to you today; how much less depends on which merchant you are: the patient merchant doesn't mind waiting at all, the ordinary merchant loses 4% a year, the merchant with a loan 10% a year. The till shows the fee, what it is worth today, the fuel, and the profit today.

Boosters and fuel

Each booster makes the rocket go half as fast as light, faster than it already was. Every booster burns fuel, and the bill climbs steeply near light speed. Nothing beats light: however many boosters you fire, the rocket cannot arrive before light would. But boosters do shrink the rocket's clock without limit, so the flowers can be made to arrive as young as you like, at a price.

The race

Every rocket races a beam of light launched at the same moment. Yellow is always light. Red is always your rocket.

Rounds

  • Test range: not the game yet. One star, as many launches as you like, nothing counts. Slide the boosters and watch the race, the clocks and the till.
  • Order book: the game. First choose which merchant you are. Then four stars: set each rocket's boosters, or refuse the order, and launch them all. Each card shows the fee, what it is worth to you today, and the fuel, before you launch.
  • The long haul: a museum, a hundred light-years away.

How it ends

At the end of the book you die, as merchants do, and the verdict says how: the richest merchant in Bern, a comfortable man, solvent, a poor man, or a pauper, by how much of the money there was to be made you made.
