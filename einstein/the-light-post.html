How to play
Deliver flowers to the stars at a profit.

Every order is a star and a customer. The customer names a fee, the most they will pay. What they actually pay depends on what they care about.

Customers

A florist pays for freshness: the full fee for flowers that haven't aged at all, less as they age, nothing once they are older than the flowers keep. A hospital pays for speed: the full fee if the rocket arrives as soon as light could, less for every year it arrives later than that, nothing once its patience runs out. A gallery pays half for each.

Boosters and fuel

Each booster makes the rocket go half as fast as light, faster than it already was. You choose 0 to 6 per rocket; 0 refuses the order. Every booster burns fuel, and the bill climbs steeply the closer the rocket gets to light speed. Profit is what the customer paid minus the fuel.

The race

Every rocket races a beam of light launched at the same moment. Yellow is always light. Red is always your rocket.

The two clocks

Home clock: the star's station clock, set to agree with home; how long the customer waited. Flowers' clock: a clock riding in the rocket with the flowers; how much they aged. The bar at the bottom of each dial is where that customer stops paying. Hand in the white half: still worth something.

Rounds

  • Test range: not the game yet. One florist, as many launches as you like, nothing counts. Slide the boosters and watch the race, the clocks and the till.
  • Order book: the game. First choose whom you fly for: the florists' guild (every order pays for freshness), the hospital service (every order pays for speed), or a mixed book. Then four orders: set each rocket's boosters, or refuse the order, and launch them all. Each card shows what the customer would pay and what the fuel costs before you launch; the launch confirms it. In the mixed book one order can't be made to pay: refusing it is right.
  • The long haul: a museum, a hundred light-years away.
About this game

The Light Post

The game in one line

You choose how many boosters each rocket fires. The speeds, the clocks and the fuel bills are Einstein's 1905 equations, computed exactly. The customers, their fees, what they pay for and the price of fuel are this game's.

What the paper found

Einstein started from two rules. The laws of physics are the same for anyone moving steadily. And light always travels at the same speed, whoever sent it. From these alone he showed that clocks that agree for one observer disagree for another who is moving (§1–2). He showed that a moving clock runs slow by the factor √(1 − v²/c²) (§4), and that a clock sent on a journey and brought back lags behind one that stayed. He showed that speeds combine as (v + w)/(1 + vw/c²) (§5), so two speeds below light's always make one below light's: half and half make four-fifths, and light's speed cannot be reached by adding speeds below it. And he showed (§10) that the energy a body needs to move grows as mc²(1/√(1 − v²/c²) − 1), without limit as it nears the speed of light.

Mechanism: ported, with a market of our own on top

This game demonstrates what the paper's three equations say about rockets boosted again and again, about the clocks they carry, and about what the boosting costs. Every booster adds half light speed in the rocket's own frame by the §5 rule. Arrival on the home clock is distance ÷ speed, the far station's clock set to agree with home by light signals, the rule of §1. The flowers' time is that times √(1 − v²/c²), from §4. The fuel bill is §10's kinetic energy at a price per unit.

It assumes boosts that happen instantly, steady flight, no braking, and the booster size of half light speed. The flowers age by their own clock: the paper's moving clock applied to something alive. And it assumes a market: a florist who pays for freshness, a hospital that pays for speed, a gallery that pays for both, with fees, a keep-time for the flowers, a patience for the customer, and a price of fuel. None of that is in the paper. The shapes are: how the wait shrinks, how the flowers' clock slows, how the fuel bill climbs.

The paper establishes these relations as consequences of its two principles. That nature obeys them was a matter for later experiment, not for this paper, and certainly not for this game. The game's outcomes are the equations' outcomes, not evidence for them.

What comes from the paper and what from the game. "Two halves make four-fifths", "nothing catches light", "each booster adds less speed but slows the clock more", and "the bill climbs without limit" all follow from the paper, for any booster size. The pattern the game stages — a customer who pays for speed stops wanting boosters early, a customer who pays for freshness far away pays for the late ones — follows from the paper too: near light speed the wait beyond light's own arrival shrinks like (1 − v) while the flowers' ageing shrinks like √(1 − v), so promptness runs out of road first. The calibration check confirms it survives any sensible fuel price, fee or distance. Where exactly each order's best booster count sits is a game mechanic built on those equations: change the prices and it moves.

What is cut

The whole electrodynamical part (§§6–9: magnet and conductor, Doppler shift, aberration, light pressure). Length contraction, which would shorten the rocket but changes nothing you do. The relativity of simultaneity is not played; it is the idea everything else rests on. Acceleration, braking and coming home.
