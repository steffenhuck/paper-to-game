How to play
Sell flowers to the stars. Die rich.

Ten orders, one at a time. For each, set the boosters and launch, or refuse. Then watch the rocket fly and see what you made. After the tenth, you die, and the verdict says how rich.

Where everyone is

You are in Bern and keep Bern time. The customer is at a star, light-years away; the star's station clock has been set to agree with Bern's by light signals (the first thing Einstein's paper does). The flowers are in the rocket, and the rocket has its own clock, which runs slow: the faster the rocket, the slower.

The two clocks

Station clock: when the rocket arrives, in Bern time. That is when you are paid. Rocket clock: how much time passed inside the rocket, which is how old the flowers are when they arrive. Every order says how long its flowers keep, by the rocket's clock. Younger than that: alive, and you are paid. Older: dead, and nobody pays.

Boosters

Each booster makes the rocket go half as fast as light, faster than it already was: one is half light speed, two is four-fifths, six is all but light speed. Every booster burns fuel, and the bill climbs steeply near light speed. Nothing beats light: however many boosters you fire, the rocket cannot arrive before light would. But boosters shrink the rocket's clock without limit, so the flowers can arrive as young as you like, at a price.

Money today

The fee is paid on arrival. A fee paid years from now is worth less to you today; how much less depends on which merchant you are: the patient merchant doesn't mind waiting at all, the ordinary merchant loses 4% a year, the merchant with a loan 10% a year. Your wealth is the sum of what every order made you, in today's francs.

How it ends

After ten orders you die, as merchants do. How rich depends on how much of the money there was to be made, for the merchant you chose, you made: the richest merchant in Bern, a comfortable man, solvent, a poor man, or a pauper.

The game number

Every game is drawn from a number, shown on the start screen. The same number gives the same ten stars, so you can play the same game as another merchant, or hand the number to a friend.

About this game

The Light Post

The game in one line

You are a merchant in Bern selling flowers to customers at other stars: ten orders, one at a time, then you die. For each you choose how many boosters the rocket fires, or refuse the order. The speeds, the two clocks and the fuel bills are Einstein's 1905 equations, computed exactly. The customers, the fees, the keep-times, the price of fuel, the merchants' discount rates and the draw of the ten stars from the game number are this game's.

Where everyone is

You are at home and keep home time. The customer is at a star, light-years away; the star's station clock has been synchronised with home's by light signals, which is the rule Einstein lays down in §1 of the paper, so when the rocket lands the station clock reads home time, and that is when you are paid. The flowers ride in the rocket, and the rocket carries its own clock, which by §4 runs slow. The flowers age by that clock.

What the paper found

Einstein started from two rules. The laws of physics are the same for anyone moving steadily. And light always travels at the same speed, whoever sent it. From these alone he showed how distant clocks can be synchronised (§1) and that clocks which agree for one observer disagree for another who is moving (§2). He showed that a moving clock runs slow by the factor √(1 − v²/c²) (§4). He showed that speeds combine as (v + w)/(1 + vw/c²) (§5), so two speeds below light's always make one below light's: half and half make four-fifths, and light's speed cannot be reached by adding speeds below it. And he showed (§10) that the energy a body needs to move grows as mc²(1/√(1 − v²/c²) − 1), without limit as it nears the speed of light.

Mechanism: ported, with a market of our own on top

This game demonstrates what the paper's equations say about rockets boosted again and again, about the clocks they carry, and about what the boosting costs, and what follows for a merchant who is paid on arrival: nothing you do brings the fee sooner than light would arrive, but boosters can make the flowers arrive as young as you like. Every booster adds half light speed in the rocket's own frame by the §5 rule. Arrival on the station clock is distance ÷ speed. The rocket's clock is that times √(1 − v²/c²), from §4. The fuel bill is §10's kinetic energy at a price per unit.

It assumes boosts that happen instantly, steady flight, no braking, the booster size of half light speed, and flowers that are alive until the rocket's clock passes their keep-time and dead after. And it assumes a market: a fee paid on arrival, a keep-time per order, a price of fuel, and a merchant who discounts future francs at 0, 4 or 10 percent a year. The ten orders are drawn from the game number: nine from one pool of distances, fees and keep-times, shuffled, with two constraints (the first is near and forgiving, and every game holds at least two far stars), and the tenth is always a museum a century away. None of that is in the paper. The shapes are: how the arrival time is pinned above the light-time, how the rocket's clock falls, how the fuel bill climbs.

The paper establishes these relations as consequences of its two principles. That nature obeys them was a matter for later experiment, not for this paper, and certainly not for this game. The game's outcomes are the equations' outcomes, not evidence for them.

What comes from the paper and what from the game. "Two halves make four-fifths", "nothing catches light", "the fee can never arrive before light would", "every booster makes the flowers younger on arrival", and "the bill climbs without limit" all follow from the paper, for any booster size. The pattern the game stages, that a merchant who discounts refuses the far stars first and the near ones never, follows from the first of those together with discounting: the calibration check confirms it for any fuel price, fee and keep-time tried. Where exactly each star's best booster count sits, and which merchant sends to which star, are game mechanics built on those equations: change the prices and they move.

What is cut

The whole electrodynamical part (§§6–9: magnet and conductor, Doppler shift, aberration, light pressure). Length contraction, which would shorten the rocket but changes nothing you do. The relativity of simultaneity is used (the station clock agrees with home's because the two are at rest relative to each other) but not played. Acceleration, braking and coming home.
