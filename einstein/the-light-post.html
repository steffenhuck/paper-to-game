How to play
Sell flowers to the stars. Make money today.

Where everyone is

You are in Bern and keep Bern time. The customer is at a star, light-years away; the star's station clock has been set to agree with Bern's by light signals (that is the first thing Einstein's paper does). The flowers are in the rocket, and the rocket has its own clock, which runs slow: the faster the rocket, the slower.

The two clocks

Station clock: when the rocket arrives, in Bern time. That is when you are paid. Rocket clock: how much time passed inside the rocket, which is how old the flowers are when they arrive. The bar at the bottom of the rocket clock is the keep-time: hand still in the white, the flowers are alive; past it, they are dead and nobody pays.

Money today

The fee is paid on arrival. A fee paid in years from now is worth less to you today; how much less depends on which merchant you are: the patient merchant doesn't mind waiting at all, the ordinary merchant loses 4% a year, the merchant with a loan 10% a year. The till shows the fee, what it is worth today, the fuel, and the profit today.

Boosters and fuel

Each booster makes the rocket go half as fast as light, faster than it already was. Every booster burns fuel, and the bill climbs steeply near light speed. Nothing beats light: however many boosters you fire, the rocket cannot arrive before light would. But boosters do shrink the rocket's clock without limit, so the flowers can be made to arrive as young as you like, at a price.

The race

Every rocket races a beam of light launched at the same moment. Yellow is always light. Red is always your rocket.

Rounds

  • Test range: not the game yet. One star, as many launches as you like, nothing counts. Slide the boosters and watch the race, the clocks and the till.
  • Order book: the game. First choose which merchant you are. Then four stars: set each rocket's boosters, or refuse the order, and launch them all. Each card shows the fee, what it is worth to you today, and the fuel, before you launch.
  • The long haul: a museum, a hundred light-years away.

How it ends

At the end of the book you die, as merchants do, and the verdict says how: the richest merchant in Bern, a comfortable man, solvent, a poor man, or a pauper, by how much of the money there was to be made you made.

About this game

The Light Post

The game in one line

You are a merchant in Bern selling flowers to customers at other stars. You choose how many boosters each rocket fires, or refuse the order. The speeds, the two clocks and the fuel bills are Einstein's 1905 equations, computed exactly. The customers, the fees, the keep-times, the price of fuel and the merchants' discount rates are this game's.

Where everyone is

You are at home and keep home time. The customer is at a star, light-years away; the star's station clock has been synchronised with home's by light signals, which is the rule Einstein lays down in §1 of the paper, so when the rocket lands the station clock reads home time, and that is when you are paid. The flowers ride in the rocket, and the rocket carries its own clock, which by §4 runs slow. The flowers age by that clock.

What the paper found

Einstein started from two rules. The laws of physics are the same for anyone moving steadily. And light always travels at the same speed, whoever sent it. From these alone he showed how distant clocks can be synchronised (§1) and that clocks which agree for one observer disagree for another who is moving (§2). He showed that a moving clock runs slow by the factor √(1 − v²/c²) (§4). He showed that speeds combine as (v + w)/(1 + vw/c²) (§5), so two speeds below light's always make one below light's: half and half make four-fifths, and light's speed cannot be reached by adding speeds below it. And he showed (§10) that the energy a body needs to move grows as mc²(1/√(1 − v²/c²) − 1), without limit as it nears the speed of light.

Mechanism: ported, with a market of our own on top

This game demonstrates what the paper's equations say about rockets boosted again and again, about the clocks they carry, and about what the boosting costs, and what follows for a merchant who is paid on arrival: nothing you do brings the fee sooner than light would arrive, but boosters can make the flowers arrive as young as you like. Every booster adds half light speed in the rocket's own frame by the §5 rule. Arrival on the station clock is distance ÷ speed. The rocket's clock is that times √(1 − v²/c²), from §4. The fuel bill is §10's kinetic energy at a price per unit.

It assumes boosts that happen instantly, steady flight, no braking, the booster size of half light speed, and flowers that are alive until the rocket's clock passes their keep-time and dead after. And it assumes a market: a fee paid on arrival, a keep-time per order, a price of fuel, and a merchant who discounts future francs at 0, 4 or 10 percent a year. None of that is in the paper. The shapes are: how the arrival time is pinned above the light-time, how the rocket's clock falls, how the fuel bill climbs.

The paper establishes these relations as consequences of its two principles. That nature obeys them was a matter for later experiment, not for this paper, and certainly not for this game. The game's outcomes are the equations' outcomes, not evidence for them.

What comes from the paper and what from the game. "Two halves make four-fifths", "nothing catches light", "the fee can never arrive before light would", "every booster makes the flowers younger on arrival", and "the bill climbs without limit" all follow from the paper, for any booster size. The pattern the game stages, that a merchant who discounts refuses the far stars first and the near ones never, follows from the first of those together with discounting: the calibration check confirms it for any fuel price, fee and keep-time tried. Where exactly each star's best booster count sits, and which merchant sends to which star, are game mechanics built on those equations: change the prices and they move.

What is cut

The whole electrodynamical part (§§6–9: magnet and conductor, Doppler shift, aberration, light pressure). Length contraction, which would shorten the rocket but changes nothing you do. The relativity of simultaneity is used (the station clock agrees with home's because the two are at rest relative to each other) but not played. Acceleration, braking and coming home.
