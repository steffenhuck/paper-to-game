# Leibniz's Worlds — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-4-dark.png` and `screenshot-a-4-lamp.png` (the covered world, then the lamp dragged round the big star), `screenshot-a-2-big.png` (the big one lit and what it shut out) and `screenshot-a-3-key-out.png` (the key star put out in world 3). Then play it on a phone; the lamp is the thing to feel. Add `?seed=42` to the address to get the draw the screenshots show.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the generator's ranges for each world (star counts, weights, pairs, the lamp's holders and distances), the lamp's radius and oil, the colours, the star radius, the words for how short a world is. Change a range and run `node calibration.js`: it draws two hundred games and tells you whether every lesson still holds on every draw. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) no numbers during play: sizes carry the weights (radius 9 + 2.6 × weight), and the status line still gives the total light, so a player who wants arithmetic can get some; (b) the lamp's oil (520 px) is enough to circle the big star once and follow one line; a player who sweeps elsewhere runs dry, which is the point, but may feel cheated; (c) the two lamp worlds come in random order; (d) world 3 now needs the player to light what the step back frees, since nothing comes in on its own; the result line says so when they do not; (e) the title is a dummy; (f) About says the translation is quoted "with his permission" on the strength of Strickland's nod.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px on two seeds. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured.
