# Leibniz's Tiles — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-2-stuck.png` (world 2 with the big square in the corner, 25 lit and the long bars not fitting), then `screenshot-a-2-best.png` (the best possible world beside it, without the square), `screenshot-a-3-board.png` (world 3 as weight made it), `screenshot-a-3-pull-bar.png` (the long bar pulled: the hole), and `screenshot-a-end-lifted.png` (the verdict with the cover lifted). Then play it once on a phone; the drag is the thing to feel.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the shapes and their names, the three worlds (board size, tray, whether it is pre-filled by weight), the colours, the words for how dark a world is, and what the calibration expects. Change a world's tray and run `node calibration.js`: it recomputes the best worlds and tells you whether the lessons (the square at most 29; one pull to 36) still hold. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the toy's criterion, reality = cells, is the one big assumption; the check says the second world's lesson holds up to cells^2.5 and the third's beyond cells^4, and About says so, but a reader who holds that a grand thing is worth more than its size will want that sentence earlier; (b) world 3's trap (pulling the big square, which the last world taught you to distrust, gets 32) is ours, not the paper's; (c) the striving at *Let it be* places leftover shapes heaviest first at the first spot that fits, which is our reading of "in proportion to their reality", and it can finish a world the player left one cell short; (d) seven shapes share five colours, so two shapes in a world can be the same colour (never adjacent in the pre-filled board, but possibly in the player's); (e) the tray on the 6×6 worlds is small (15 px cells) to keep *Let it be* above the fold at 700 px; (f) "Lit", "Dark", "Waiting" on the count line are defined by contrast and context rather than in a sentence.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured.
