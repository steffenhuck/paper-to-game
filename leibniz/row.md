| **Leibniz's Tiles** | Leibniz (1697), *De rerum originatione radicali* (On the ultimate origination of things), 23 November 1697, Gerhardt VII 302–308, in Lloyd Strickland's translation (2014). Why is there this world? Because every possible thing pushes to exist in proportion to how much there is of it, not all of them fit together, and what exists is the arrangement that fits the most: Leibniz's own game of tiles, as a puzzle app at night. Three boards. Drag shapes onto the land, turn them, pull one out again; let it be, and whatever still fits comes in on its own, then the best possible world lights up beside yours. The first board cannot hold everything; on the second the big square is worth the most and is not in the best world (take it first and a fifth of the land stays dark); the third comes already filled by weight with nothing left that fits, and the only way to a fuller world is a step back, Leibniz's *reculer pour mieux sauter*. The verdict in words, then his covered picture. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/leibniz/leibnizs-tiles.html) · [memo](leibniz/memo.md) |
