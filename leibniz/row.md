| **Leibniz's Worlds** | Leibniz (1697), *De rerum originatione radicali* (On the ultimate origination of things), 23 November 1697, Gerhardt VII 302–308, in Lloyd Strickland's translation (2014). Why is there this world? Because every possible thing pushes to exist in proportion to how much there is of it, not all of them can be together, and what exists is the set that holds the most. Four worlds in a night sky. Tap a thing to let it be; it lights, and what it cannot be with goes dark, which is how you find out. Tap it again to put it out. Let it be, and whatever still can be comes in on its own; then the best possible world lights up beside yours. The grandest thing shuts out four lesser ones worth more together; the third sky arrives filled by weight and gets fuller only by a step back, Leibniz's *reculer pour mieux sauter*; the fourth is his covered picture: two worlds seen through the same window, two torches to see more, and one thing you may change before the cover lifts.
