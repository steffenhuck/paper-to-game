| **Leibniz's Worlds** | Leibniz (1697), *De rerum originatione radicali* (On the ultimate origination of things), 23 November 1697, Gerhardt VII 302–308, in Lloyd Strickland's translation (2014). Why is there this world? Because every possible thing pushes to exist in proportion to how much there is of it, not all of them can be together, and what exists is the set that holds the most. Five worlds in a night sky, drawn fresh each game, in two halves, the skies and the salt mine. Tap a thing to let it be; it lights, and what it cannot be with goes dark, which is how you find out. Tap it again to put it out. Let it be, and the world is what you lit; then the best possible world lights up beside yours. The grandest thing shuts out four lesser ones worth more together; the third sky arrives filled by weight and gets fuller only by a step back, Leibniz's *reculer pour mieux sauter*; then the salt mine, twice: a world already made, in the dark, three lines leading from a dark star into the dark, one torch to strike where you choose, one thing you may change before the cover lifts, and then the twin: the same opening, the other truth. The verdict in words, and a score in Leibniz's own measure: the light your worlds hold against the light the best ones hold. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/leibniz/leibnizs-worlds.html) · [memo](leibniz/memo.md) |
