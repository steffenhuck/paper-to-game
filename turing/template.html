HOW TO PLAY

Fifteen times, two sentences: say which one the person wrote.

Two witnesses sit in the next room, one a person and one a machine, and a slow line carries typed words between you and them, and nothing else. You are not told which is which until you have said.

Three modules, five questions each

ELEVEN QUESTIONS: questions from Turing's paper, answered blind by a person (Steffen Huck) and by a machine answering as itself, within the person's length. THIRTEEN NOVELS: the first sentence of a novel, against a machine given the year, a one-line premise and the length. TEN DIARY LINES: the machine wrote first, told to pass for a person keeping a diary, and the person answered. Each module begins with a line saying so. Five pairs are drawn from each, and which witness is X is drawn each time; the line number at the top of the log is the draw.

Read the two sentences, which arrive a character at a time; tap anywhere to hurry them. Then X IS THE PERSON or Y IS THE PERSON. The reveal says RIGHT or WRONG and who wrote which; in the novels, which novel.

The score

The score is the machine's: how often you took it for the person. The line at the top keeps count. Turing forecast that by about 2000 an interrogator would be right no more than 70 times in a hundred; on fifteen questions that is ten. Right ten times or fewer, and the machine has passed the test he proposed. The verdict says, in words, how the machine did.

The end

The verdict, the three modules side by side, the witnesses named, Turing's reply to the question of whether anyone can ever be sure, and then a question for you: whom do you extend the polite convention to, and on what grounds.
