| **Turing's Witnesses** | Turing (1950), *Mind* 59(236):433–460. Computing machinery and intelligence, played as the experiment Turing proposed: interrogators judge, and the statistics are the machine's. On a 1970s terminal, green on black, three modules of five, drawn from thirty-four real pairs: a person (Steffen Huck) and a machine answering the paper's own questions blind, the machine as itself; the first sentences of thirteen novels against a machine given the year and a premise; and ten diary lines where the machine wrote first, told to pass for a person, and the person answered. Fifteen times, two sentences: say which the person wrote, be told. The verdict is the machine's, on a scale set by Turing's 1950 bar, from failed completely to taken for the person more often than the person. Then his reply to the argument from consciousness, and the polite convention handed to you.
