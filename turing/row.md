| **Turing's Witnesses** | Turing (1950), *Mind* 59(236):433–460. Computing machinery and intelligence, read against its reputation: the test is the famous part and in 2026 machines pass it, so the game asks the question underneath. On a 1970s terminal, green on black, a person (Steffen Huck) and a machine (the one that built the game) sit in the next room and a slow line carries only words. Five questions from the paper, in its order: poetry, sums and mistakes, something new (Lady Lovelace's objection), feeling, what it is made of. Each time, tick what you will go by, read both real answers, say which has a mind behind it, and learn who wrote which. Then the two checks that do not use the line, opening them up and being them, and the polite convention handed to you: whom do you extend it to, and on what grounds.
