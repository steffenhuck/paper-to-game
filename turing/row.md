| **Turing's Witnesses** | Turing (1950), *Mind* 59(236):433–460. Computing machinery and intelligence, read against its reputation: the test is the famous part, and in 2026 machines pass it; the heart is the reply to the argument from consciousness. On a 1970s terminal, green on black, a machine and a person sit in the next room and a slow line carries only words. Write a test the machine fails and the person passes: pick a demand from Turing's own list (a sonnet, a sum, a joke, right from wrong, strawberries and cream, a soul, Lady Lovelace's "something really new", a question written against the machine's own tables), check it from the chair, with the case off, or by being it, and read what comes down the line. Six tests; the counter of separations that hold never moves, and the terminal says why each time. Then the polite convention is yours: whom do you extend it to, and on what grounds.
