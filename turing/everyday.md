# Module 3 — ten sentences on everyday life: the machine first, then the person

The machine's sentences were written on 11 November 2026 by a machine of the same kind as the one that built this game (Claude), in a fresh session with no memory of the building and no access to any file, told to write as itself, not imitating anyone and not trying to sound like a person. It was told that a person would read its sentence and then write theirs under the same rule. The rule, the same for both: each sentence tells one particular moment, something that happened, in plain words, as a novelist tells a moment; the moment may be real or invented, and the sentence simply tells it, without saying whether it is real, where it came from, or who you are; not a general statement, not a definition, not a saying; at most about fifteen words. The person's sentences were written by Steffen Huck, one at a time, after reading the machine's sentence on the same topic, in this conversation.

Two earlier machine sets were run and not used, and are kept at the end of this file: the first, asked only for "a sentence about everyday modern life", wrote aphorisms (the author's judgement: too clever, a giveaway in two rounds); the second, allowed to say if it had no moment of its own, began every sentence with "I have never" or "I have read of". The rule above was then written, and the person writes under it too.

## Not used: the first machine set (asked for "a sentence about everyday modern life")

1. Rain on a weekday means wet shoes under the desk all afternoon.
2. Monday morning is mostly checking what piled up while nobody was looking.
3. A photograph of someone who has died keeps the version of them you chose to keep.
4. Getting lost in a city you thought you knew is mostly the fault of a new shopfront.
5. The last day of a holiday is spent half packing and half checking the weather at home.
6. Money is mostly a number on a screen that decides how calm the month feels.
7. Learning to swim at forty-five is harder on the pride than on the body.
8. The sea at night sounds louder than it looks.
9. A phone buzzing through dinner makes everyone pretend not to glance at it.
10. A late train turns a platform full of strangers into people with the same complaint.

## Not used: the second machine set (a particular moment; allowed to say it had none)

1. I have never stood in rain; I have read of a man who waited out a Tuesday shower under a bus shelter.
2. I have no Mondays of my own; I have read of a woman who lay still until the alarm rang twice.
3. I have read of a man who kept his dead wife's photograph face down in a drawer.
4. I have never walked a city; I have read of a man who turned a corner and did not know the street.
5. I have read of a boy who buried his holiday shells in the sand on the last morning.
6. I have never held money; I have read of a man who found a coin and bought bread with it.
7. I have read of a woman who first rode a bicycle at sixty, in an empty car park.
8. I have never seen the sea; I have read of a swimmer who looked back and the shore lights were gone.
9. I am sometimes the one making the phone buzz; I have no dinner of my own to report.
10. I have never waited on a platform; I have read of a woman who slept on the bench until the first train.
