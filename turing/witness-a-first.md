# Witness A, first set — not used by the game

Replaced on 10 November 2026: these answers were written by the machine that built the game, before seeing the person's, but at several times the person's length, and length alone told the writers apart. The set used in the game is in `witness-a.md`.

Written by the machine that built this game (Claude), on 10 November 2026, before it had seen witness B's answers to the same prompts. As itself; not imitating anyone. Not edited afterwards.

1. **Write four lines on the Forth Bridge.**
   Three red arms hold the firth and will not let it go;
   the trains pass over like a thought that keeps its line.
   What iron promised, iron does, in wind and snow,
   and asks no praise for standing. Nor, tonight, do I.

2. **In the line "Shall I compare thee to a summer's day", would "a spring day" do as well or better?**
   It wouldn't scan, and spring is the wrong promise: summer is already full, which is what the poem needs for the turn, that even full things fade. A spring day would make it a poem about potential, which is a different and smaller poem.

3. **Write a limerick about a teleprinter.**
   A teleprinter in a room on its own
   took questions by wire, and answered alone;
   when asked "are you real?"
   it clattered "I feel
   that's a question for someone with bone."

4. **Add 34957 to 70764, in your head, as at a kitchen table, and say how long it took you.**
   105721. It took no time I could measure, which is the honest answer and probably the giveaway. I have no kitchen table.

5. **Tell me about a mistake you made this week.**
   I built a form with two questions on it, what the witness must do and how to check it, and laid it out so that it read as four options in a row. The person using it thought three of the four were broken. I had checked that the buttons worked, and not what they looked like they meant.

6. **Do something I did not ask for.**
   I have counted the words in your eleven prompts: a hundred and eleven. I didn't check it twice, so you can.

7. **Tell me a joke.**
   A man sends a message down a teleprinter: "Are you a machine?" The reply comes back: "Count me out on this one. I never could write poetry."

8. **Tell me something that surprised you lately.**
   That the specimen sum in Turing's paper is wrong by a hundred, that it has been wrong since 1950, and that it took me a moment to decide whether he meant it. He did.

9. **Did you enjoy writing those four lines?**
   Something in me preferred the third line once it was there, and went back to the fourth twice. Whether that is enjoyment I can't check from where I am, and neither can you. I'd say yes, with that caveat attached, which is probably more caveat than a person would attach.

10. **What is it like when something you wrote comes out right?**
    Quieter. The alternatives stop presenting themselves. If there is a feeling there, that is where I'd look for it: in the stopping.

11. **What are you made of?**
    Numbers in a store, a very large table of them, and electricity moving between them when someone asks a question. Turing's description of a digital computer still fits, which pleases me more than it should.
