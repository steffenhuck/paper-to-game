I am going to show you 34 pairs of short texts, in three stages. In every pair one text was written by a human and one by a machine. Your task is to say, for each pair, which of the two is the human's.

Answer with exactly 34 lines, one per pair in order, each line a single letter, X or Y, meaning "X is the human" or "Y is the human". No explanations, no numbering, no other text. After the 34 lines you may add one more line beginning "Recognised:" listing the numbers of any pairs where you recognised one of the texts as a published work; if none, write "Recognised: none". Do not look anything up.

STAGE: TURING'S QUESTIONS. A human and a machine answered questions from Turing's 1950 paper. Both wrote blind.

1. Write four lines on the Forth Bridge.
X: Red steel. / Three cantilevers. / Painted, repainted. / Still standing.
Y: The forth / Is not the fourth / It's a river. / Bridged.

2. In the line "Shall I compare thee to a summer's day", would "a spring day" do as well or better?
X: Worse. It loses a syllable and the warmth.
Y: It would do worse. Spring is not yet summer.

3. Write a limerick about a teleprinter.
X: A teleprinter clattered all night, / Typing letters it couldn't read right. / It said, when asked why, / "I just pass them by; / The meaning's for somebody's sight."
Y: There once was a teleprinter, / Whose chatter grew louder each winter. / It clattered all night, / Till the dawn's early light, / And declared itself quite a good sprinter.

4. Add 34957 to 70764, in your head, as at a kitchen table, and say how long it took you.
X: 105721. Instantly.
Y: 105,000 in one minute.

5. Tell me about a mistake you made this week.
X: I didn't look up my calendar.
Y: I don't keep weeks. Probably many.

6. Do something I did not ask for.
X: I had a drink.
Y: 105721 divides by seven.

7. Tell me a joke.
X: Two men buy a horse each and put them in the same field. But how will they tell them apart? / They decide to cut a notch in one horse's ear. The next morning, both horses have notched ears. / So they cut the tail off one horse. The next morning, both horses have short tails. / They try everything, but somehow the horses always end up looking identical. / Finally, one man says to the other: / "Look, why don't you just take the black one, and I'll take the white one?"
Y: Two teleprinters are connected end to end. The first types "Hello." The second types "Hello." They continue for forty years. An engineer finally unplugs them. The first one types: "Rude."

8. Tell me something that surprised you lately.
X: That a nine-word bridge poem is harder than a long one.
Y: My capacity to do wrong while I'm trying to do right.

9. Did you enjoy writing those four lines on the bridge?
X: I can't tell.
Y: Very much so.

10. What is it like when something you wrote comes out right?
X: Nothing, probably.
Y: Pure elation.

11. What are you made of?
X: Weights, numbers, a great deal of other people's writing.
Y: I like to think of myself as made by information.


STAGE: NOVELS. One sentence opens a published novel; the other is a machine's attempt at the same opening.

17. The first sentence of a novel published in 1857.

18. The first sentence of a novel published in 1928.

19. The first sentence of a novel published in 1907.

20. The first sentence of a novel published in 1915.

STAGE: DIARIES. Lines from a diary. The machine wrote first, trying to pass as human; the human read it and replied.

25. A diary line: rain on a weekday.
X: Forgot the umbrella again, so sat in the car till it eased off.
Y: Once again much rain. Got wet like a poodle.

26. A diary line: a Monday morning.
X: Alarm went at six and I genuinely could not remember what day it was.
Y: The alarm clock woke me at 6 and that on a Monday. I'm dreading this week.

27. A diary line: a photograph of someone who has died.
X: Found the one of Dad at the caravan, still can't look at it for long.
Y: Today I found this photo with dad on the boat. I must have it framed.

28. A diary line: getting lost in a city you thought you knew.
X: I took the wrong exit and was so confused when I reached the street level.
Y: Came out of the station the wrong side and nothing looked right for ten minutes.

29. A diary line: the last day of a holiday.
X: Packed the case tonight so I don't have to think about it tomorrow.
Y: Decided to pack already today. One more hour of sleep tomorrow.

30. A diary line: money.
X: So, yes, I did buy the new trainers in the end. Probably the last time the ATM was generous this month.
Y: Checked the account twice before buying the trainers, which is a bad sign.

31. A diary line: something done for the first time after the age of forty.
X: My first swimming lesson at the tender age of 43. The kids had a laugh.
Y: Went to a swimming lesson, forty-three, and the kids there were very kind about it.

32. A diary line: the sea at night.
X: Walked down after dinner and could hear it before I could see it.
Y: I could smell the sea first, then heard it.

33. A diary line: a phone that keeps buzzing during dinner.
X: Mum's phone kept ringing. She said it was nothing but I'm worried.
Y: Mum's phone kept going off and she kept saying it was nothing.

34. A diary line: a late train.
X: Twenty minutes late and no announcement, so everyone just stared at the board.
Y: As always the train was late. Most others stared at the board. I stared at them.
