# Turing's Witnesses — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-A-1-answers.png` (the first question, X and Y, the two buttons), `screenshot-A-1-reveal.png` (RIGHT or WRONG, who was who, the paper's line), `screenshot-A-end-verdict.png` (the machine against Turing's bar) and `screenshot-A-end-readback.png`. Then play it on a phone; the sides and the order inside each subject change with the line number.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the paper's numbers (pp. 434, 442, 448), from which the bar of seven is derived, and the line speed. The eleven prompts with both witnesses' answers, the five subject introductions and every line the terminal says are in the same file. The answers must stay byte for byte what `witness-a.md` and `witness-b.md` hold, and the machine's within your lengths: edit the files and the mechanism together, or `node calibration.js` (65 checks and the play-it-in-numbers table) fails. Then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to build if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) eleven pairs from two writers are the whole evidence, and the About page says so; (b) a final full stop was added to every answer that lacked one, on both sides, at your request, and both files say so; what is left is your occasional lower-case start, right about seven times in eleven for anyone who notices it, which the About page names; (c) the machine was told not to imitate, which is a harder test than Turing's, and said on About rather than in the verdict; (d) the bar of seven is 70 per cent of eleven rounded down, and one interrogator is a noisy measure of it; the verdict says a coin would get about five or six; (e) the machine's answer about a nine-word poem reveals that it had a word limit, which is true and declared.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px, two games through. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length (three to five minutes hurried, longer read at the line's pace) is an estimate.
