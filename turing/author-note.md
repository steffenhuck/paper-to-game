# Turing's Witnesses — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-1-set.png` (the form: a demand chosen, FROM THE CHAIR lit, the check's line under it), then `screenshot-5-result.png` (BE IT on "feel pleasure": could not be run on A, could not be run on B, and the reply to Jefferson typed once), `screenshot-6-result.png` (the Gödel question: a separation for one machine, A rebuilt, the counter back at zero) and `screenshot-end-readback.png` (the convention adopted, read back beside Turing's). Then play it once on a phone; tap to hurry the line.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the forecast (p. 442), the specimen sum and the paper's wrong answer (pp. 434, 448), the two pauses, the E.S.P. numbers (p. 453), the 2026 premise, the six-test budget and the forty-card run (ours). The fifteen demands with their pages, the three checks, every transcript and every line the terminal says are in the same file; edit the words there. Run `node calibration.js` (130 checks and the play-it-in-numbers table), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to build if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the 2026 premise is the brief's; the About screen cites, from memory, a 2025 study in which interrogators took the best machine for the human more often than the human, and asks you to verify it; (b) witness A's answers are our writing, standing for what a 2026 machine says, and A's sonnet, joke and limerick are ours; (c) the "holds" rule merges two of the paper's conditions (the next machine answers the Gödel question, p. 445; a body is not penalised, p. 435) into one counter, which is the game's yardstick and not the paper's; (d) the demand "be made of nerves" is played as a body finding under OPEN IT UP, which reads p. 434 (artificial flesh), p. 439 (electricity) and p. 451 (continuity) together; (e) the four grounds at the end are ours, each with a Turing line beside it, and the readback does not judge the player's choice; (f) the terminal's one forced run (when a player never leaves the chair in six tests) is our device for making sure the second half of the argument is met.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px, three games through. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length (five to eight minutes with the line hurried, longer read at its own pace) is an estimate.
