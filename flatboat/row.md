| **Leibniz's Flatboat** | Leibniz (c. 1705), *How to Cross a Large River in View of an Enemy Army*, LH 36 Bl. 184–185, in Lloyd Strickland's translation (2023). A boat carries the weight of the water it could hold, so what goes into it, men, guns or earth? As an engineer's sheet in white on blueprint blue: the river in plan with their guns in red on the far bank, the flatboat in section below, three dials (feet of earth at the bow, guns, men), and the water line climbing with every tun until it comes over the gunwale. Push off, watch the ball stop in the hatching or go through the planks, and watch the same earth come ashore as the parapet the men dig in behind; a gun behind it drives their battery off, and the rest pass in open boats. Three crossings, three pushes before dark in each: their battery faced directly (four feet, a gun, twenty men), the bend where only foot waits (a foot, sixty men), and shallow boats of six tuns that cannot carry both the wall and the gun, so you land where a foot will do. The verdict in words, the army's fate, and where your loadings parted from the paper's. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/flatboat/leibnizs-flatboat.html) · [memo](flatboat/memo.md) |
