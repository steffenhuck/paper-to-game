# Leibniz's Flatboat — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-1-guns.png` (a boat of guns and men loaded, the water line at eight tuns), `screenshot-a-1-p1-through.png` (the ball across the hull), `screenshot-a-1-sinkpreview.png` (the hull flooded at fourteen tuns, before the push), `screenshot-a-1-p2-stops.png` and `screenshot-a-1-p2-landed.png` (the ball in the hatching, then the same hatching on the bank with the forty behind it), `screenshot-a-1-p3-drive.png` (the gun fires, the battery drawn off), `screenshot-a-3-B.png` (the shallow boat with the two landings) and `screenshot-a-end.png` (the verdict). Then play it on a phone: the thing to judge by hand is the dials (whether three dials and a push feel like loading a boat), and by eye the ball (whether its stopping in the earth lands as the moment).
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the weights (a man a tenth of a tun, a gun two, a foot of wall two), what stops what (one foot a musket ball, two a light ball, four a six-pounder), the crews and hands, the pushes in a day, the fleet and its open boats, the two hulls, the three crossings with their landings and fire, and the fates. Every number there is ours: the paper gives the carrying rule and no quantity. Change one and run `node calibration.js`: it searches every loading and push sequence for the best in each crossing, runs nine ways of playing against them, and re-runs the lessons with the lines moved. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the feet of earth a ball needs are on the light side of period field works and were chosen so that the great hull carries wall, gun and crew exactly (8 + 2 + 2 = 12) and the shallow one cannot carry wall and gun; the earth-then-gun lesson falls if the wall no longer fits with a gun (the calibration says so at five feet); (b) "one served gun behind earth drives a battery off an open bank" is our reading of Leibniz's "even dislodging the enemy", not a rule he states; (c) the rest pass as five open boats of sixty a push, which makes the rest the bulk of the score, so the size of the first wave matters little once the bank is safe (that is the paper's own emphasis, but a reader who wants the first-wave arithmetic to count more can lower `FLEET`); (d) the sheet shows, at every landing, how many feet their ball needs, so the first push is a puzzle of arithmetic and priorities rather than a trial; the memo's switch condition (§10) is a reader who finds that too easy, in which case the feet are withheld and the first push finds them; (e) the translation's web page could not be reached from the build session, so About and the memo cite the site without a checked address, and the game does not claim permission to quote; (f) mortars, secrecy, the bend and the flosses are read on the end screen, not played.

Revision 1 (same day) answered the author's two comments: larger type everywhere, a wider page on laptops, and the rule that decides a push stated on the sheet while loading and in the result the moment it bites (memo, Revision 1).

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px, two sessions (a learning stranger and the paper's loading throughout). It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured; from the session, three to six minutes is the guess.
