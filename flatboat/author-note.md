# Leibniz's Flatboat — note for the author

1. Look first at the general's boat on the one drawing, `screenshot-a-0-1-push.png`, `-2-through.png`, `-3-sunk.png`; then the shot that sticks with its depth on the heap, `screenshot-a-1b-p1-2-stuck.png`, the sacks going up the bank, `-3-land.png`, the rest waiting, `-4-wait.png`; the cannon driving theirs off in `screenshot-a-1b-p2-3-land.png` and the rest crossing in `-4-rest.png`; the narrows at A and B, `screenshot-a-2-A.png`, `screenshot-a-2-B.png`; the musket case in `screenshot-c-2-p1-2-hit.png`; and `screenshot-a-end.png`. Then play it on a phone without knowing the feet: the thing to judge is whether one drawing carries the whole push, and whether the gamble between too much earth and too little feels like a decision.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the weights (a man a tenth of a tun, a gun two, a foot of wall two), what stops what (one foot a musket ball, two a light ball, four a six-pounder), the pushes in a day, the fleet and its open boats, the two hulls, the two crossings with their landings and fire, and the fates. Every number there is ours: the paper gives the carrying rule and no quantity. Change one and run `node calibration.js`: it searches every loading and push sequence for the best in each crossing, runs nine ways of playing against them, re-runs the lessons with the lines moved, and checks that no frame's title block runs ahead of its frame. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch; `node session-test.js` plays three sessions in headless Chromium at phone width and writes a screenshot of every frame.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the feet of earth a cannonball needs are on the light side of period field works and were chosen so that the big hull carries the wall, a cannon and twenty soldiers exactly (8 + 2 + 2 = 12) and the shallow one cannot carry the wall and a cannon with anyone aboard; the earth-then-cannon lesson falls if the wall no longer fits with a cannon (the calibration says so at five feet); (b) "one cannon behind earth drives the enemy cannons off an open shore" is our reading of Leibniz's "even dislodging the enemy", not a rule he states; (c) the rest cross as five ordinary boats of sixty a push, which makes the rest the bulk of the score, so the size of the first wave matters little once the bank is safe (that is the paper's own emphasis; a reader who wants the first-wave arithmetic to count more can lower `FLEET`); (d) the feet a shot needs are withheld, and a stopped shot goes in half a foot less than it needs (`CONFIG.PEN_SHORT`), so one successful push gives the exact answer and one failed push gives only a lower bound; (e) the translation's web page could not be reached from the build session, so About and the memo cite the site without a checked address, and the game does not claim permission to quote; (f) mortars, secrecy, the bend and the flosses are read on the end screen, not played; (g) the losses are ours in every number: all aboard when a cannonball goes through, one in six under musket fire, nobody when an overloaded boat settles at the bank; a replayed crossing keeps its dead.

Revisions 1 to 5 (24–25 November) are in the memo: bigger type and the rule on the sheet; a push one step at a time in plain words, the general's boat first, white paper; the feet withheld and the drawings reworked; the dead counted and a lost day replayable; the sheet streamlined. Revision 6 (25 November, midday) rebuilt the sheet as one drawing, the river in profile, cut the bend so that there are two crossings, merged the frames to three or four a push, and made the title block follow the frames instead of running ahead of them (memo, Revision 6; `illustration-notes.md`, postscript 2). The revision-5 build and its sources are in `previous/`.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and three scripted browser sessions at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured; from the sessions, three to five minutes is the guess.
