# Leibniz's Flatboat — note for the author

1. Look first at the general's boat, `screenshot-a-0-general.png` and its frames `screenshot-a-0-2-fire.png`, `-3-through.png`, `-4-back.png`; then the shot that sticks with its depth on the heap, `screenshot-a-1b-p2-3-stuck.png`, the wall with the crouching figures, `-5-wall.png`, the rest waiting, `-6-wait.png`, and the rest crossing in `screenshot-a-2-p1-6-rest.png`; the sinking in `screenshot-a-1-sink-2-sunk.png`; the shallow boat with its two landings in `screenshot-a-3-B.png`; and `screenshot-a-end.png`. Then play it on a phone without knowing the feet: the thing to judge is whether the depth drawn on the heap is enough to learn from, and whether the gamble between too much earth and too little feels like a decision.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the weights (a man a tenth of a tun, a gun two, a foot of wall two), what stops what (one foot a musket ball, two a light ball, four a six-pounder), the pushes in a day, the fleet and its open boats, the two hulls, the three crossings with their landings and fire, and the fates. Every number there is ours: the paper gives the carrying rule and no quantity. Change one and run `node calibration.js`: it searches every loading and push sequence for the best in each crossing, runs nine ways of playing against them, and re-runs the lessons with the lines moved. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the feet of earth a cannonball needs are on the light side of period field works and were chosen so that the big hull carries the wall, a cannon and twenty soldiers exactly (8 + 2 + 2 = 12) and the shallow one cannot carry the wall and a cannon with anyone aboard; the earth-then-cannon lesson falls if the wall no longer fits with a cannon (the calibration says so at five feet); (b) "one cannon behind earth drives the enemy cannons off an open shore" is our reading of Leibniz's "even dislodging the enemy", not a rule he states; (c) the rest cross as five ordinary boats of sixty a push, which makes the rest the bulk of the score, so the size of the first wave matters little once the bank is safe (that is the paper's own emphasis, but a reader who wants the first-wave arithmetic to count more can lower `FLEET`); (d) the feet a shot needs are withheld since revision 3 and a stopped shot goes in half a foot less than it needs (`CONFIG.PEN_SHORT`), so one successful push gives the exact answer and one failed push gives only a lower bound; whether that is the right amount of help is the thing to judge by playing; (e) the translation's web page could not be reached from the build session, so About and the memo cite the site without a checked address, and the game does not claim permission to quote; (f) mortars, secrecy, the bend and the flosses are read on the end screen, not played; (g) since revision 4 the losses are ours in every number: all aboard when a cannonball goes through, one in six under musket fire, nobody when an overloaded boat settles at the bank; and a replayed crossing keeps its dead, which is what makes a replay a decision rather than a free retry.

Revision 1 (same day) answered the author's first two comments: larger type everywhere, a wider page on laptops, and the rule that decides a push stated on the sheet while loading. Revision 2 (same day) answered the next: a push plays one step at a time with a sentence each and a Next button; plain words (cannonball, cannons, soldiers, tons); the general's own boat of sixty soldiers pushed off first, so the first failure is his; the crew and hands rules gone; and the sheet on near-white paper in blue ink. Revision 3 (25 November) answered "too easy" and "weak illustrations": the feet of earth a shot needs are withheld and measured from the detail view, and the drawings were reworked from `illustration-notes.md`. Revision 4 (same day) withdrew the no-deaths constraint at the author's request: a cannonball through sinks the boat with everyone aboard and ends the day, musket fire through costs one in six, a lost day can be played again with the dead carried, and the verdict counts soldiers lost and attempts (memo, Revisions 1 to 4).

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px, two sessions (a learning stranger and the paper's loading throughout). It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured; from the session, three to six minutes is the guess.
