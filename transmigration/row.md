| **Beam Me Up, Leibniz** | Leibniz (c. 1702–1705), *On Unities and Transmigration*, LBr 40 Bl. 22–23, in Lloyd Strickland's translation (2016). Can a soul move from one body to another? As a 1960s space-age transfer station: pods in the dark, a pilot in each, a porthole on every pod showing the whole fleet from where it is, and two tapes under the pad, the pod's log and the pilot's, running in step with no wire between them. Three keys for the dog in pod A: burn (a pod under every layer, past a million; the fire scorches the outside), swap quietly (every porthole and every log the same, so the station's book writes nothing, and a swap it cannot write is no swap), swap with a mark (the lamp, a roll, a new course: the sign goes into pod B's log and the log explains it from the line before). Then you: a stick attached to nothing, and a beam whose one sign the second book records is a memory, so the far pilot is, morally, you, and you are still here. Book your passage by beam or by pod; a verdict in words. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/transmigration/beam-me-up-leibniz.html) · [memo](transmigration/memo.md) |
