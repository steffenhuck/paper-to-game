# Beam Me Up, Leibniz — note for the author

1. Look first at the quiet swap's before-and-after portholes, `screenshot-a-1-quietcat-2-windows.png`, then the mark's arrow on the tape, `screenshot-a-1-marklamp-3-arrow.png`, the burn with its counter, `screenshot-a-1-burn100-2-peel.png`, the stick out, `screenshot-a-2-stick-out.png`, the memory's two bubbles, `screenshot-a-2-beammemory-5-speakA.png`, and the three endings, `screenshot-a-end.png` (by beam), `screenshot-b-end.png` (by pod), `screenshot-c-end.png` (beam and burn). Then play it on a phone: the thing to judge is whether the three keys feel like trying something or like being shown something, and whether the moment the second book opens and your own pod still has you in it lands as the text's hedge rather than as a joke.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the five rules as the screen states them, the fleet with its pilots and positions, the two logs and their causes, the three signs and the memory with their causes, the burn depths and the counter's cap, the three bookings, and Leibniz's lines in the game's words. Every number there is ours: the text has none. Change one and run `node calibration.js`: it checks the exchange invariant on the configured fleet and on two hundred random fleets with random logs, that every log line follows from an earlier line of the same log after every mark, the burns, the diamond, the second book, the stick, the bookings, the shifts, and the words. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the duplicate: the text says a life could be "reborn elsewhere" that is morally me, and does not say the original remains; the game shows two pilots claiming one life, which follows from the text's own rules (no body destroyed, nothing leaves a unity) but is our reading, and About says so; (b) the quiet swap fails on screen for the text's reason (nothing shown, so nothing written, so "contrary to order") and also for a drawn one (the porthole is the point of view, which belongs to the position), and a reader may find the second reason does the work the text gives to the first; (c) the three signs and their causes are ours, as the "means" the text asks for and does not list; (d) the memory in shift 2 is both mechanically caused (the first book) and morally recorded (the second), which is the text's "the mechanical laws themselves would make reborn elsewhere a life which continues my life", but the two books on one screen may be one book too many for a stranger; (e) the booking is unranked by design (the text says "possible" against "more in keeping with order"), so the game ends without a score and the verdict carries the weight; (f) the translation's web address could not be checked from the build session.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px, three sessions (a learning stranger with every setting tried, the text's reader, and beam-and-burn). It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured; from the sessions, four to six minutes is the guess.
