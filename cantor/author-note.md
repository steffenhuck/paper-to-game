# Not in the Book — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-round2-first-tap-striking.png` (the endless book striking and about to refill), then `screenshot-round3-midway.png` (Cantor's line, rows dying on their number) and `screenshot-end-rerun.png` (the verdict and the rule running on its own against a book that now contains your row).
2. Everything tunable is in the `CONFIG` block at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the paper's two characters and its three example rows, the widths and row counts of the three books, the number of letters in round 2, the seed range. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (22 checks against the paper's construction and the game's framing), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to finish if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the reactive Lister. The paper's list is fixed in advance; the game's Lister turns to rows further down (round 2) and copies you (round 3), as a dramatisation of "the book has everything", and the About screen says so. (b) Whether round 3 is too easy once the bargain card has been read: the calibration shows that the round-1 habit of striking the rows still alive coincides with Cantor's rule there, so the discovery is "the trick I already had is the proof", and only a player who ignores the page slips. (c) The wording "more rows than whole numbers" and "some infinities are bigger than others" as the plain form of "M does not have the power of the number series".

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and the three-minute length is an estimate.
