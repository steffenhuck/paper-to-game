The Great Lister has a book that never ends. Every line of it is a row of two letters, m and w, and the rows never end either. He says: whatever row you write, I already have it in my book. Somewhere.

Write a row of m's and w's that is not in his book.

Cantor's bargain, 1891

You cannot strike out all the rows at once. So stop trying.

Take them one at a time. Your 1st letter deals with row 1. Your 2nd letter deals with row 2. Your 3rd with row 3. And so on. Then every row is struck out by the time you reach its number, and it no longer matters how many rows there are: you never have to be finished.

How? Look at row 1's 1st letter and write the other one. Row 1 is struck out. Look at row 2's 2nd letter and write the other one. Row 2 is struck out. The letters you have to look at run in a line from corner to corner. We colour that line teal.

The Lister agrees, on one condition: he writes each row only when you get to it, so he can copy what you have written so far. He thinks that will save him.

The Lister's last try

The Lister tries once more. He puts your row into his book as row 1 and moves every other row down one. "Now it is in the book," he says. Watch the rule run on its own.

Georg Cantor, Über eine elementare Frage der Mannigfaltigkeitslehre (On an elementary question of the theory of manifolds), 1891. The two letters m and w, and the first three rows of the first book, are his. He went on, in the half of the paper this game leaves out, to show that the same trick works for any collection at all: there is no biggest infinity.

How to play

Write a row of m's and w's that is not in the Lister's book.

The Lister's book is a list of rows. Every row is made of two letters, m and w, and goes on for ever; the page shows the first six or ten letters of each. Your own row is the gold band underneath. Tap the m ticket or the w ticket to write your next letter.

Striking out a row

A row matches you while every letter you have written is the same as its letter in the same place. The moment one of your letters is different from the row's letter in that place, the row is struck out: it greys out, a line runs through it, and the letter that did it gets a ring. A struck-out row cannot be your row. Your row is "not in the book" once every row is struck out.

Round 1: the small book

Five rows, six letters each. Strike out all five before your six letters run out. Tip: look at the rows that still match you, and pick the letter that strikes out more of them.

Round 2: the endless book

The same game, but the book never ends. Every time you strike out rows, the Lister turns to rows further down that still match you. Write all six letters; then he has a word. Nobody wins this round. That is the point.

Round 3: Cantor's bargain

Ten rows, ten letters, a teal line from corner to corner. Your 1st letter deals with row 1, your 2nd with row 2, and so on: look at the letter on the teal line and write the other one. The Lister writes each row only when you reach it, copying what you have written so far, so the letter on the line is the only one that can strike the row out in time. If you write the same letter instead, the row slips: it stays in the book, still matching you, unless a later letter happens to strike it out.

The end

You see what your row proves, and the Lister gets one last try.
