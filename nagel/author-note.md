# Nagel’s Camera — note for the author (revision 7)

1. Look first at `screenshot-n-1-built.png` and `screenshot-n-1-graded.png` (a window built and settled), `screenshot-a-end-3.png` (the verdict's third step); then at `screenshot-a-3-red-0.png` through `screenshot-a-3-red-100.png` (the red leaving the poppies, the ring of colours, the field shrinking to the eye and the wave filling the frame), then `screenshot-a-1-sunset-75.png` and `-100.png` (the earth as a ball), `screenshot-a-3-red-means.png` (the What that means panel), `screenshot-a-evidence-red.png` beside `screenshot-a-evidence-bat.png` (the mirror: the unread frame full for red, blank for the bat), `screenshot-a-end-5.png` (the stepped debrief, fully revealed) and `screenshot-c-2-lightning-eye-sent.png` (an unreadable print). Then play it on a phone: the thing to judge is the ring under the thumb, whether the motion carries the argument before any words do, and whether the snap to a detent feels like a camera.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: how much of a thing each frame holds (`HOLDS`), who has which senses, each reader's reply, the "what left the picture" lines (`LEFT`), the closing question and answers. Change a number and run `node calibration.js`; then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch. The moving pictures are in `template.html` (`SCENES`: one `markup` and one `apply(svg, z)` per thing; `z` is the ring from 0 to 1); the labelled drawings for What that means and the prints are `FRAMES` in the same file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the middle stop of the sunset is the weakest picture; (b) the lightning physics is small at phone width; (c) the closing question is gone (revision 4): the three readings of the blank are the verdict's last step, read and not chosen; (d) pain is cut from play and lives in the verdict, including the machine question; if it is missed, a fifth round with the physiologist is a scene away; (e) the evidence panels show drawings at half width, where labels are small; (f) the second half rests on bat biology from general references, not consulted page by page: the sly calls (the sun's disc out, the white crack in as a bare flash, the dark coming in) are the ones to check with a bat person; (g) the settled window is pretty rather than informative, and the list under it does the explaining.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px, three strategies, one real drag. Steffen played the first build and revisions 1 to 6; nobody has played this one. The verdict's opening sentences have been generated and checked for every path, which is not the same as having been read for every path: the 27 first-half sentences and twenty night sentences are printed in `calibration-result.txt` to read. Its length has not been measured.
