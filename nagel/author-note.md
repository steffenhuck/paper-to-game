# Zoom Out with Nagel — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-3-red-shape.png` and `screenshot-a-3-red-physics.png` (round 3, the collision: the only frames Ada can read are the ones the red has left), then `screenshot-b-5-pain-physics-sent.png` (the physiologist gets the whole brain and none of the pain) and `screenshot-a-6-bat-inside.png` (the blank frame). Then play it on a phone; the thing to judge is whether turning the ring and watching the colour drain out of the frame carries the argument on its own, before the rule under the print says it.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: how much of a thing each frame holds (`HOLDS`), who has which senses (`ROUNDS[k].reader.has`), what each frame says, every reader's reply, the closing question and answers. Change a number and run `node calibration.js`; then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch. The frames themselves are drawn in `template.html` (`FRAMES`).
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the game is short, six sends and a question, and round 4 (the trumpet to Ada) and round 5 (the pain) each carry one lesson; if a player finds the ring obvious after round 3, the fix is more readers at intermediate distances (a dog, a person deaf from birth), not more rounds of the same; (b) the shape frames are the game's own attempt at Nagel's objective phenomenology, and a philosopher may think they smuggle in more than structure ("the colour of blood" is a cross-sense association he would not count); (c) the closing question is a bet-and-card close with three answers, and the verdict's "had yours stood" is where the paper's second half is read; (d) the pain round's reader is a Martian, so that the complete reader is also one who cannot read from inside.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px, three strategies. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured.
