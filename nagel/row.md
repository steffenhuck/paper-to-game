| **Nagel’s Camera** | Nagel (1974), *The Philosophical Review* 83(4):435–450. What is it like to be a bat? As a 1972 instant camera whose zoom ring steps back from your own eyes: the ring turns freely and the picture changes under your thumb, the colour draining, things leaving the frame and other things coming in, and the shutter fires at three stops (naked eye, the shape, the physics). Three readers, two without your senses, ask about three things from the paper. Zoom out on lightning for the eyeless Martian and the bolt stays in the middle and gains the cloud, the ground and the charge; zoom out on red for Ada, blind from birth, and the red leaves the poppies before any frame she can read arrives. Then night falls and the camera turns on a bat: three windows, and under each a tray of pieces of your own evening, storm and field; tap the ones a bat has, and the window settles into the shape of the bat's evening, echoes and loud waves and warmth, the colour fading out. The verdict sets your window beside the frame one stop further in, which is blank, and ends with the three ways to read it.
