# Ostrom's Pond — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-1-result.png` (a talk: ten buckets, the pond count, "nobody knows who"), then `screenshot-a-3-result.png` (the first watched summer: the watcher names whom she saw, buckets for the rest), then `screenshot-a-7-result.png` (the occasional policeman: only the caught are named, "that's his job now"), then `screenshot-a-end.png` and `screenshot-b-end.png` (the seventh summer revealed, after watching and after six summers of the daily policeman). Then play it once on a phone; `#v=1234` on the URL replays that village. Revision 8 restored the game as it stood at revision 4 and cut it down: six summers, one honest policeman who costs nothing, the watcher one of us with good eyes; revisions 5 to 7 (hunger counts, the policeman's pay in people, the red flag) are undone and recorded in the memo.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the type shares, the pond's breeding, the choice weights (temptation, reciprocity, base reluctance, noise), the five arrangements (promise pull, shame, assurance, the watcher's chance of seeing, the believed chance before and after a telling-off, the graduated fines and what a sanction teaches, the town's fine, the two policemen's chances and fees), the winter (imitation, reinforcement, being suckered, grabbing, crowding out under each policeman), and the newcomer. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (43 checks, one sensitivity line, and the play-it-in-numbers table), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to build if the embedded mechanism differs from the file. `node sweep-test.js 8` plays eight villages with random cards in a headless browser and checks, after every summer, that what the game says agrees with what it draws and records.
3. The player sees only what the village meeting could know (revision 3): the pond count always, names only from the watcher or a catch; `sweep-test.js` checks after every summer that no hidden name leaks into the text or the drawing.

4. What we were least sure about: (a) the whole thing is a toy, and its sizes are ours: the paper gives directions and reported numbers from other studies, not a model; (b) the daily policeman's seventh summer falls below leave-it-be's only if crowding out runs at least three-quarters of the rate chosen (the sensitivity line in `calibration-result.txt`); the verdict screen says the result depends on how fast a norm withers; (c) the paper's "worst of all worlds", weak outside monitoring, is not played: it was a second policing card in a game about self-governance, and was cut; (d) the pond's rule is ours and deliberately checkable (each fish left can have one baby, food for ten a winter, room for fifty), so one each holds the pond and every fish beyond that is a fish short next summer; under "have a talk" the egoists eat double and the pond pays for it, ending about half full; (e) the newcomer (the paper's in-migration threat) is in the mechanism but switched off, `MIGRATION_AFTER: 0`, as a complication a six-summer game does not need; (f) every sanction is in kind (a word; both fish back into the pond; both fish back and a summer out; both fish to the policeman), so that the hands in the row always show what was eaten; the deterrent sizes are therefore two fish and a summer's fishing, not a tariff.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length is an estimate.
