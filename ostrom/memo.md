# Ostrom's Pond — design memo

## 0. Given

Mine: Steffen's signature and counter. Author title: *Ostrom's Pond*. One idea, given in the first reply: "now is finally a good moment to have childlike drawings in the pantheon." Taken literally: not a picture book in the manner of an illustrator (Coase's Cows, Bruna) and not a seven-year-old's crayons on sugar paper drawn by an adult who has seen a lot of them (Me and My Friends), but the drawing a child actually makes of their own village, felt-tip on the squared paper of a school exercise book, with the names written underneath in a child's hand. Where this overrode precedent: the register family (Drawn) is the family of two of the last three games, which section 5 would forbid; the Given outranks it, and everything else about the chassis differs (section 6).

## 1. Paper

Elinor Ostrom (2000), "Collective Action and the Evolution of Social Norms", *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 14(3): 137–158. doi:10.1257/jep.14.3.137. Open access at https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.14.3.137. The upload was named "2010" (a reprint); the text is the 2000 JEP article. Only the typeset PDF was available; no code, no data. The paper is a survey and an argument, not a model or an experiment of its own: every number in it is reported from other work and is used here as reported.

## 2. What the paper hands over

**Primary: an argument.** A verbal theory of collective action with three types of player: *rational egoists*, *conditional cooperators* (who contribute if they expect enough others to, and reduce their contributions when disappointed, which without communication or sanctions produces a downward cascade), and *willing punishers* (who pay to rebuke or sanction free riders). Norms are intrinsic additions to objective payoffs (the indirect evolutionary approach of Güth and Kliemt): preferences adapt to the material success of the types around them, so norm-users survive only where their type can be told apart from egoists, at least noisily. External rules crowd norms out: strong external monitoring enforces cooperation with no norms behind it, no external rules let norms evolve, and a mild degree of external monitoring is "the worst of all worlds". Long-surviving self-governed commons are run by their own users with their own monitors and *graduated* sanctions, the first of which is so small it works as information, not deterrence.

**Secondary: estimates, all reported from other papers.** The seven lab facts (contributions of 40–60 percent in a one-shot game or first round; decay afterwards but well above zero; over 70 percent contribute nothing in an announced last round; believers in others' cooperation cooperate more; experience raises cooperation, with decay inversely related to the number of rounds; face-to-face communication raises cooperation substantially and sustains it to the last period; subjects pay to punish below-average contributors, including in the last period). Ahn, Ostrom and Walker: 40 percent of 136 subjects rank mutual cooperation above exploiting a cooperator, 27 percent indifferent; after twelve rounds of a repeated prisoner's dilemma with random partners, 19 percent and 17 percent. Cain: "nice" players cooperate 69 percent of the time with other nice players and 39 percent with stingy ones. Frohlich and Oppenheimer: the group given an external incentive-compatible mechanism earned more while it ran and cooperated less than the control group once it was removed. Bardhan's 48 irrigation systems: worse maintenance where the rules are seen as an elite's, frequent violations and lower contributions where a government agency allocates the water. Ray and Williams: the loss from water theft on government-owned systems in Maharashtra approaches a quarter of the revenue an efficient regime could earn.

## 3. The discovery, and its kind

**A counterintuitive result**, twice over. First: the zero-contribution thesis says self-interested people will not provide a public good, and they do, about half, and learning the game makes them cooperate longer, not less. Second, and the one this game is built around: the obvious remedy, rules and policing from outside, is not neutral. It works while it is there, it costs, it stops the village's own norms from growing, and a little of it is worse than none. The remedy that lasts is the village watching itself with sanctions that start as a word.

Underneath both is **a mechanism made intelligible**: three types, a cascade, and norms that strengthen or wither by what pays.

## 4. The core narrative

A village has a pond with fish in it. Every summer everyone takes fish, and over the winter the fish have babies. If everyone takes one fish there are always fish. Some people take two. When the others see that, they stop bothering too, and soon the pond is empty. The village can do different things about it: say nothing, sit in a circle and promise, take turns watching the pond and telling off whoever is greedy, or ask the town to send a policeman, now and then or every day. The policeman works, as long as he is there and you pay him. But in the summer when nobody is watching, the village that had the policeman grabs everything, and the village that watched itself and told each other off still takes one fish each. Looking after yourselves is slow, and it is the only thing that sticks.

## 5. Who lives in the paper

- **The rational egoist** — controls how much to take; anticipates the chance of being caught and the fine. A candidate player: the obvious one, and the dullest, because the paper says he is a third of the room at most.
- **The conditional cooperator** — controls how much to take; anticipates what the others will take and whether anyone is watching the rest. A candidate player: the paper's majority type, the one who gets suckered and cascades.
- **The willing punisher** — controls whether to pay to rebuke or sanction; anticipates whether a word will do or a fine is needed. A candidate player: the paper's hero, and a rare position.
- **The users as rule-makers** (design principle 3: most of those affected can participate in making and modifying the rules) — control who watches, how sanctions escalate, whether to turn to outside help. A candidate player: Ostrom's own subject.
- **The external authority** — controls the strength of monitoring and the fine. A candidate player: the planner, the position the paper argues against.
- **The experimenter** — controls communication, sanctions, the number of rounds, whether the end is announced. The lab's seven findings are their results.

## 7. The aim and the verdict

**Aim**, as the player reads it: *Keep the pond alive and the village fed for ten summers, and leave villagers who share when nobody is looking.*

**Yardstick during play**, in the aim's units: the fish in the pond, drawn and countable, and a word for its state (full, half, low, empty); the fish eaten this summer, drawn in the villagers' hands, and the village's eaten fish so far as bones on the shelf; and the plain statement, each summer, of how many of the ten took one fish and who took two. The third part of the aim is hidden until the eleventh summer by design: a norm is what people do when nobody checks.

**Verdict**, the lines the end screen leads with:

1. *The summer nobody watched: N of ten still took one fish.* (and, if the pond is empty, *There was nothing left to share.*)
2. *After ten summers the pond is full / half full / nearly empty / empty, and the village ate well / thinly / went hungry.*
3. What the paper says about the path taken, in one or two sentences: for the policeman every day, that cooperation enforced from outside vanished the moment it was removed and left the village worse off than one that never had it; for the policeman now and then, that mild outside monitoring is the worst of all worlds; for leaving it be, the cascade; for the talking circle, that cheap talk works, and that few long-surviving commons rely on trust alone; for taking turns watching, that this is how the commons that lasted for generations were run, with the first sanction a word.

No score, no best path, no "could have done": the paper sets a village's own rules against outside rules and sides with the former on survival, so the verdict says what became of the pond, the bellies and the norm on this path, and the player judges.

## 8. Three concepts

**A. The Meeting** (pick). *Position:* Agent, the village at its summer meeting. *Format:* Strategic, ten summers. *Control:* one, each summer, chosen from five arrangements drawn as cards: leave it be; have a talk; take turns watching; ask the town for a policeman now and then; ask the town for a policeman every day. Touching a card draws the arrangement into the village picture (a circle of heads, a watcher on a stool, a policeman by the pond) and says in two lines what it costs and what happens to someone greedy; nothing about the outcome. *Deliberation:* "the pond is falling: do I bring the policeman in, and what does that do to us?" and "the watching is slow and costs a fish a summer: is it working?" *After:* the villagers fish, one at a time; the greedy ones hold two fish; a watcher wags a finger or the policeman grabs a sleeve; the pond is redrawn; the game says who took what. *Discovery moment:* the eleventh summer, nobody watching: the village that was policed grabs, the village that watched itself shares. Before that, the mild policeman's summer, when the villagers stop telling each other off. *Fun:* anticipation (the fishing plays out one villager at a time), agency (the arrangement visibly changes who grabs), discovery (the eleventh summer). *Second playthrough:* a different sequence (police first, then self-governance; talk throughout; nothing until the pond falls) gives a different pond, a different norm, and a different paper sentence.

**B. One Fisher** (runner-up). *Position:* Subject, one conditional cooperator among nine villagers. *Format:* Strategic. *Controls:* each summer, one fish or two; from summer four, a second control, whether to spend a fish telling off a grabber. *Deliberation:* "they took two last summer; do I keep taking one and be the fool?" *After:* the row redrawn, the pond redrawn. *Discovery:* the cascade from the inside, and that one scolding stops a grabber for a summer. *Fun:* agency, discovery. *Second playthrough:* be the grabber. Fails the paper's second discovery: there is no policeman, so the crowding out is never played.

**C. The Watcher** (reckless). *Position:* Agent, a willing punisher. *Format:* Strategic, in real time, with no words on the play screen. *Control:* tap a villager while they are at the pond to wag a finger at them; each wag costs you a fish; a second wag at the same villager is a fine; a third sends them home. *Deliberation:* perception and timing: who is taking two, and is a word enough? *After:* the villagers' behaviour next summer; by summer eight you hardly need to wag. *Discovery:* that the first sanction works as information, and that the norm grows until monitoring is nearly free. *Fun:* perception, mastery, agency. *Second playthrough:* a village with more egoists. Gives up the policeman, so gives up the crowding out, and a real-time tap game on a phone with ten small figures fights the "fewer, bigger things" rule.

## 10. The pick, and why

**A, The Meeting.** It puts the player where Ostrom put her whole career, in the seat of the users who make their own rules, and it hands them the paper's own menu, including the tempting wrong answers. Both discoveries land inside play: the mild policeman's summer, when the villagers stop telling each other off, and the eleventh summer, when the policed village grabs. The deliberation is real: the pond falls, the policeman is there to be hired, and the stranger has to decide whether to use him knowing only that he costs and that he works. The sentence afterwards has no "guess": *I had a talk and three of them grabbed anyway; I set the village watching and the grabbers were told off, then fined, then stopped; I hired the policeman and the pond was full till the summer nobody watched.* Originality: no game in the table has had the player choose a governance regime for a population of hidden types, and none has made the verdict a round the player does not play.

**Author test.** Ostrom would recognise every card: the lab baseline, Ostrom and Walker's communication treatment, her own design principles 4 and 5 as the watching and the graduated sanction, the Cardenas–Stranlund–Willis weak external enforcement, and Frohlich and Oppenheimer's removed mechanism as the eleventh summer. What she would enjoy is watching a stranger hire the policeman when the pond falls, see it work, and then meet the eleventh summer.

**Runner-up: B, One Fisher.** Switch if the author wants the cascade felt in the player's own hands rather than seen in the row, and accepts losing the policeman, which is to say the second half of the paper.

**What the reckless concept would have given.** The first sanction as a thing the player's own finger does, and the discovery that monitoring gets cheaper as the norm grows. Kept: the wagging finger drawn on the watcher, the graduated schedule stated on the card and played out in the row, and the result line that says "nothing else happened to them". Given up: the player's finger doing it, real time, and the wordless screen.

## 11. The shape

One village, ten summers, one decision each, and an eleventh summer that plays itself; then the verdict. Screens: start; the play screen, which is one page redrawn (the pond, the row, the shelf, the cards, the button; after the commit the cards give way to the result and a "next summer" button); the eleventh summer, the same page with no cards; the verdict; How to play and About as overlays. No presets: the five cards are the paper's regimes (the lab baseline; communication; local monitoring with graduated sanctions; weak external enforcement; strong external enforcement), and the player orders them. The types are drawn from a visible seed (three egoists, five conditional cooperators, two willing punishers among the ten names), so a replay has different grabbers in the row but the same counts. Five to six minutes, by the walkthrough; unmeasured.

## 12. Controls, mechanism, feedback, moment

**Control.** One: the arrangement for this summer, five cards, none preselected, each summer from the same place. Touching a card redraws the village (the preview of what the arrangement looks like, not what it does to the catch) and writes the card's two lines: what it costs us, and what happens to someone seen taking two. FISH! commits.

**Mechanism** (toy; section 16). Ten villagers of three hidden types. Each takes one fish or two. The chance of taking one is a logistic in the difference between the temptation (the second fish) and the sum of: the villager's norm strength times what they expect of the others (reciprocity) plus a base reluctance to grab; the expected sanction under this summer's arrangement (believed chance of being seen times the fine for the next offence); shame, if someone will rebuke them and they have a norm to be ashamed against; and assurance, under local watching, that someone is checking the others. Egoists start with no norm. Over the winter, norms move: toward the kind that ate better (the imitation of the successful that the indirect evolutionary approach rests on), up a little for cooperating in a cooperating village, down for being suckered, and down every summer a policeman is in charge (crowding out). The pond breeds logistically; takes are rationed when it is low; fines paid under local watching go back into the pond; the town's fines and fees leave the village.

**Feedback.** The reveal: after FISH!, each villager in turn goes to the pond and comes back holding one fish or two, the fish leaving the pond one at a time; a watcher's wagging finger or the policeman's hand on a sleeve is drawn on the villager it happened to; then the pond is redrawn after the winter; the game states the summer in three plain sentences; the shelf grows by what was eaten.

**Moment.** The eleventh summer. The cards are gone; the line says nobody is watching and nobody is talking; the villagers go to the pond one at a time; and the row shows what the village has become.

## 14. What is cut

The announced last round and its 70 percent zero contributions (the villagers do not know the game ends). Group size, heterogeneity, boundary rules (design principle 1), proportional cost-sharing (2), conflict-resolution arenas (6), recognition of the right to organise (7), nested enterprises (8), the threats other than migration (migration is in: after five summers a norm-user moves away and a newcomer who has never fished here moves in, an egoist, which is our reading of the paper's words about in-migrants who do not trust and do not rapidly learn the norms). The dictator-then-prisoner's-dilemma signalling (Cain). The deontic-reasoning psychology. The trust game and the Güth–Kliemt information regimes in their own right: they appear only as the imitation rule that moves norms toward whoever ate better. Rewards for over-contributors. The leader or entrepreneur.

## 15. Divergences

- **Clock:** a summer is a round; the lab's ten to sixty rounds and the field's generations are both compressed into ten summers plus one.
- **The resource:** a pond that breeds logistically is ours; the lab is a linear public good and the field is irrigation, fisheries and forests. A fish is the unit of everything so that the one visual rule can hold.
- **Types and shares:** three egoists, five conditional cooperators, two willing punishers in ten is our reading of Ahn, Ostrom and Walker's 40 percent strict norm-users, 27 percent indifferent and the rest; the paper gives shares of rankings, not a census of types.
- **Payoffs:** one fish against two, fines of one and three fish to the pond under local watching, a flat three to the town under a policeman, a fee of one fish for the occasional policeman and a quarter of the catch for the daily one (the size is borrowed from Ray and Williams's quarter, which is a loss from theft, not a fee; declared as ours), the watcher's day at one fish: all ours.
- **The policeman:** the paper's external authority imposes rules; here the village asks for him, because the player is the village. The paper's threat 4 is turning to external help too often, so the seat is defensible.
- **The eleventh summer:** Frohlich and Oppenheimer's second phase, played as a summer with no arrangement; in the lab the removal was the experimenters' choice, here it is the game's.
- **Presentation:** ten people, fifty fish, named villagers, a sun with a face.

## 16. Mechanism kind and claim boundaries

**Kind: toy.** The paper hands over an argument with reported numbers from other studies; the game runs a model built to illustrate that argument, with its directions and rough sizes anchored to those numbers.

- **Demonstrates:** that a population of the paper's three types, with norms that adapt to what pays and wither under outside enforcement, produces the paper's pattern: about half cooperate with nothing in place and it decays; talk raises it and holds it; local watching with graduated sanctions brings grabbers into line and leaves a village that shares unwatched; a little outside policing is worse than none; a lot works while it lasts and leaves less behind than never having had it.
- **Assumes:** the toy: the logistic choice, the reciprocity and shame weights, the sanction schedule and beliefs about being seen, the imitation and crowding-out rates, the pond's biology, the shares of types, the fees. None of these is in the paper.
- **The paper establishes:** the seven lab regularities as reported from the literature; the field finding that user-made and user-enforced rules with graduated sanctions characterise long-surviving regimes; the Frohlich–Oppenheimer crowding-out result and its cousins; and an argument, not a theorem, that an evolutionary approach with multiple types explains them. It does not establish a "worst of all worlds" quantitatively (the paper says "may be"), and the game's weak-policeman card is tuned to be worse than leaving it be by a little, not by a lot.

Inside the game the writing says "in this village" and "under this game's rules", never "if Ostrom is right". The About screen carries the three lines above.

**Which discoveries follow from the paper's mechanism and which from design.** The cascade under *leave it be*: the paper's (conditional cooperators disappointed). Talk raising cooperation without changing the egoists: the paper's (cheap talk, promises, tongue-lashings; egoists unmoved). The graduated schedule bringing grabbers into line, with the first sanction a word that works as information: the paper's. The weak policeman being worse than nothing: the paper's claim, with the mechanism (crowding out, punishers standing down, low risk of being caught) the paper's and the size ours. The daily policeman's eleventh summer being worse than *leave it be*'s: the paper's (Frohlich and Oppenheimer), with the crowding-out rate ours. The daily policeman costing a quarter: ours. The egoists learning the norm under local watching: the paper's imitation argument (Börgers and Sarin, Boyd and Richerson), with the rate ours.

**Robustness.** The calibration check re-runs the mechanism with the crowding-out rate halved, the imitation rate halved, and the type shares changed to four egoists and one punisher, and requires the ordering of the eleventh-summer norm across the five constant strategies (watch ≥ talk > none ≥ weak > daily police) to survive each; see section 17.

## 17. Calibration plan

Against the paper's reported numbers, in Node, on `mechanism.js`, over 400 seeds unless stated:

1. *Leave it be*, summer 1: the share taking one fish lies in 40–60 percent (finding 1).
2. *Leave it be*, summers 1–10: the share decays and stays well above zero (finding 2): summer 10 below summer 1 by at least ten points, and above 15 percent.
3. *Have a talk*: raises the share substantially (finding 5): at least fifteen points above *leave it be* in summer 1 and sustained (summer 10 within ten points of summer 2).
4. *Take turns watching*: the share rises over the summers (finding 6 and design principles 4–5): summer 10 above summer 1 by at least twenty points and above 85 percent; a villager told off once (no fine) is less likely to take two the next summer than one never told off (the first sanction as information).
5. Egoists under *take turns watching* earn less over ten summers than conditional cooperators; under *have a talk* they earn more (the differential that drives the imitation rule); egoists' norm strength is higher at summer 10 under watching than under talk.
6. *A policeman now and then*: the eleventh-summer share is no higher than under *leave it be*, and the ten-summer pond is no fuller (the worst of all worlds, "may be": we require not better, and check that it is worse in the mean).
7. *A policeman every day*: the ten-summer share is above 85 percent (cooperation enforced); the eleventh-summer share is below *leave it be*'s eleventh summer (Frohlich and Oppenheimer); the village's eaten fish are below *take turns watching*'s.
8. The pond: with all ten taking one, the pond never falls below three-quarters full; with all ten taking two, it is empty within six summers; takes are rationed, never negative, and bones equal fish eaten.
