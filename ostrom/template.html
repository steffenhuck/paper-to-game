Ostrom’s Pond

This is our village: ten of us and one pond. The pond has 40 fish in it today, and it has room for 50.

Every summer we all go fishing. The fair share is one fish each. Every winter the fish that are left have babies: each fish can have one baby, but the pond only has food for 10 babies.

So if we all take one, 10 fish come out and 10 babies go in, and the pond stays as it is. But some of us take two. Then more fish come out than go in, and the pond shrinks.

Everyone carries their catch home in a bucket. So unless somebody was at the pond watching, nobody knows who took two.

You are the village meeting: all of us, deciding together. Each summer you pick one card. Every card is a way of putting people off taking two: do nothing, have a talk, take turns watching, or get a policeman from the town. Then everyone goes fishing, and you see what the village can see.

Keep the pond full and everyone fed for six summers. Then comes a seventh summer when nobody is watching. Will they still take one fish each?

How to play

The aim. Keep the pond full and everyone fed for six summers. Then comes a seventh summer when nobody is watching. Will they still take one fish each?

The pond. The blue blob is our pond. Every orange fish in it is a real fish, and the number next to it counts them. We start with 40, and there is room for 50.

The babies. Every winter the fish that are left have babies. Three rules: each fish can have one baby; the pond only has food for 10 babies a winter; and the pond never holds more than 50 fish. So if we all take one, 10 come out and 10 go in, and the pond stays as it is. Every fish we take beyond that makes the pond one fish smaller next summer. Watch out: if the pond drops below 20, even one each is too many, because fewer than ten fish are left to be parents.

The village. The ten stick people are us, with our names under us. Each summer everyone takes one fish or two. One each is fair. Nobody can tell in advance who will take two.

The buckets. Everyone carries their catch home in a bucket. A bucket with a ? on it means nobody saw what was in it. Fish drawn in someone’s hand are what somebody saw them take: the watcher, or the policeman when he caught them. The meeting always finds out how many fish left the pond, so it always knows how many of us took two. It just doesn’t always know who.

The cards. Each summer, touch a card to see what it does. It is drawn into the picture (a circle on the grass, a watcher on a stool, a policeman with a tall hat), and the box with the green line says what it costs, what happens to someone who takes two, and what we get to find out. Leave it be: nothing happens to anyone, and nobody knows who took two. Have a talk: we all promise to take one; if fish go missing, the circle tells everybody off next summer; nobody knows who broke the promise. Take turns watching: one of us watches instead of fishing and sees everyone; seen taking two means a telling-off the first time, both fish back in the pond the second time, and both fish back plus no fishing next summer the third time. Ask the town for a policeman: he stands by the pond every day and catches nearly everyone who takes two; the fish go back in the pond. He tells us only who he caught, and we stop telling each other off.

FISH! Everyone goes to the pond one at a time and comes back with a bucket, or with their fish showing if someone saw them. Under each person it says what happened to them: told off, fish put back, caught, no fishing. Then the pond is counted, the babies come, and the game tells you what the village knows: how many fish came out, how many of us took two, who (if anyone saw), what the pond did, and what we ate. The bones on the shelf are all the fish we have eaten so far.

Next summer. Pick again from all four cards. Nothing is kept from last summer except what the villagers learnt.

The seventh summer. After six summers there is one more, with no card: nobody watches and nobody talks. If the pond is fished out before then, the game ends there instead: with no fish there is nothing to watch and nothing to share. Then the game shows you what really happened, which the village itself never saw, and who never cared about the rule from the start.
