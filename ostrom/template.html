Ostrom’s Pond

This is our village. There are ten of us and one pond. Every summer we all go fishing, and over the winter the fish have babies. A full pond makes about ten new fish a summer: one each. Some of us take two.

You are the village meeting. Each summer you pick what we do about the pond: nothing, a talk, taking turns watching, or a policeman from town. Your hands will touch a card to see what it means, send everyone fishing, and see who took one fish and who took two.

Keep the pond alive and the village fed for ten summers, and leave villagers who share when nobody’s looking.
steffen huck

How to play

The aim. Keep the pond alive and the village fed for ten summers, and leave villagers who share when nobody’s looking.

The pond. The blue blob is our pond; every orange fish in it is a fish, and the number next to it counts them. A full pond holds fifty. Over the winter the fish have babies: a full pond makes about ten new fish, one for each of us. If we take more than that, the pond gets smaller every year. Below about ten fish, even one each is more than it can replace.

The village. The ten stick people are us, with our names under us. Each summer everyone takes one fish or two. One fish each is fair. Nobody can see in advance who will take two.

The cards. Each summer, touch a card to see what it means. It is drawn into the picture (a circle on the grass, a watcher on a stool, a policeman with a tall hat) and the green-lined box says what it costs us and what happens to anyone who takes two. The five cards: leave it be (nothing happens to anyone); have a talk (we promise in a circle, and promise-breakers get a telling-off next summer); take turns watching (one of us watches instead of fishing; seen taking two: a telling-off, then both fish back in the pond, then both fish back and no fishing next summer); a policeman now and then (the town’s policeman on some days, both fish to the town if he catches you, one fish a summer to the town for him, and we stop telling each other off); a policeman every day (the same, every day, for a quarter of our catch).

FISH! Everyone goes to the pond one at a time. Each person comes back holding one fish or two, or nothing if their fish went back into the pond or to the policeman. A telling-off, fish put back or a catch is written under the person it happened to. Then the fish have babies, and the game says what happened: how many took one, who took two, what the pond did, what we ate. The bones on the shelf are the fish we have eaten so far.

Next summer. Pick again from all five cards. Nothing is kept from last summer except what the villagers learnt.

The eleventh summer. After ten summers, one more: no cards, nobody watching, nobody talking. What the village does then is the verdict.
