| **Ostrom's Pond** | Ostrom (2000), *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 14(3): 137–158. Collective action and the evolution of social norms: why people share when the theory says they won't, and why rules from outside can wear away the rules a village makes for itself. As a child's felt-tip drawing in a squared exercise book: ten stick villagers and a pond, and each summer you pick a card for what the village does about it: leave it be, have a talk, take turns watching with a telling-off first and the fish put back after, or ask the town for a policeman. Everyone goes fishing one at a time and comes home with a bucket, so the meeting learns how many fish are gone but a name only from its own watcher or the policeman's catch; the fish have babies. Six summers, then a seventh with no card and nobody watching, which shows what the village has become: the policed village grabs, the village that watched itself still takes one each. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/ostrom/ostroms-pond.html) · [memo](ostrom/memo.md) |
