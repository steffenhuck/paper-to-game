# Leibniz's Register — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-1696-choose.png` (the year that came back from before the register: the memory strip fading left to right, the book with two pages, neither of them this year) and `screenshot-a-1699-result.png` (the crowd after rest harmed, and the game's line that this kind of year came in 1690 and 1691 and nobody wrote it down); then `screenshot-a-1696-register.png` (the register open) and `screenshot-a-end.png` (the verdict, the four doctors, the eleven years). Then play it on a phone; the thing to judge by hand is whether matching the four cards against the book feels like reading a record or like a chore, and whether the two lost years (1696, 1699) bite when you are the one holding the pen. Add `?seed=42` to the address for the draw the screenshots show.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the eleven-year plan (`PLAN`: which kind comes when), the year the register begins, the three counts of the dead (five, twenty, thirty of a hundred), the memory steps, the weather and the lines about harvest and beasts, and how far apart two kinds of year must be. Change a thing and run `node calibration.js`: it draws three hundred games, checks the plan's promises on every one (trusting last year is right at 1691, 1693, 1695, 1698 and loses thirty at 1692, 1696, 1699), and prints the doctors' table. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the plan is dense with returns, so a doctor who trusts last year does about as well over the eleven years as one who gives the same remedy every year (memo §18); the order memory < register from 1694 < register from 1690 holds on every draw and under other sizes and noise, but the sizes are the plan's; (b) a register that the game writes for you: the Given asked for the ledger as the place to look, and the player reads it rather than writing it (the pen was concept C, rejected as a quiz; memo §8); (c) the four years before the letter are always the unwritten ones, so a second play differs in which remedy is which and in the player's strategy, not in where the trap is; (d) the link to Strickland's page (leibniz-translations.com/medicine.htm) could not be reached from the build environment and was taken from the PDF's file name; please check it; (e) the look is 1950s on a 1690s letter, on purpose, and the Isotype crowd is the one picture the rest hangs on.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 px on two seeds and a laptop window. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured.
