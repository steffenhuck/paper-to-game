| **Leibniz's Register** | Leibniz (1694), "On the Way of Perfecting Medicine", *Journal des sçavans*, 26 July 1694, pp. 338–340, in Lloyd Strickland's translation (2019). The same bark cures in one year and fails in the next, years like past years come back, and a doctor with only his own years to go on learns from scratch at the expense of the sick; a few lines a year from every province, collected, would tell him. As a 1950s annual report in cream, charcoal, mustard, teal and tomato: you are the physician of a province for ten winters, 1690 to 1699. Two cards say what kind of winter it is, cold or mild and dry or wet; a hundred Isotype figures are the sick who came; you give them bark, bleeding or rest, and the dead lie down in red. Four winters alone, with nothing but your own past winters in a row of small cards, and the lookalike winter where last winter's remedy kills. Then Leibniz's letter: by order of the King every physician in every province writes a few lines a year, and the collection comes back to you, twelve provinces' winters grouped by kind, with a count of what helped and what harmed. What one doctor sees in a lifetime, twelve see in a year. At the end, your dead before and after the letter, against a doctor with only his own winters, one who read the letters from 1694, and one who had had them from 1690. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/leibniz-medicine/leibnizs-register.html) · [memo](leibniz-medicine/memo.md) · [previous version](leibniz-medicine/previous/memo.md) |
