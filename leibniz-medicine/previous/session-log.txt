a VERDICT: 1700 · THE CENTURY TURNS / What became of / the sick / Of the eleven hundred sick who came to you, 200 died: about one in six. Fewer than memory alone would have lost; more than a register kept from 1690 would have. / What Leibniz says: years like past years come back, and the same bark cures in one and fails in the next; a doctor with only his memory learns it again each time at the expense of the sick, and a register of a few lines a year would have told him. / What you found: four years were the same kind as the year before them, and your head held them; once the year that matched was in your register; twice it was a year nobody had written down, and you learned it again from scratch; four years were new, and nothing could have told you. / Where you part: you lost 200; a doctor with nothing but memory, who repeated what helped last year and otherwise tried another, about 205; a doctor who kept the register from 1694 and read it, about 195; a doctor who had kept it from 1690, as Leibniz wished it had been done "for a while now", about 160; a doctor who knew every year, 55. / What you leave: a register of seven years for the doctor who comes after you.
