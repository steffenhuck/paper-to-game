About

The paper. G. W. Leibniz, "On the Way of Perfecting Medicine": an extract from a letter to Germain Brice, printed in the Journal des sçavans, 26 July 1694, pp. 338–340. Read in Lloyd Strickland's translation from the French (© 2019), published at leibniz-translations.com; the game's words for Leibniz's ideas lean on his. No DOI. Game built from this paper; the paper is the authority where the two disagree.

What the paper says

Leibniz asks whether anyone in Paris is still keeping the registers of baptisms and funerals, and points to the Bills of Mortality in London, from which able people have drawn observations. One could go further: an annual history of medicine for each province, on the plan of Bernardino Ramazzini in Modena, who writes first of the seasons and the constitution of the air, then of how the grains and fruits did and what diseases the animals had, then of the chief matter, the health of human bodies, and above all of which medicines had the best effect, what harms and what helps. In two such years Ramazzini has already seen great variations, "as it were changes from black to white", quinquina (cinchona bark, the remedy for intermittent fevers) succeeding "incomparably better in one year than in the other". And years similar to past ones often return, so the past would be of the greatest use in deciding; "whereas now we are reduced to learning almost always from scratch at the expense of the sick". What a treasure trove there would be if this had been done for a while; but it is always time to begin good things. It costs scarcely any trouble: a letter of a few pages a year from an able practitioner in each province, to the First Doctor of the King. France, with its police, could do it best, and the king would deserve more by it than the kings who gave their names to a few medicines, or than Alfonso and Ulugh Beg by their astronomy. The soul and the body, he ends, are the first things that should be thought about, and the last that are.

What the game does

It gives you the practitioner's year, eleven times. A year has a kind, shown as four season cards and a line about the harvest and the beasts, Ramazzini's first three headings. A hundred sick come with the year's fever and you give them one remedy for the year: bark, bleeding or rest. In every kind of year one remedy helps, one does nothing and one harms, and which is which depends only on the kind; the same bark helps in one kind and harms in another. You see what the remedy you gave did and nothing about the others. Your head holds last year whole, the year before it in part, and little beyond; nobody wrote the years down before you. In 1694 Leibniz's letter arrives and the register begins: from then on every year is written, in Ramazzini's order, and stays. The four years before it are not in it. The plan of the eleven years is fixed and the kinds are drawn fresh each game: a year that repeats, when trusting last year is right; a year of a new kind, when nothing can tell you; a year that came back from before the register, when last year's remedy is the natural one and harms; and at the end a year the register holds. The year that matches this one is sometimes last year, sometimes a page in the book, and sometimes a year nobody wrote down; the game says which after you have acted, never before.

At the end the game sets your eleven years beside three other doctors on the same years: one with nothing but memory, who repeats what helped last year and otherwise tries another; one who kept the register from 1694 and read it; and one who had kept it from 1690, as Leibniz wished it had been done "for a while now". The score, the dead out of eleven hundred sick, is the game's; the unit, the sick, is Leibniz's.

What kind of mechanism this is

Toy. The game runs a model built to illustrate the paper's argument, and where the argument had to be made exact, the toy chose: a year's kind is its seasons; a remedy's effect depends on the kind and on nothing else; five, twenty and thirty of a hundred die under the remedy that helps, does nothing and harms; memory fades in four steps; and the plan of which kind comes when is ours. There is no noise: the same remedy in the same kind of year does the same thing every time.

  • What the game demonstrates: that under these rules a doctor who trusts the remembered year is right when the year repeats and wrong when an older kind returns; that a register finds the older kind when it holds it; and that a register begun late cannot find the years before it. The headless check runs the four doctors over hundreds of draws and finds the same order every time, with other sizes for the dead and with noise added.
  • What the game assumes: everything in the paragraph above. In particular, the plan: in these eleven years a doctor who trusts last year is right four times and wrong three, and so does about as well as one who gives the same remedy every year; the order of the four doctors does not depend on that, but the sizes do.
  • What the paper establishes: an argument, from Ramazzini's two years, that remedies vary from year to year with the constitution of the times, and a claim that similar years often return. It does not establish how remedies map to years, how fast a doctor forgets, or any number in this game. On screen the game says "in this game", never "as Leibniz showed".

What is cut, and where the game departs

The Bills of Mortality and the Paris registers are three lines on the start screen and here. Ramazzini's dedication of his second year to Leibniz, the German doctors "curious about nature" and their Ephemerides, the Lieutenant of Police, Louis XIV, Mithridates, Juba and Gentius, Alfonso and Ulugh Beg are read here, not played. The grains and the beasts are a line of colour, not a variable. No patient is diagnosed: one fever a year, a hundred sick, one remedy. Other provinces are not played; the register is yours alone. The cost of the register, scarcely any, is not a choice: the letter arrives and the register begins. Bark is Leibniz's; bleeding and rest are the game's, so that the choice has three live options. The look is three hundred years after the letter, on purpose.
