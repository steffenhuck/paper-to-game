A province of France · 1690 to 1699

Register

You are the physician of a province. Every winter a fever comes, a hundred sick come to you with it, and you give them one remedy for the winter. The same remedy saves them in one kind of winter and kills them in another, and all that tells the kinds apart is two things: whether the winter is cold or mild, and whether it is dry or wet.

Nobody writes the winters down. In 1694 a letter from Mr Leibniz will change that, for every doctor in the kingdom.

Read the two cards of the winter. Read the letters, once there are any. Give the hundred sick one remedy.

Lose as few of the sick as you can in ten winters: each winter, give the one remedy that fits the kind of winter it is.

The collection

How to play

Lose as few of the sick as you can in ten winters: each winter, give the one remedy that fits the kind of winter it is.

The winter

Two cards show the winter: cold (pale, snowflake) or mild (mustard, sun); dry (red, wind) or wet (teal, drops). That makes four kinds of winter, and the kind is all that matters.

The remedy

Three remedies: bark, bleeding, rest. In every kind of winter one of them helps (five of a hundred die), one does nothing (twenty die), one harms (thirty die). Which is which depends only on the kind of winter, and the same remedy can help in one kind and harm in another. Tap a remedy to see the move, then tap Give it to the hundred sick. You see what the remedy you gave did; you never see what the others would have done.

What you have to go on

Your own past winters, every one, as small cards: the two cards of the winter, what you gave, what came of it. From 1695, the collection: every province's letters for every winter since 1694, grouped by kind of winter, with a count of how often each remedy helped, did nothing or harmed there, and your own winters among them. Tap it to open it and look for your winter's kind.

The end

After 1699 the game says what became of the sick, before the letter and after it, sets your ten winters beside three other doctors (memory alone, the letters from 1694, the letters from 1690, and one who knew every winter), and shows the ten winters as they were. Add ?seed= and a number to the address to replay a draw.

What the paper says

Leibniz asks whether anyone in Paris is still keeping the registers of baptisms and funerals, and points to the Bills of Mortality in London, from which able people have drawn observations. One could go further: an annual history of medicine for each province, on the plan of Bernardino Ramazzini in Modena, who writes first of the seasons and the constitution of the air, then of how the grains and fruits did and what diseases the animals had, then of the chief matter, the health of human bodies, and above all of which medicines had the best effect, what harms and what helps. In two such years Ramazzini has already seen great variations, "as it were changes from black to white", quinquina (cinchona bark, the remedy for intermittent fevers) succeeding "incomparably better in one year than in the other". And years similar to past ones often return, so the past would be of the greatest use in deciding; "whereas now we are reduced to learning almost always from scratch at the expense of the sick". What a treasure trove there would be if this had been done for a while; but it is always time to begin good things. It costs scarcely any trouble: a letter of a few pages a year from an able practitioner in each province, to the First Doctor of the King, by public order. France, with its police, could do it best, and the king would deserve more by it than the kings who gave their names to a few medicines, or than Alfonso and Ulugh Beg by their astronomy. The soul and the body, he ends, are the first things that should be thought about, and the last that are.

What the game does

It gives you the practitioner's winter, ten times. A winter has a kind, and the kind is two things you can see: cold or mild, dry or wet. A hundred sick come with the winter's fever and you give them one remedy: bark, bleeding or rest. In every kind of winter one remedy helps, one does nothing and one harms, and which is which depends only on the kind; the same bark helps in one kind and harms in another. You see what the remedy you gave did and nothing about the others. For four winters you have only your own past winters to go on, and you can see all of them: your memory is not the problem, your sample is. One province meets one winter a year, and a kind you have never had tells you nothing. Then Leibniz's letter arrives, and by public order every physician in every province writes a few lines a year to the First Doctor, who sends the collection back. From 1695 you read, for each kind of winter, what twelve other doctors gave and what came of it; by 1696 the collection holds the remedy that helps in every kind. The hard part dissolves, and that dissolving is what the game is for. The other doctors choose at random, so the collection shows what harmed as well as what helped; the draw is checked so that the letters of 1694 hold the remedy for your 1695 winter and the letters of 1694 and 1695 hold it for every kind.

The plan of your own ten winters is fixed and the kinds are drawn fresh each game: a winter that repeats, when trusting last winter is right; a lookalike, one card the same and the other different, when last winter's remedy harms; a new kind with nothing to read; and from 1695 winters the letters answer. At the end the game sets your ten winters beside three other doctors on the same winters: one with nothing but his own, who repeats what helped last year and otherwise tries another; one who read the letters from 1694; and one who had had letters from 1690, as Leibniz wished it had been done "for a while now". The score, the dead out of a thousand sick, is the game's; the unit, the sick, is Leibniz's.

What kind of mechanism this is

Toy. The game runs a model built to illustrate the paper's argument, and where the argument had to be made exact, the toy chose: a winter's kind is two cards; a remedy's effect depends on the kind and on nothing else; five, twenty and thirty of a hundred die under the remedy that helps, does nothing and harms; twelve other provinces, each with its own winter and a doctor who chooses at random; and the plan of which kind comes to you when. There is no noise: the same remedy in the same kind of winter does the same thing every time, everywhere.

  • What the game demonstrates: that under these rules a doctor with only his own winters is right when the winter repeats and wrong when a lookalike comes; that a collection of every province's winters answers, within a year or two, what one province cannot learn in a decade; and that the collection cannot answer for the winters before it began. The headless check runs the four doctors over three hundred draws and finds the same order every time, with other sizes for the dead and with noise added.
  • What the game assumes: everything in the paragraph above, and in particular that the other doctors go on choosing at random after the letters arrive, so that the collection keeps showing harm as well as help. A doctor with only his own winters does about as well over the ten as one who gives the same remedy every winter; that is the plan's doing, and the order of the four doctors does not depend on it.
  • What the paper establishes: an argument, from Ramazzini's two years, that remedies vary from year to year with the constitution of the times, a claim that similar years often return, and a proposal: a few lines a year from every province, collected. It does not establish how remedies map to winters or any number in this game. On screen the game says "in this game", never "as Leibniz showed".

What is cut, and where the game departs

The Bills of Mortality and the Paris registers are a line on this screen. Ramazzini's seasons, grains and beasts are two cards; his dedication of his second year to Leibniz, the German doctors "curious about nature" and their Ephemerides, the Lieutenant of Police, Louis XIV, Mithridates, Juba and Gentius, Alfonso and Ulugh Beg are read here, not played. No patient is diagnosed: one fever a winter, a hundred sick, one remedy. The collection comes back the winter after it is written; Leibniz says only "every year". Bark is Leibniz's; bleeding and rest are the game's, so that the choice has three live options. The look is three hundred years after the letter, on purpose.
