# Gödel's Endgame (the Marker build) — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-1-board.png` (the board on arrival: two lines marked, the chips, "this line"), then `screenshot-a-2-praise.png` (the cheap tick), `screenshot-a-3-loophole.png` (the loop and what the board says follows), `screenshot-a-4-mk2-found.png` (Marker 2's loophole one line down), `screenshot-a-5-omega.png` (the days), `screenshot-a-6-promise-run.png` (the promise's chain) and `screenshot-a-end.png`. Then play it on a phone. Earlier builds are in `previous/` (chalk rulebook), `previous/four-keys/` and `previous/endgame/` (the three-act build this replaces).
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the two start lines, the number and fact chips, the budgets (six lines, three for Marker 2, four days), and `GENEROUS`, which turns the toy Marker into the paper's weaker one. Change anything and run `node calibration.js`: it writes every line the chips allow at every position and checks the paper's structure. Then `python3 build.py`, which runs the check again and refuses to build on a failure or a mechanism mismatch.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) "never marks wrong" as the promise, which is soundness rather than the paper's consistency, chosen because a teenager has the first word and not the second; (b) the marking rule "a mark must come out right, tick tried first", which gives Löb's cheap tick as a live wrong branch; (c) self-reference by the chip "this line", with the paper's hole-and-number construction moved to About; (d) the days scene is watching rather than deciding; (e) a quarter of random chip-tappers find the loophole, so a player can stumble on it without the thought; (f) the board scrolls once it is long, so the top lines leave the screen while writing; (g) the look: the board is back, animated this time; the Wiener Werkstätte cabinet of the three-act build is in `previous/endgame/` if you prefer it, and the mechanism does not care which skin it wears.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and a scripted browser session at 380 × 700. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length has not been measured.
