Gödel's Loophole

A rulebook for whole numbers: a few rules, and a way of proving things from them. Hilbert believed that every sentence about numbers could be proved or refuted from the right rulebook.

Gödel, twenty-five, picked up the chalk.

Fill the holes in sentences and run the rulebook. Write a sentence of your own. Then try to fix what you find.

Find a sentence the rulebook can neither prove nor refute. Then try to close the loophole.

    Gödel proved this for Principia Mathematica, for set theory and for ordinary arithmetic with a handful of rules: whatever the rulebook, as long as its rules can be listed, there is a true sentence it cannot prove, and it cannot prove that it never contradicts itself. The sequel he promised at the end of the paper was never needed.

    How to play

    Find a sentence the rulebook can neither prove nor refute. Then try to close the loophole.

    The board

    At the top is the rulebook: Rulebook 1 works out facts about whole numbers, and it can read its own ledger of what it has proved and refuted. Under it are shapes, numbered by their line: sentences with a hole, □, where a number goes. Shapes 1 to 3 are about numbers. Shapes 4 and 5 are about the rulebook itself. Line 6 is empty until you write it.

    Running a sentence

    Tap a shape, then tap a number for its hole; the sentence appears in yellow on the ruled line. Tap Run the rulebook. The rulebook writes its working in white chalk and ends with a tick (proved from the rules), a cross (refuted from the rules), or neither. Tap the working to make the chalk finish at once. Another sentence clears the line.

    Writing shape 6

    After three sentences, Write shape 6 appears. A shape of your own is made from three parts: proves or does not prove; shape □ or a shape number 1 to 5; filled with □ or a number 1 to 5. □ means "the number you fill in later". Write it on the board puts it on line 6 in yellow; then fill it and run it like any other. You can rewrite it. You have six sentences in this act; if none is unsettled, Gödel's chalk writes shape 6 for you and you fill it.

    The loophole

    An unsettled sentence is marked with a pink loop that never closes: the rulebook can neither prove nor refute it. That is a loophole. The board then says what follows from the sentence being unproved.

    Closing the loophole

    Add a rule to Rulebook 1: the loophole sentence, or its opposite. Touch a rule to see it written at the foot of the rulebook; Add it makes a new rulebook. With the loophole sentence added, the board gains a line about the new rulebook, and you have three runs to find the new rulebook's loophole. With the opposite added, the rule says a page of Rulebook 1's ledger proves the sentence; show the rulebook pages (your own runs) and see what it says of each. Then the other rule. Last, a bet: will Rulebook 1 prove the certificate that it never contradicts itself? Run it and see.

    The verdict

    In words: what the paper found, what you found, where the two part, and what would have followed had your result stood.
