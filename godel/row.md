| **Gödel's Loophole** | Gödel (1931), *Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik* 38:173–198. On formally undecidable propositions of Principia Mathematica and related systems, as chalk on a green board: a marking robot, the Marker, ticks or crosses every line on the board by its rules and is never wrong. Two lines are there when you arrive; write up to six from chips, a number and a fact, or "Marker 1 will tick / will never tick line …" with the last chip the line you are writing. Earlier lines get read off the board; "will tick this line" gets a cheap tick; "will never tick this line" gets a pink loop that never closes, and is true. Then close the loophole three ways: make it a rule and Marker 2 has a line of its own it cannot mark; make the opposite a rule and Marker 3 swears Marker 1 ticks the line and says no to every day you ask about; ask Marker 1 to tick "I never mark wrong" and the chalk shows the promise would let it tick the loophole.
