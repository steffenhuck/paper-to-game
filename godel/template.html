Gödel's Loophole

There is a marking robot in the classroom, the Marker. Write any sentence about whole numbers on the board and it marks it: a tick if the sentence follows from its rules, a cross if the opposite does. It is never wrong.

Hilbert said: with good enough rules, a Marker like this marks everything. Gödel, twenty-five, picked up the chalk.

Write lines on the board and watch the Marker mark them. Write one that talks about the Marker. Then try to fix what you find.

Write a line the Marker can neither tick nor cross. Then try to close the loophole.

    Gödel proved this for Principia Mathematica, for set theory and for ordinary arithmetic with a handful of rules: whatever the rules, as long as they can be written down, there is a true sentence they cannot reach, and they cannot show that they never lead to a wrong mark.     How to play

    Write a line the Marker can neither tick nor cross. Then try to close the loophole.

    The board

    At the top is the Marker: it marks every line on the board with a tick (the line follows from its rules) or a cross (the opposite does), and it is never wrong. Lines are numbered by their place on the board. Two lines are there when you arrive, one ticked and one crossed. The Marker's marks sit at the right of each line.

    Writing a line

    Below the board's lines is the line you are writing, in yellow, and under it the chips that make it. A number: tap a number, then a fact (is even, is a prime, is bigger than 3). About the Marker: tap will tick or will never tick, then a line number; the last chip is the number your new line will get, "this line". Tap Write it on the board: the line goes on the board and the Marker marks it at once, writing its working in white chalk. Tap the working to make the chalk finish. Next line clears the chips. You have six lines; if every one is marked, Gödel's chalk writes a line for you.

    The loophole

    A line about the Marker's own mark on that very line is decided by one rule, which the Marker states on the board: a mark must come out right, and it tries the tick first. A line with no mark is shown with a pink loop that never closes: the Marker can neither tick nor cross it. That is a loophole. The board then says what follows from the line not being ticked.

    Closing the loophole

    Add a rule to Marker 1: the line itself (so it gets a tick by rule), or its opposite (so it gets a cross). Touch a rule to see it written under the Marker's name; Add it makes a new Marker, 2 or 3. With the line as a rule, you have three lines to write Marker 2 a line it cannot mark. With the opposite as a rule, Marker 3 holds that Marker 1 ticks the line; ask it about day after day and see what it says. Then the other rule. Last, a bet: will Marker 1 tick its own promise, "Marker 1 never marks wrong"? Run it and see.

    The verdict

    In words: what the paper found, what you found, where the two part, and what would have followed had your result stood.
