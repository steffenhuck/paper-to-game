A game from a paper

Ganguli’s Letters

A town wants its streets green. Every household on a street gets ten pounds and can put any of it towards saplings for its front garden: a pound, a sapling. The rest it keeps.

You are the council’s planner. You cannot make anyone plant. You write the letter: it says who will get to see what each household chose, and when. No names, ever.

Your hands will pick a letter for a street, plant the street, and bet on what the next street plants once it sees what the letter sends.

Twelve streets, six letters: get the town to plant as many saplings as you can.

How to play

Ganguli’s Letters

Twelve streets, six letters: get the town to plant as many saplings as you can.

The streets. Every household on a street gets ten pounds and may put any of it towards saplings for its front garden, keeping the rest. A plot is one household; a sapling is a pound it gave. A plain street, where nobody is told anything and nobody sees anything, plants 57 saplings. Green grows only where someone chose it.

The letter. Streets come in pairs. The top street gets your letter, which tells each household who will see its pledge, and when. The bottom street sees only what the letter sends, before it decides. Touch an envelope and the picture shows who will see whom: an eye on a plot that is told it will be seen, cards and arrows on the road for what travels, a slot on a plot that receives something. The six letters are: the plain letter (nobody sees anything); the sheet (all twenty pledges to every household on the next street, before they decide); the spokesperson (one household’s pledge to all twenty on the next street, before; the other nineteen told nothing); pass it on (each pledge to one household on the next street, before); afterwards (each pledge to one household on the next street, after they have decided); the clipboard (all twenty pledges to one household on the next street, before). No letter carries a name.

Plant this street. The top street fills, plot by plot, and its bar and number show how many saplings it planted against the plain mark at 57. The game then says what the letter told the households, what they planted, and what the paper found about that letter.

The bet. When the letter sends something to the next street before it decides, you say how many saplings the next street will plant. The slider starts at the plain street’s 57; the marigold marker on the bottom bar shows your bet. Send, and the bottom street fills. The game says what they saw, what they planted, what you expected, and what the paper found. When nothing reaches the next street in time, there is no bet, and it plants like a plain street.

Next streets. Six pairs, one letter each; you may send the same letter again. Nothing carries over from one pair to the next. After the sixth pair the town is counted: your saplings against a town of plain letters and against the best letter on every street, and the paper’s word for how you bet.
