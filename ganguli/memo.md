# Ganguli's Letters — design memo

## 0. Given

Not Steffen's: a gift for someone else, with the beta line in the footer and no signature or counter. No recipient named.

Ideas given, and they bind the design: *"the choice of perspective is important here. perhaps the planner? or we take this outside the survey and look at its real life equivalent. the visuals should reflect the green topic."* Three instructions, taken as: the player is the planner, not a participant; the game is set in the real-life equivalent of the experiment, not in a survey; and the look is green. All three are honoured below, and they do not conflict with the precedent rule: the planner is a controller, which is the position of Jevons's Noon two games back, but the paper and the brief both want it, so the other three dimensions differ instead (section 6).

## 2. What the paper hands over

**An estimate**, primary. An online experiment on 4,701 UK adults in two waves. Everyone gets £10 and chooses how much of it to spend on tree planting (a carbon offset) and how much to keep; the amount is the paper's "willingness to mitigate". Wave-1 participants are told, before choosing, what will happen to their anonymised choice:

| Treatment | What the wave-1 participant is told | Mean pledge |
|---|---|---|
| BASE | nothing | £2.83 |
| OBS-(1,1) | one wave-2 person will see it, after they have chosen | £2.82 (−0.014, n.s.) |
| INF-(1,1) | one wave-2 person will see it before they choose | £3.30 (+0.477, p<0.05) |
| INF-(20,1) | twenty wave-2 people will each see it (and only it) before choosing | +0.131 on INF-(1,1), n.s. |
| INF-(1,20) | one wave-2 person will see it among twenty others' before choosing | −0.496 on INF-(1,1), p<0.05 |
| INF-(20,20) | twenty wave-2 people will each see it among twenty others' | −0.411 on INF-(1,1), p<0.1 |

Table 2 (column 1), Table 3 (column 1, baseline INF-(1,1), constant 3.307). Wave 2's response (Table 4, column 1): a wave-2 person who sees one pledge gives £2.54 plus 7.5 pence per pound seen. The social multiplier for a pound of extra mitigation, if influence propagated through many people, is about 1.1 (footnote 14). Beliefs (section 3.3): asked how much a wave-2 person would give on seeing £2, £5 or £8, wave-1 participants expected the step from £2 to £5 to add £1.62 when it added £0.27, and the step from £5 to £8 to add £1.16 when it added £0.68. About one in ten was accurate (within 10%), 15–20% underestimated, most overestimated (Figure 1). Participants also underestimate others' baseline pledge: £2.42 guessed, £2.87 actual.

**An argument**, secondary: why influence does not scale. The extensive margin (more people seeing you) is flat, which the paper reads as scope insensitivity; the intensive margin (being one of many seen) kills the effect, which it reads as diffusion of responsibility or dilution. And a boundary the paper draws itself: the choices are anonymous, so social image is switched off; it does not claim image concerns are absent in the world.

## 3. The discovery, and its kind

A **counterintuitive result**, in three parts, each the opposite of what a campaign planner would assume. (1) Being watched is not what moves people; what moves them is the chance that their choice shapes someone else's. The same observer, seeing the same pledge *after* instead of *before*, changes nothing. (2) The motive does not scale the way a planner scales things: giving one person twenty followers adds nothing; making one person one of twenty seen takes it all away. (3) The chain reaction people imagine is a ripple. The person who sees your pledge copies seven pence in the pound; people think it is five or six times that.

## 4. The core narrative

A town council wants its streets to plant trees. Every household gets ten pounds and can put any of it towards saplings for its front garden, keeping the rest. The council cannot make anyone plant, but it can write the letter: it can tell a household who will get to see what it chose, and when. The planner thinks the way to get more trees is to show everyone's choice to everyone, or to put the greenest household on a poster. Neither works. What works is the humblest letter: your pledge goes to one household on the next street, before they decide. Told that, people plant about a sixth more. Told that someone will see it only afterwards, they plant what they would have planted anyway. Told that twenty will see it, they plant no more than for one. Told that theirs is one pledge among twenty, they plant as if nobody were looking. And the household that sees a greener pledge plants only a little more, far less than the planner expected.

## 5. Who lives in the paper

- **The wave-1 participant** (the one who may be seen): controls the pledge; anticipates whether and how their choice will reach someone else. The paper's main subject.
- **The wave-2 participant** (the one who sees): controls their own pledge after seeing one or twenty; anticipates nothing. The paper's measure of actual influence.
- **The experimenter / the planner**: controls the arrangement, which is who sees whom, how many, and when; anticipates the total. The paper's own policy question ("the potential as well as the limits of social influence") is the planner's question, and the brief asks for the planner.
- **The belief**: each participant's guess about the one who sees. The paper's third result; a side-bet in the game.
- **The offset scheme / the trees**: 2,037 tonnes of CO₂ offset by the end. The thing counted.

**What this game does differently.** *Position*: the planner is a controller, which Carbuccia's Counter and Jevons's Noon both were; the paper's policy question and the brief ("perhaps the planner?") both want it, so the position is kept and the other three dimensions move. *Format*: a challenge with a discovery inside it: a total to maximise, where the obvious moves fail and the humble one works, plus a side-bet on the ripple that the paper's own belief question supplies. *Device*: the sightlines (who sees whom, drawn as lines between two streets before the player commits) and the gardens filling (each pound a sapling, counted) with an expectation marker the player set beforehand; not a counter, a dial, a fold, a fill of squares, or a trajectory. *Chassis*: Retro, a 1970s British public-information poster, flat greens on cream with a chunky rounded face; after a Techno counter, an austere glass plate and an engraved chart; after Barlow, Spectral and Bodoni, a rounded poster face; after serrated stubs, pills and double rules, envelopes and solid poster blocks. Light, like Carbuccia's; the register and its family differ from all three.

## 7. The aim and the verdict

**The aim**, on its own line: *Twelve streets, six letters: get the town to plant as many saplings as you can.*

**The yardstick**, in the aim's units: each pair of streets shows its saplings against the plain pair (what the same two streets plant when the letter says nothing about anyone seeing anything): *this street 66, next street 58; plain streets plant 57 each.* A player who has just done what was asked sees that this pair beat the plain pair, now.

**The verdict** leads with: *You sent six letters: [the list]. Your twelve streets planted 702 saplings. Plain letters would have planted 679; pass it on everywhere, 741. You expected the next streets to plant about 20 more than a plain street when they saw what you sent; they planted about one more. In the paper's words, you substantially overestimated your influence; so did about one in five of its participants, and most of the rest overestimated it mildly.* Then the paper's lines: *Told that one person will see their pledge before deciding, people gave about a sixth more. Told that the person would see it afterwards, they gave no more. Told that twenty would see it, no more than for one. Told that theirs was one of twenty seen, no more than if nobody saw it. And the person who saw a pledge copied seven pence of every pound.* Then the boundary: the pledges in the paper, and in the game, carry no name.

## 8. Three concepts

**A. The Planner's Letters** — *Controller, challenge with a discovery inside.* Six pairs of streets, twenty households each. For every pair the planner picks one of six letters, each a real-life arrangement with a plain name: the plain letter (nothing), afterwards (your pledge shown to one household on the next street, after they decide), pass it on (to one, before), the sheet (all twenty pledges to every household on the next street, before: the neighbourhood report), the clipboard (all twenty to one household, before), the spokesperson (one household's pledge to all twenty, before). Touching a letter draws the sightlines between the two streets. PLANT: this street's gardens fill. Then the side-bet: how many saplings will the next street plant, having seen what the letter sends? The marker starts at the plain street's 57. Then the next street fills. *Deliberation*: which arrangement reaches the most people, and how much will the ripple be. *Discovery*: the sheet and the spokesperson, the two that sound strong, do nothing or next to nothing; pass it on works; the ripple is one sapling. *Fun*: anticipation (letter chosen, marker set, gardens fill), agency (the letter decides the street), discovery (the humble letter), mastery (the second half of the game is played knowing it, and the side-bet tightens). *Second playthrough*: try the letters not tried; aim for the best town; or bet the ripple right from the first pair.

**B. The Household** — *Subject, dilemma.* The player is one household on a street, choosing pounds to saplings six times under six letters, and after each choice sees what the paper's thousand did under that letter, and at the end what their own pattern says. *Deliberation*: how much would I give if one person will see it. *Discovery*: the sample's pattern against one's own. *Fun*: discovery only. *Second playthrough*: none. Fails the author test on contact: the authors know the answer, and a game about one's own introspection is a quiz about the result. Not picked.

**C. The Chain — the reckless one** — *Controller, challenge, with a play screen that is a map and no menu.* Forty houses on a map; the player draws who sees whom by tapping a sender then a receiver; each house may receive one card (so every viewer sees exactly one pledge, which keeps every house in a measured cell), and a second tool, the sheet, gives a house every pledge before it (the "one of many" cell). Cards are limited (eight for twenty houses), so the player chooses where to put them, and the trap is to fan them out from one house. PLANT: the map greens. *Deliberation*: where do the cards do the most. *Discovery*: drawn by one's own hand: the star wastes cards, the chain spends every card on a motive. *Fun*: agency and discovery; mastery in the second map. *Second playthrough*: a different drawing. The risk: forty taps on a phone, a drawing that is hard to read once drawn, and the discovery is the same one as A's, arrived at more slowly; and the best drawing (a chain) is also the obvious one once seen, so the deliberation thins after one map. Its best parts go into A: the sightlines drawn in the picture when a letter is touched, and the star as one of the six letters.

## 10. The pick, and why

**A, The Planner's Letters.** It is the brief's seat (the planner), set in the brief's world (a street, letters, saplings) and the discovery lands on the first or second letter the stranger reaches for, because the letters that sound strong are the paper's dead cells and the humble one is its live cell. The side-bet is the paper's own belief question, asked about the next street instead of about a wave-2 stranger, and it gives the paper's third finding a place in the player's own hands rather than on the verdict screen. The sentence afterwards has no "guess": *I sent the sheet and nothing happened; I put one household on a poster and one household planted more; I passed each pledge to one neighbour and the street planted a sixth more; and the neighbours who saw it planted one sapling more between them.* Originality: no game in the table has had the player design who sees whom, nor drawn the design as sightlines before the commitment.

**Author test.** The authors would recognise every cell of their own design in the six letters, their own belief elicitation in the marker, the social multiplier in the ripple, and the "no names" boundary they draw themselves. What they will enjoy is watching a player reach for the sheet first, which is the home-energy-report arrangement the literature leans on.

**Runner-up: C, The Chain.** Switch if the author wants the star-versus-chain lesson drawn by hand for a talk, at the cost of a slower first discovery and a harder phone screen.

**What the reckless concept would have given.** The structure of influence in the player's own hand, as a drawing. Kept: the sightlines, drawn in the picture the moment a letter is touched, so the player sees the shape they are choosing; the spokesperson letter, which is the star. Given up: drawing it.

## 11. The shape

- **Screens**: start; the plain street (read, not played); six pairs, each with a letter screen, this street's result, the bet, the next street's result; the verdict; How to play; About.
- **Rounds**: six pairs of streets, twelve streets, 240 households. One letter per pair; the letter resets to none each pair; the bet marker resets to 57 each time it is asked.
- **Presets** are the paper's own cells, as letters: BASE (plain), OBS-(1,1) (afterwards), INF-(1,1) (pass it on), INF-(20,20) (the sheet), INF-(1,20) (the clipboard), INF-(20,1) (the spokesperson, realised in a closed street as one household seen by the twenty on the next street). The order on screen is the planner's, not the paper's: plain first, then the ones that sound strong (the sheet, the spokesperson), then pass it on, afterwards, the clipboard; the discovery arrives early because the strong-sounding ones are dead.
- **Score**: the town's saplings against the plain town and the best town, in the aim's units; the bet is described during play and concluded only in the verdict, in the paper's four types. No tally.

## 12. Controls, mechanism, feedback, moment

- **Controls**: six envelopes (one tap selects, and draws the sightlines; the chosen one turns marigold), PLANT THIS STREET; the bet marker (a slider over the next street's tally, starting at 57), SEND; NEXT STREETS. The bet is asked only when the letter sends something to the next street before it decides.
- **Mechanism** (reconstructed): each household's pledge is a quantile of the paper's histogram for its cell (BASE or INF-(1,1), read off Table 2's right panel and adjusted to the reported means), shifted by the cell's effect from Table 3 where the cell is another; the street is the paper's average street, with no sampling noise. A household on the next street that sees a pledge before deciding adds 7.5 pence per pound by which what it saw differs from a plain pledge (Table 4's slope; the centring is ours). The extensive margin is played as exactly zero where the paper's point estimate is +0.131 and not significant, because the paper's claim is "does not scale up"; the switch is in the config.
- **Feedback**: the sightlines on touch; the gardens filling household by household with the tally climbing against the plain mark; the game's line (what the letter said, what the street planted, what the paper found); the bet marker against the next street's fill.
- **Moment**: the sheet's street filling to 58 against the plain 57, with twenty fans of lines still drawn above it.

## 14. What is cut

The OBS treatments other than OBS-(1,1) (the paper reports them only in Figure 1); the heterogeneity by attitudes and demographics (Tables D.6–D.8, D.12); the underestimation of others' baseline pledge (£2.42 against £2.87; read on the About screen); the ceiling-effect checks; the wave-2 regressions on mean, median and mode of twenty pledges (the game uses the mean, which the paper says is not distinguishable from the others); the welfare discussion; the 2,037 tonnes (on the About screen).

## 15. Divergences

- **Clock**: the paper's two waves become six pairs of streets run one after another; nothing carries from one pair to the next, as nothing carried between the paper's treatments.
- **The spokesperson**: INF-(20,1) in a closed street of twenty is one household seen by the twenty on the next street and nineteen told nothing; the paper gave every wave-1 participant twenty observers by recruiting twenty wave-2 participants each. Realised this way so that attention is a fixed resource, which is the paper's own constraint ("collecting a large sample was prohibitive").
- **The extensive margin** is played as zero (the point estimate is +0.131, not significant, and the claim is "no further increase"); the point estimate is in the config behind a switch.
- **The ripple**: Table 4's slope is applied additively to a reader's cell-mean pledge, centred on the plain pledge; the paper measured readers who saw one pledge and did not combine motive and imitation in one person. In the game a household on the next street is a reader only; it is never also told who sees it, so the two are never added in one household.
- **No sampling noise**: each street is the paper's average street. A real street of twenty would swing by fifteen saplings either way, more than the effect; the paper needed a thousand people. The About screen says so.
- **The bet's types**: the paper's cutoffs for accurate, mild and substantial overestimation are in an appendix figure we do not have; the game's cutoffs in saplings are ours and tagged.
- **Names**: the paper's pledges were anonymous and so are the game's letters ("no names"); a real street would add social image, which the paper does not test and does not deny.
- **Presentation**: pounds become saplings one for one; the paper's histogram is played as quantiles so that streets show the paper's lumps at 0, 5 and 10.

## 16. Mechanism kind and claim boundaries

**Kind: reconstructed.** `mechanism.js` runs a data-generating process built from the paper's estimates. Each household on the lettered street pledges, in expectation, the plain mean (Table 2's constant) plus the effect of the arrangement it was told, from Tables 2 and 3; each household on the next street that sees a pledge before deciding adds Table 4's 7.5 pence per pound by which what it saw differs from a plain pledge. Streets are expected streets, with no draws. The plots are whole saplings taken as quantiles of the histogram beside Table 2 (read off the figure, adjusted to the reported means), nudged so that each street adds up to its rounded total; the histogram decides only how a street looks.

- **Demonstrates**: the paper's estimates as a street shows them: +0.477 for a pledge one person will see before deciding (a sixth more; 57 → 66 on a street of twenty); nothing for a pledge seen afterwards; nothing more for twenty viewers than for one; nothing for a pledge that is one of twenty seen by one; a slight rise (+0.066) when twenty see twenty; and the copy, 0.075 per pound seen, which turns a street's nine extra saplings into less than one on the next.
- **Assumes**: the six letters as closed-street arrangements, in particular the spokesperson (one household seen by the twenty on the next street, nineteen told nothing) in place of the paper's twenty recruited observers per person; the extensive margin, OBS-(1,1) and INF-(1,20) played as exactly zero where the paper's claims are "no increase", "does not change substantially" and "no higher than baseline" (points +0.131, −0.014, −0.019); the copy centred on the plain pledge (Table 4's own line puts a reader of a plain pledge at 2.75, eight pence lower); additivity of the copy to a reader's own plain pledge, with readers never also told who sees them; expected streets rather than draws; the mean of twenty for the sheet and the clipboard (Table D.11 says mean, median and mode are not pairwise distinguishable); the bet's range and cutoffs; pairs of streets and the town of twelve.
- **The paper establishes**: the average effect of each arrangement on what UK adults gave online in 2023–24, with standard errors (Tables 2, 3); that the main effect is influence rather than observability (INF-(1,1) against OBS-(1,1), p = 0.025); that twenty viewers add nothing significant, with limited power; that being one of twenty removes the effect; the wave-2 response per pound seen (Table 4); the social multiplier of about 1.1; and that most participants overestimate that response (section 3.3, Figure 1). It does not establish what named pledges would do, what a town does over years, or whether influence propagates beyond one step.

**Where each discovery comes from.** That pass it on beats every other letter follows from the paper's cells: twenty households each with the INF-(1,1) motive, against one (the spokesperson), none (plain, afterwards, the clipboard) or the slight INF-(20,20) rise (the sheet). It is not a product of the pairs, the bet or the town. The ripple's smallness is Table 4's slope. The calibration check re-runs the pair totals with the extensive margin at its point estimate, with OBS and INF-(1,20) at theirs, with the copy centred on Table 4's own constant, with twice the slope, with no copy at all, with the sheet copied at its mode (5) instead of its mean, and with INF-(1,1) at the bottom of its 95% interval: pass it on wins in every case, by at least seven saplings a pair except in the last, where it still edges the sheet. The bet's typology is ours and is presented as the game's reading in the paper's words, with the paper's own shares quoted beside it.

## 17. Calibration plan and result

`calibration.js` checks, against the paper's typeset numbers: the two histograms add to 100 and reproduce the reported means within 0.01, with 0 and 5 as the two most frequent choices and 10 above its neighbours; Table 3's constant equals Table 2's constant plus the INF-(1,1) coefficient (3.307 = 2.830 + 0.477); the 17% of the abstract; the three zeros played are each within one standard error of zero or within 0.03 of the baseline, and INF-(20,20) is at its point; Table 4's slope gives the text's 75 pence for £10 against £0, lands within 0.05 of the text's raw 0.27 for £2 → £5, and gives the multiplier of footnote 14; the belief ratio of about six; the plain street (56.6 → 57), the pass-it-on street (66), the ripple (0.72) and the pair multiplier (1.075); the spokesperson street (one at 3.307, nineteen plain) and its ripple (equal to pass it on's); the sheet and clipboard streets; every drawn street is twenty whole plots adding to its rounded total, with the plain street showing five bare plots, three or more at five and one at ten, and the spokesperson's plot at 3; the bet's cutoffs; a six-pair game, the verdict's numbers (679, 741), its lines free of decimals; the robustness runs of section 16; and the play-it-in-numbers table. **Result: 82 checks, all pass** (`calibration-result.txt`).
